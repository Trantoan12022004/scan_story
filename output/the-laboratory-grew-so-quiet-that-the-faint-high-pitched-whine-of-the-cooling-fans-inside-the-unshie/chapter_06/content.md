The moment the boardroom doors closed behind the federal delegation, Sloan didn't celebrate.

She grabbed her leather folio, walked briskly to the private elevator, and descended straight to the ground-level exit.

She needed to find Dean.

She had checked the facilities log earlier that morning. Dean hadn't clocked out after the test burn. Instead, he had taken a four-hour emergency leave request, cashed the corporate check at the downtown branch across the street, and driven his battered twelve-year-old Ford pickup straight toward Harbor Crest Medical Center.

By 1:15 PM, Sloan’s luxury sedan pulled up outside the entrance of Harbor Crest Medical Center.

She strode through the sliding glass doors of the pediatric ward, her heels clicking rapidly against the linoleum floor, past nurses' stations and sterile waiting rooms, until she reached Room 412.

The door was partially ajar.

Inside the small, quiet room, the steady, rhythmic beep-beep-beep of a heart monitor echoed softly.

Dean sat in a plastic chair beside the hospital bed, his head bowed, his calloused hands clasped tightly around the small, pale hand of an eight-year-old girl. Emma Miller lay sleeping against the white pillows, her chest rising and falling beneath a thin pink blanket, a tiny IV taped to the back of her hand.

Standing near the doorway, speaking quietly with a senior pediatric cardiologist, was hospital administrator Dr. Sarah Vance.

"...funds have cleared the hospital accounting office, Mr. Miller," Dr. Vance was saying, looking at a clipboard with an expression of profound relief. "The administrative hold has been lifted. The surgical team is prepping Operating Room B right now. We can perform the valve repair procedure at 4:00 PM today."

Dean didn't look up. He just squeezed his daughter's small hand tighter, his shoulders trembling with the suppressed emotion of a man who had walked through hell and finally seen the light at the other end.

"Thank you, Doctor," Dean whispered, his voice thick and rough. "Just... thank you."

Sloan stood quietly in the doorway, watching the scene unfold.

For the first time since she had taken over Hutton Aerospace, the ruthless, iron-willed billionaire felt a lump form in her throat. She realized that the fifty thousand dollars she had paid him wasn't just a business transaction—it was the difference between life and death for a little girl who loved star stickers on her ceiling.

Sloan stepped softly into the room.

Dean looked up, blinking away the moisture in his eyes as he saw her standing by the door. He gently laid Emma's hand down on the blanket, stood up, and walked out into the quiet corridor, pulling the door closed behind him.

"You didn't have to follow me here, Ms. Hutton," Dean said, his voice regaining its calm, steady composure as he wiped his face with the back of his sleeve.

"I signed a contract, Dean," Sloan replied softly, stopping just a foot away from him. "And part of that contract was ensuring my lead propulsion architect was compensated fairly."

Dean let out a dry, weary chuckle. "I'm a maintenance worker, remember?"

"Not anymore," Sloan said, her gray eyes locking onto his with absolute determination. "As of ten minutes ago, Paul Gregson stepped down as lead of the A9 division after admitting he tried to bury your diagnostic reports. The chief engineering chair is currently vacant. And my HR department has been instructed to reinstate Dr. Dean Miller with full retroactive patents, back pay, and executive voting rights."

Dean stared at her, stunned. "Sloan... I can't go back into that corporate circus. I'm a mechanic. I belong in the shop."

"You belong wherever you can build things that work," Sloan said, stepping closer. "And as for the circus? You'll have me running interference against the lawyers. Trust me, nobody at Hutton Aerospace will ever forge your signature again."

Dean looked down the hallway toward the operating room prep doors, then back at Sloan. The overwhelming weight that had crushed his chest for three long years finally dissolved completely.

"Only if I get to keep my gray work shirt," Dean said quietly, a faint, genuine smile touching his lips.

Sloan laughed—a warm, bright sound that echoed down the sterile hospital corridor.

"Deal, Dr. Miller. Deal."
