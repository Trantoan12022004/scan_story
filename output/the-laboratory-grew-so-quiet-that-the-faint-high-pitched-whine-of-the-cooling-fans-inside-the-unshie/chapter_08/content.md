The morning sun cast a brilliant golden glow across the glass facade of Hutton Aerospace’s central campus. Inside the executive boardroom on the fiftieth floor, the atmosphere was a far cry from the tense, frantic panic of a month ago.

The walls were lined with clean, white architectural models, schematic diagrams of the next-generation propulsion network, and the official patent reinstatements bearing Dean Miller’s name at the top.

Dean sat at the center of the mahogany table. He wore a sharp, tailored navy suit—a deliberate departure from his old facilities uniform, though he had insisted on keeping his favorite stubby pencil tucked securely inside his breast pocket.

Around the table, senior engineers and division heads listened in absolute silence as Dean walked them through the stress-matrix updates for the upcoming sub-orbital flight tests.

"We aren't just pushing for raw thrust anymore," Dean explained, his voice calm, steady, and commanding the complete respect of everyone in the room. "The breakthrough with the A9 core taught us a vital lesson. Speed without harmonic stability is just controlled self-destruction. Every system we design from here on out must be built to listen to its own environment."

Paul Gregson’s replacement, a brilliant young propulsion theorist named Dr. Maya Lin, nodded approvingly, jotting down notes. "The telemetry from yesterday's secondary burn confirms it, Dr. Miller. Vibration tolerances are down by forty percent across all three primary nodes."

The heavy oak doors swung open, and Sloan Hutton walked in. She carried a thick leather portfolio under her arm, her eyes scanning the room with quiet satisfaction.

As she took her seat at the head of the table, the meeting paused.

"Carry on, Dr. Miller," Sloan said smoothly, offering him a brief, encouraging nod. "I just dropped in to observe the future of our aerospace division."

Dean smiled faintly, picking up his marker to sketch a final adjustment on the digital whiteboard. Looking around the room at the faces of engineers who were finally working together with honesty and precision, he knew the dark years of litigation and shadow were truly behind him. He wasn't hiding anymore. He was building.
