Six months later, the skies above the Hutton Aerospace testing range were clear and brilliant under a crisp morning sun.

On the primary launch pad, the newly christened A9-Atlas rocket stood tall against the horizon, gleaming in the sunlight—a masterpiece of honest engineering, flawless structural integrity, and revolutionary propulsion architecture.

On the observation deck, a small crowd had gathered to watch the historic unmanned sub-orbital test flight.

Emma stood right beside the reinforced observation railing, wearing a bright red jacket and holding a pair of heavy binoculars, her eyes glued to the launch pad below.

Beside her, Sloan Hutton stood with her hands resting on the railing, the wind catching her hair as she watched the final countdown sequence tick down on the master console.

"T-minus ten seconds," the automated launch voice echoed across the open-air deck.

Dean stood at the central command console, his fingers resting steady on the master override switch. He looked across the room, catching Sloan’s eye. She offered him a warm, confident nod, her gray eyes reflecting the bright morning light.

"Ten... nine... eight..."

Dean turned his gaze to the monitor showing his daughter smiling brightly in the reflection of her binoculars.

"...three... two... one... ignition."

The earth beneath the observation deck rumbled with a deep, magnificent power. The A9-Atlas surged upward, leaving a clean, perfectly stable column of blue plasma in its wake, climbing higher and higher into the boundless sky without a single tremor or acoustic flaw.

The control room erupted into cheers. Engineers embraced, screens flashed green across the board, and the telemetry line remained as smooth and straight as a drawn line.

Emma cheered loudly, jumping up and down and pointing at the streak of light fading into the stratosphere. "Look at it go, Dad! It’s flying straight to the stars!"

Dean walked over, wrapping his arms around his daughter and lifting her up so she could see over the railing, while Sloan stepped in close beside them, resting her hand over his.

The past was fully reconciled, the machine was singing true, and as they looked up together toward the limitless horizon, Dean knew that the most important calculation of his life had finally led them home.
