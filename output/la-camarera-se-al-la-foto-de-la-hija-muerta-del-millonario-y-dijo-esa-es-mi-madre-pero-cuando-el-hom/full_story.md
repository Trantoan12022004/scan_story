## THE WAITRESS POINTED TO THE PHOTO OF THE MILLIONAIRE'S DEAD DAUGHTER AND SAID: "THAT'S MY MOTHER"... BUT WHEN THE MAN WHO HIDDEN HER ENTERED THE CAFE, HE TRIED TO TEAR HER BRACELET OFF

### PART 1 — THE MAN AT THE DOOR

—The man who hid her just came in.

Don Ricardo Valdés turned towards the door of the café.

A man with white hair, a dark coat and a cane with a silver handle had just crossed the threshold.

It was Ignacio Valdés.

His younger brother.

His partner for more than thirty years.

And the person who had identified Sofía's remains after the fire.

Ignacio saw Lucía next to the table.

Then he looked at Ricardo's open wallet and the photograph.

His face lost color.

"That girl shouldn't be here," he muttered.

Ricardo got up.

"Why shouldn't I be here?"

Ignacio understood his mistake.

—I meant that employees should not bother customers.

Lucia took out an old hospital bracelet from her apron pocket.

—I saw him two weeks ago in front of my house. I was looking out the window while my mother was sleeping.

Ignacio tried to snatch it away.

Lucia stepped back.

Ricardo stood between them.

—Don't touch her again.

The bracelet was yellowed from time. The ink was barely visible, but two initials could still be read:

S.V.

And a date.

It was the same day that Sofía had disappeared twenty-six years earlier.

Ricardo felt like he was short of breath.

—Where did you get this?

"My mother was carrying her when they found her," Lucía answered. I didn't remember his name. A nurse registered her as Elena Ferrer because she needed some identity to receive treatment.

Ignacio headed towards the exit.

Ricardo grabbed his arm.

—You said that you had recognized Sofía's body.

—I recognized him because of his jewelry.

—The report stated that there were no jewels left.

Ignacio stopped moving.

The nearby customers began to observe them.

Lucia stared at her cane.

—My mother draws that handle every night.

The silver handle was shaped like a falcon's head.

For years, Elena had filled notebooks with the same drawing. He always woke up afterwards screaming:

—The man with the falcon is coming for the girl!

Ignacio raised the cane, but Ricardo grabbed it before he could hide it.

Three letters were engraved under the handle:

I.V.M.

Ignacio Valdés Montalbán.

"This is all a coincidence," he said. The girl wants money.

Lucia held his gaze.

"I didn't know who he was until ten minutes ago."

—So someone sent you.

—No one sent me. You came to look for me.

Ignacio looked around.

Two men had entered behind him. They didn't look like customers.

Lucia recognized them.

They were the same ones who had followed his mother to the medical center the week before.

One advanced towards the table.

Ricardo raised his voice:

—If you take another step, the entire cafe will know your faces.

The owner closed the front door. A waiter called the police.

Ignacio pursed his lips.

—You're making a mistake, Ricardo.

—My biggest mistake was believing you twenty-six years ago.

Ricardo held up the photograph.

—Tell me who was inside that car.

Ignacio did not respond.

Lucía placed the bracelet next to the image of Sofía.

"My mother is still alive," he said. And yesterday he remembered a word.

Ricardo looked at her with despair.

—Which one?

Lucía did not take her eyes off Ignacio.

—Brother.

### PART 2 — THE WOMAN CALLED ELENA

The police detained Ignacio and his companions until their identities were verified.

They couldn't arrest them yet for a crime committed twenty-six years earlier, but they found something strange in Ignacio's briefcase:

recent photographs of Lucía,

your home address,

Elena's medical center schedules,

and an order to transfer to a private residence in Portugal.

The document was signed, although Elena had never asked to leave.

Ricardo accompanied Lucía to her home.

It was a small apartment in a quiet neighborhood.

Elena was sitting by the window, sewing a coat.

He was fifty-four years old.

Her hair retained the same dark tone as the woman in the photograph, although it was now crossed with gray strands.

When Ricardo entered, Elena looked up.

He didn't recognize him immediately.

He couldn't speak.

For twenty-six years he had imagined that moment.

I had dreamed that Sofía crossed the door of the mansion.

That he forgave him.

Which told him that everything had been a terrible mistake.

But the woman in front of him seemed scared.

"Mom," said Lucía, "this man keeps a photograph of you."

Ricardo placed it on the table.

Elena touched it with trembling fingers.

"The big house," he whispered. The blue windows.

Ricardo began to cry.

—You painted them when you were nineteen years old.

Elena looked at him.

—Dad?

That single word destroyed the last twenty-six years of silence.

Ricardo wanted to hug her, but stopped.

Sofía had recovered a word, not a whole life.

It was she who extended her hand.

—I thought you didn't look for me.

—I looked for you for years. Ignacio showed me a report stating that you had died.

Sofia closed her eyes.

The memories came back slowly.

Before the fire he had discovered that Ignacio was diverting money from the family business to foreign companies. He copied the accounting books and made an appointment with a prosecutor.

She brought her six-month-old daughter with her because she didn't trust leaving her at the mansion.

On the road, another vehicle hit them.

Sofia's car left the road, but did not catch fire immediately. She managed to get the girl out before a second collision caused the fire.

He walked with the baby to a rural house.

The owner was Carmen Ferrer, a retired nurse.

Sofia had a serious head injury. He remembered his daughter's face, but not his own last name.

Carmen called the emergency services.

Ignacio arrived before the police.

He introduced himself as a family member and assured that he would take care of both of them.

However, Carmen heard how he threatened Sofía:

—If you remember your name, the girl will disappear.

Carmen hid mother and daughter and took them to another province.

To protect them, he registered Sofía as Elena Ferrer and the baby as Lucía, his supposed granddaughter.

Ignacio found them months later.

He did not kill them because he did not know where Sofía had hidden the copies of the accounts. For years he paid several people to monitor them.

Carmen kept the secret until her death.

Before he died, he gave Lucía a closed box.

Inside was a bracelet, several newspaper clippings, and Sofia's old locket.

Ricardo recognized the jewel.

—That medallion belonged to your mother.

Sofia pressed the edge.

The silver oval opened from behind.

Hidden under the photograph of a baby was a tiny key.

The key belonged to a safe deposit box hired two days before the accident.

The bank confirmed that no one had opened it in twenty-six years.

Inside they found the original accounting books, photographs of contracts and a letter from Sofía addressed to Ricardo.

The last sentence said:

"If something happens to me, it wasn't because I ran away from our family. It was because I discovered that Ignacio was stealing from us."

The vehicle's mechanical report also appeared.

The brakes had been tampered with.

The document was signed by a technician who died shortly after in an alleged work accident.

The police reopened the case.

The DNA tests confirmed what Ricardo already knew:

Elena Ferrer was Sofía Valdés.

Lucia was his daughter.

The granddaughter that everyone thought was dead.

But one question still needed to be answered.

If Sofía and Lucía had escaped, who was inside the burned car?

### PART 3 — TWENTY-SIX YEARS BACK

New forensic analyzes showed that the remains belonged to Ana Beltrán, a young accountant who worked for Ignacio.

Ana had helped falsify several transfers, but wanted to report him when she discovered that he was planning to kill Sofía.

Ignacio forced her into the vehicle after the accident and placed his niece's belongings inside.

The report also mentioned remains of a baby.

That was false.

The medical examiner admitted that Ignacio paid him to add that information and close any investigation into the missing girl.

The two men who followed Lucía confessed that they had received orders to transfer Sofía out of Spain before she regained her memory.

Ignacio was arrested for attempted murder, kidnapping, forgery, fraud and responsibility for Ana's death.

During the trial he continued to deny everything.

However, the key, the accounts, the manipulated reports and the payments to the guards formed a story that was impossible to dismantle.

He was sentenced to a long prison sentence.

Ana's family finally received the truth and was able to bury her remains under her real name.

Ricardo recovered part of the stolen money and sold the properties that Ignacio had bought through false companies.

He allocated those funds to an organization to help people without documentary identity and families of missing people.

Sofia did not immediately return to the mansion.

"I can't get back twenty-six years by entering a house," he explained. I need to remember at my own pace.

Ricardo accepted.

I visited her every week.

Sometimes Sofia remembered a song.

Other times, a room.

There were also days when I didn't want to see him.

Ricardo never pressured her.

Lucía continued working in the cafe for several months, although she now knew that she was the heir to one of the richest families in the country.

When Ricardo offered her a position in the company, she responded:

—First I want to meet my family. Then I will learn to run your hotels.

He studied administration and created a training program for workers who had never been able to access a university.

A year later, Ricardo returned to the same cafe.

He sat by the window.

He opened his wallet.

The old photograph was still there, but now he had added another one.

In the new image Sofía, Lucía and him appeared.

They were not trying to recover the past.

They were building something that Ignacio had not been able to erase.

On the wall of the cafe they placed a small plaque:

“For twenty-six years they took away his name, but they couldn't stop his daughter from recognizing his face.”

Ricardo believed that a wallet only held money and memories.

That day he discovered that he could also open an empty tomb, return two identities and destroy the lie that had divided a family for half a life.
