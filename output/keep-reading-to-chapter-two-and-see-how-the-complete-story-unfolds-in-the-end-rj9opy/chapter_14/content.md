-14

Arthur Prescott was dead.

Chadworth had watched him die.

Cancer had reduced the most intimidating man he had ever known to eighty pounds beneath a hospital blanket.

Chadworth had stood beside the coffin.

He had watched it lowered into the family crypt.

There was no secret survival.

No impossible resurrection.

And yet the ARGENT administrator had used Arthur’s language.

His philosophy.

His nickname.

“AI voice model,” Marcus suggested.

Daniel shook his head.

“The system existed before modern voice models were convincing.”

“Recording library?”

“Possible.”

Chadworth stared at the ceiling.

“My father recorded everything.”

Edmund looked at him.

“What?”

“Board meetings. Dictation. legal notes. personal journals.”

Arthur had obsessed over legacy.

He believed future executives should learn directly from his decisions.

Thousands of hours of recordings existed in the Prescott archives.

“What if ARGENT isn’t a person?” Chadworth asked.

Daniel’s expression changed.

“What do you mean?”

“What if it’s a governance system?”

Edmund stood.

“Arthur experimented with decision modeling.”

Chadworth looked at him.

“You knew?”

“Early expert systems. Risk scoring. succession planning. Nothing like modern AI.”

“But enough to create rules.”

“Yes.”

Daniel whispered, “A system that decides who threatens family control.”

Chadworth felt sick.

Arthur had spent his entire life terrified that weak heirs would destroy what he built.

He designed trusts.

Voting structures.

Succession tests.

He had once told Chadworth that family was “an asset requiring governance.”

What if ARGENT had begun as a succession system?

Not intelligent.

Not conscious.

Simply a structured protocol that identified threats and recommended interventions.

Then someone like Claire could turn those recommendations into crimes.

“Where would it be hosted?” Marcus asked.

Chadworth knew immediately.

“The old family office.”

Edmund’s face changed.

“The tower?”

“Sub-basement.”

Prescott Tower had been built by Arthur forty years earlier.

Most systems had moved to modern data centers.

But Arthur’s private archive remained beneath the original building.

Federal agents accompanied them this time.

Chadworth refused secrecy.

No more private missions.

No more isolated evidence.

At midnight, they entered the old server vault.

Rows of obsolete equipment stood beside modern storage arrays.

A technician located an isolated system labeled:

A.R.G.E.N.T.

Arthur R. Governance Evaluation and Network Trust.

Edmund laughed bitterly.

“Of course he named it after himself.”

The system contained decision trees.

Family profiles.

Threat classifications.

Succession scenarios.

At first, nothing illegal.

Then Daniel found modified modules installed after Arthur’s death.

Medical vulnerability.

Guardianship probability.

Behavioral compliance.

Identity displacement.

Someone had transformed Arthur’s cold succession model into a weapon.

“Administrator logs,” Marcus said.

They opened them.

The first unauthorized modification occurred six months after Arthur died.

User credential:

T.VOSS.

Later:

C.VOSS.

Then:

S.DEVEREAUX.

Claire.

Sabina.

Several others.

But above all remained one root account.

ARGENT-PRIMARY.

“Who used it?” Chadworth asked.

The technician traced authentication tokens.

Physical access required.

Not remote.

Someone had entered this vault repeatedly.

Security archives loaded.

Dates appeared.

One visitor came again and again.

Arthur Prescott’s former personal attorney.

Jonathan Crane.

Chadworth stared.

Crane was eighty-one.

Retired.

Living quietly in Connecticut.

The man who had drafted Arthur’s trusts.

The man who had told Chadworth no illegitimate-child clause existed.

The man who had handled Evelyn’s estate after her death.

The man who still sent birthday gifts to Lorelai and Gemma.

“Find him.”

Federal agents moved immediately.

But Chadworth already knew the final secret would not be inside Crane’s house.

It was here.

Crane had not invented Arthur’s obsession.

He had preserved it.

A video file sat inside the root directory.

Recorded eight years earlier.

Three days before Arthur died.

Chadworth opened it.

His father appeared on screen.

Weak.

Pale.

But mentally sharp.

“If this file is being accessed,” Arthur said, “then the Prescott family has encountered a succession threat beyond ordinary governance.”

Chadworth felt old anger rising.

Arthur continued.

“I have authorized Jonathan Crane to preserve controlling continuity by any lawful means available.”

Lawful.

That word mattered.

“The system must never physically harm a member of my family.”

Chadworth looked at Edmund.

Arthur had not ordered murder.

Not explicitly.

But he had created something dangerous.

Arthur continued.

“However, weakness cannot be allowed to destroy what generations built.”

The video ended.

Another file appeared.

Recorded years later.

Jonathan Crane.

“Arthur misunderstood the fundamental limitation of law,” Crane said.

“The law is useful only when facts remain uncontested.”

Daniel whispered, “There.”

Crane continued.

“Change the facts, and law follows.”

Medical records.

DNA evidence.

Psychiatric reports.

Witness testimony.

Crane had transformed Arthur’s defensive system into an offensive one.

The entire operation.

Voss.

Claire.

Sabina.

Corrupt officials.

All served a legal fiction machine.

Crane did not steal fortunes.

He manufactured realities in which control legally moved where he wanted it.

“Why?” Chadworth asked the screen.

The next file answered.

Crane sat older now.

“The families Arthur protected became lazy. Emotional. irresponsible. They allowed children, spouses and weak heirs to dismantle institutions that employed thousands.”

Chadworth felt disgust.

“He thought he was preserving empires.”

Edmund nodded.

“A private succession dictator.”

Another file.

This one concerned Evelyn.

Threat classification: CRITICAL.

Reason:

Subject discovered ARGENT operations and intends disclosure.

Recommended intervention:

DIScreditation through psychiatric instability.

Then someone had modified the recommendation.

C.VOSS.

Escalation authorized.

Vehicle intervention.

Chadworth’s vision blurred.

Claire had ordered Evelyn’s crash.

Not Arthur.

Not the machine.

Claire.

But Crane had built the structure that allowed her to do it.

Another file appeared.

Target:

CHADWORTH PRESCOTT.

Classification:

UNSTABLE SUCCESSOR.

Recommended action:

INCAPACITY.

Target:

GEMMA PRESCOTT.

Classification:

TRANSFER LEVERAGE.

Target:

LORELAI PRESCOTT.

Classification:

CONTINUITY ASSET.

His daughters had been reduced to categories.

One disposable.

One useful.

Chadworth’s hands shook.

Then the technician spoke.

“There’s a live connection.”

“From where?”

“Someone logged in ten minutes ago.”

“Crane?”

“Credential is ARGENT-PRIMARY.”

Marcus leaned over.

“Location?”

The technician traced.

Then looked up.

“Prescott estate.”

Chadworth’s blood turned cold.

The mansion should have been empty except for investigators.

He called Rosamund.

No answer.

Then he remembered.

Rosamund was at the hospital with Daniel.

The girls were with federal protection.

Who remained at the mansion?

One person.

Sabina.

Released temporarily from hospital treatment into federal supervised custody for a planned transfer.

She had insisted she needed to retrieve one piece of evidence from her room.

Agents had accompanied her.

Chadworth called the lead agent.

No answer.

A security feed from the mansion appeared on the ARGENT terminal.

Sabina stood in Chadworth’s office.

Beside her was an elderly man.

Jonathan Crane.

He held a gun.

But it was not pointed at Sabina.

She was holding it.

Chadworth stared.

Sabina looked directly into the office camera.

And smiled.

Then she spoke.

“I’m sorry, Chadworth.”

His heart sank.

“I lied about one thing.”

Crane sat calmly beside her.

Sabina continued.

“Claire never recruited me.”

She looked at Jonathan Crane.

“He did.”
