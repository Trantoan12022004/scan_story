The billionaire pretended to leave for Canada... But what he witnessed on the hidden cameras between his housekeeper and his daughters left him completely frozen.

The billionaire switched off the lights in his mansion, grabbed his suitcase, and said goodbye to his daughters as though the trip were completely real.

“I’ll only be gone for a few days,” he told them with a relaxed smile. “Be good.”

The girls wrapped their arms tightly around him.

They had no idea their father was pretending.

The plane never left the ground.

There was no business trip.

No Canada.

There was no hotel suite waiting for him overseas.

Instead, less than an hour after his car disappeared through the front gates, the most powerful man in the city quietly returned through the mansion’s back entrance, accompanied only by his head of security.

He had not come home to surprise anyone.

He had come back to watch.

Because the seed of doubt had already been planted.

The night before, his fiancée had leaned across the dinner table, lowered her voice, and whispered something that refused to leave his mind.

“You trust that maid far too much,” Sabina had said quietly. “She’s stealing from you. And worse... she’s manipulating your daughters.” Those words stayed with him throughout the entire night.

Not because he immediately accepted them as true.

But because some part of him was afraid they might be.

For years, Chadworth Prescott had trusted the young woman who cleaned his mansion and cared for his daughters whenever he was away. Rosamund had always been quiet, cautious, and respectful. She was the kind of employee wealthy families often overlooked. She moved through the mansion almost like a shadow, never asking for attention and never interfering in matters that did not concern her.

But Sabina had started making little remarks.

At first, they sounded harmless.

Then there were more of them. “I noticed one of my bracelets wasn’t where I left it.”

“The girls seem closer to her than they are to anyone else.”

“She’s getting too comfortable in this house.”

“She knows far too much.”

“She acts like she’s invisible, and those are the ones you have to watch.”

At first, Chadworth brushed the comments aside.

But doubt works in strange ways.

It never needs to kick down the door.

It finds the smallest cracks and slips inside.

And once it gets in, it begins changing the way you see everything.

Before long, Chadworth found himself thinking about moments that had never seemed unusual before.

The way Rosamund always knew exactly how Gemma wanted her sandwiches prepared.

The way Lorelai ran to Rosamund the moment she came home from school.

The way both girls appeared more relaxed around Rosamund than they did around anyone else in the mansion.

Before Sabina started making accusations, those things would have looked like simple affection.

Afterward, they seemed different.

Suspicious.

Dangerous.

Like signs he had somehow missed. So Chadworth made up his mind.

At dinner, he announced that he had to make an unexpected trip to Canada.

“I have to leave tomorrow morning,” he said, barely touching his meal.

Lorelai was the first to look up.

“Again?” She never said the word aloud, but the disappointment in her expression carried more weight than shouting ever could.

Gemma said nothing. She simply tightened her grip around her spoon and stared down at her plate.

For a moment, Chadworth felt something tighten inside his chest.

Perhaps it was guilt.

But he pushed the feeling aside.

“Just a few days,” he promised.

Beside him, Sabina smiled with calm elegance and slipped her hand into his beneath the table, playing the part of the perfect wife-to-be.

Rosamund stood near the kitchen entrance, quietly gathering the dishes, her expression impossible to read.

The next morning, the driver placed Chadworth’s suitcase into the car.

His daughters hugged him at the front door.

“I love you, Dad,” Gemma whispered.

He kissed each of them on the forehead, forced another smile, and climbed into the car.

As the vehicle pulled away, he looked back once through the tinted glass.

The girls remained on the doorstep, watching him disappear.

Behind them, inside the mansion, Rosamund carried a breakfast tray and respectfully lowered her eyes when she realized he was looking at her.

It looked like an ordinary goodbye.

A father leaving for work.

A family returning to its normal routine.

Nothing unusual at all.

Except that none of it was what it appeared to be.

Thirty minutes later, Chadworth was back.

He slipped through a service entrance at the rear of the mansion while the staff still believed he was already on his way to the airport.

No footsteps.

No conversation. No warning.

His head of security escorted him through a private hallway to a locked monitoring room that was normally used only for system inspections and high-level security checks.

Inside, a wall of screens cast a pale glow across the dark room.

The kitchen.

The foyer.

The formal living room.

The upstairs hallway.

The backyard.

The playroom.

The breakfast nook.

Every angle.

Every corner.

Every small, private moment inside the home he had built and paid for, yet somehow never truly understood.

“The cameras are live,” the security chief said quietly.

Chadworth nodded and took a seat.

“I want to see what happens when they believe I’m gone.”

At first, there was nothing unusual.

Rosamund cleared away the breakfast dishes.

The girls finished their milk.

A housekeeper carried a stack of freshly folded towels upstairs.

One of the gardeners walked across the yard. Everything looked painfully ordinary.

For several minutes, Chadworth almost felt embarrassed.

Maybe Sabina had been wrong.

Maybe he had allowed suspicion to turn him into someone he did not want to be.

Maybe he was sitting in a dark room watching an innocent woman because fear had gotten the better of him.

Then the front door clicked shut after the last employee of the morning left the hallway.

And Sabina walked into the living room.

The expression on her face changed instantly.

The warm smile was gone.

The polished elegance disappeared.

So did the sweet, understanding fiancée persona she always showed Chadworth.

It was as if he were watching a mask fall away in real time.

Her entire posture changed.

The sweetness drained from her face and was replaced by something colder. Something sharp. Irritated. Impatient. Cruel.

Chadworth leaned closer to the screens.

Lorelai was sitting on the rug with an open book in her lap. Gemma sat beside her, holding a stuffed rabbit tightly against her chest.

Sabina slowly walked toward them.

“What did I tell you about sitting here?” she snapped.

Both girls immediately jumped.

They weren't simply frightened.

They were conditioned.

That realization was what sent a chill through Chadworth’s blood.

These were not children hearing a raised voice for the first time.

These were children who already knew exactly what was coming next.

Lorelai immediately shut her book. Gemma dropped her eyes to the floor.

Sabina grabbed the stuffed rabbit from her hands and tossed it onto the sofa.

“I’m tired of having to repeat myself,” she said. “When your father isn’t here, you do what I tell you the first time.”

Gemma’s lower lip began to tremble.

Lorelai shifted closer to her little sister.

In the monitoring room, Chadworth stopped breathing for a moment.

Because his daughters did not look like children being disciplined by their future stepmother.

They looked like children who were afraid of her.

Then Rosamund entered the room.

She had most likely heard Sabina’s voice from the hallway.

She approached cautiously, without arguing or creating a confrontation, simply positioning herself between Sabina and the girls in a way that was subtle enough not to escalate the situation.

“Miss Sabina,” Rosamund said gently, “the girls haven’t done anything wrong.”

Sabina spun toward her so quickly that the movement almost looked violent.

“Did I ask for your opinion?”

Rosamund did not move.

“No, ma’am.”

“Then remember your place.”

Silence filled the room.

On the screen, Lorelai reached for Gemma.

Chadworth focused on that tiny movement longer than anything else.

Not the confrontation.

Not Sabina’s expression.

Not even Rosamund stepping in.

It was the way his daughters immediately reached for each other.

As though they had done it before.

As though they already knew how to brace themselves for what was coming. And suddenly, Chadworth felt sick.

Because all those months, Sabina had been whispering into his ear that Rosamund was the dangerous one... Yet he had never stopped to ask why his daughters had grown so quiet.

Why they looked at him with that strange combination of love and distance.

Why the mansion had begun feeling colder long before he was willing to admit it.

Chadworth Prescott had spent twenty years learning how to remain calm when entire companies were collapsing around him.

He had sat through hostile acquisitions, federal investigations, lawsuits that could have destroyed his empire, and boardroom betrayals involving men he once considered brothers.

Nothing had prepared him for the sight of his youngest daughter shrinking away from the woman he intended to marry.

On the largest screen in the monitoring room, Gemma sat pressed against Lorelai’s shoulder, fingers twisted tightly into her sister’s sleeve.

Sabina stood over them.

Rosamund remained between her and the girls.

“Go back to the kitchen,” Sabina said.

Her voice was low now.

That frightened Chadworth more than the shouting.

Rosamund didn’t move.

“I only came because I heard Gemma crying.”

“She cries because people like you encourage weakness.”

Gemma’s face changed.

She was seven years old, yet something about the expression in her eyes belonged to someone much older.

Someone who had learned that crying could make things worse.

Chadworth gripped the edge of the desk.

Beside him, his head of security, Marcus Vale, remained perfectly still.

“Has this happened before?” Chadworth asked.

Marcus hesitated.

The delay lasted less than two seconds.

It was enough.

Chadworth turned toward him.

“I asked you a question.”

Marcus cleared his throat. “I’ve never personally witnessed anything inappropriate, sir.”

“That wasn’t my question.”

“No.”

Chadworth stared at him.

“No, you haven’t witnessed it?”

“No, sir. I don’t know whether it has happened before.”

On the screen, Sabina stepped closer to Rosamund.

“You’re becoming a problem.”

Rosamund lowered her voice.

“The girls are frightened.”

“They’re children.”

“Children know when they’re frightened.”

Sabina smiled.

It was not the smile Chadworth knew.

There was no warmth in it.

“You think because they like you, you matter here?”

Rosamund said nothing.

“You wash sheets,” Sabina continued. “You polish silver. You clean up after people who actually belong in this house.”

Lorelai suddenly stood.

“She does belong here.”

Every muscle in Chadworth’s body locked.

Sabina turned toward her.

“What did you say?”

Lorelai swallowed.

The courage vanished from her face almost instantly, but she forced herself not to sit down.

“I said Rosamund belongs here.”

Sabina walked slowly toward her.

Rosamund moved at the same time.

“Miss Sabina.”

“Stay out of this.”

Chadworth pushed himself away from the desk.

He was already reaching for the door when Rosamund did something unexpected.

She did not challenge Sabina.

She did not raise her voice.

Instead, she looked directly toward the camera hidden inside the molding above the fireplace.

For one second.

Only one.

But Chadworth saw it.

Rosamund knew the camera was there.

His hand froze against the door handle.

“Rewind that,” he said.

Marcus blinked. “Sir?”

“Ten seconds.”

Marcus touched the controls.

The image moved backward.

Sabina stepped away.

Rosamund returned to her original position.

Marcus played the footage again.

There.

Rosamund’s eyes shifted upward.

Directly toward the lens.

Not accidentally.

Not vaguely.

Directly.

Chadworth’s pulse began to pound.

“How does she know where that camera is?”

Marcus did not answer.

“Marcus.”

“The staff know there are cameras in common areas.”

“They know cameras exist. They do not know the exact locations.”

Marcus stared at the screen.

Chadworth suddenly remembered Sabina’s warning.

She knows far too much.

For a terrible second, suspicion returned.

Then Rosamund spoke.

Her voice came through the monitoring speakers clearly.

“Girls, why don’t you go upstairs?”

Neither child moved.

Sabina folded her arms.

“No.”

Rosamund looked at her.

“They should get ready for their lessons.”

“They can stay.”

Lorelai’s hand tightened around Gemma’s.

Sabina saw it.

Something dark moved across her expression.

“You two have been talking again, haven’t you?”

Lorelai immediately looked down.

Rosamund’s face changed.

For the first time, Chadworth saw fear in her.

Not fear for herself.

Fear of what Sabina had just said.

Sabina walked toward the girls.

“What did you tell her?”

“Nothing,” Lorelai whispered.

“What did you tell Rosamund?”

“Nothing.”

Sabina grabbed Lorelai’s wrist.

Chadworth opened the monitoring-room door.

“Sir,” Marcus said sharply.

Chadworth turned.

Marcus was standing now.

Something in his posture was wrong.

“You should wait.”

Chadworth stared at him.

“My daughter is being grabbed.”

“I understand. But if you go out there now, we lose the opportunity to determine the full extent of what’s happening.”

Chadworth looked through the glass panel in the door.

Then back at the screen.

Every instinct in him screamed to cross the mansion and tear Sabina’s hand away from Lorelai.

But Marcus’s logic was cruelly correct.

If Chadworth exposed himself now, Sabina would deny everything.

She would apologize.

She would cry.

She would claim stress.

And if there was something larger happening, it would disappear.

On the monitor, Rosamund stepped forward.

“Let her go.”

Sabina’s eyes sharpened.

“Excuse me?”

“Let go of her wrist.”

There was something different in Rosamund’s voice.

The submissive employee was gone.

Sabina noticed it too.

For several seconds, neither woman moved.

Then Sabina released Lorelai.

“You’re forgetting who signs your paycheck.”

“No,” Rosamund said.

“I’m remembering exactly who does.”

Chadworth frowned.

Sabina did too.

“What is that supposed to mean?”

Rosamund looked once more toward the hidden camera.

This time, Chadworth knew beyond doubt that the movement was intentional.

Then she said something that turned his blood cold.

“It means Mr. Prescott needs to come home.”

Silence filled the monitoring room.

Marcus slowly turned toward Chadworth.

On the screen, Sabina laughed.

“He’s halfway to Canada.”

Rosamund’s gaze remained steady.

“No,” she said quietly.

“I don’t think he is.”

Chadworth stopped breathing.

Sabina’s smile disappeared.

“What?”

Rosamund did not answer.

Instead, she took Gemma’s hand.

“Girls. Upstairs.”

This time they obeyed immediately.

Sabina stared after them, then grabbed Rosamund’s arm.

“How do you know where he is?”

Rosamund looked down at Sabina’s hand.

“Take your hand off me.”

“Answer me.”

“I don’t know where he is.”

“You just said—”

“I said I don’t think he went to Canada.”

Sabina’s face had become pale.

Chadworth stared at the monitor.

Rosamund knew.

Somehow, she had known his trip was false before he had even returned.

That should have made her look guilty.

Instead, Sabina was the one who looked terrified.

Then a sound came from the upstairs hallway.

Three soft knocks.

Pause.

Two more.

Rosamund’s head snapped toward the staircase.

Sabina released her.

“What was that?”

Rosamund said nothing.

Chadworth looked toward Marcus.

“Which camera is upstairs?”

Marcus changed feeds.

The hallway appeared.

Lorelai was standing beside a narrow decorative table near the girls’ bedrooms.

Gemma stood behind her.

Lorelai knocked again.

Three times.

Pause.

Twice.

Rosamund suddenly left the living room and hurried upstairs.

Sabina followed.

“What are they doing?” Chadworth whispered.

Marcus shook his head.

Lorelai knelt beside the decorative table and pulled something from beneath it.

A folded piece of paper.

Rosamund reached her seconds later.

Lorelai handed it to her.

Rosamund opened it.

Her face lost all color.

Sabina appeared at the far end of the hallway.

“What do you have?”

Rosamund crushed the paper in her fist.

“Nothing.”

Sabina began walking toward her.

Rosamund slipped the note into her apron.

Sabina stopped.

“Give it to me.”

“No.”

Chadworth’s eyes narrowed.

He could hear his own heartbeat.

Sabina stepped closer.

“I said give it to me.”

Rosamund backed toward the girls.

Then Lorelai spoke.

“Daddy needs to know.”

Sabina froze.

Chadworth’s stomach dropped.

“Know what?” Sabina asked.

No one answered.

Sabina’s voice became almost a whisper.

“Lorelai.”

The girl began shaking.

Rosamund crouched beside her.

“It’s okay.”

“No,” Sabina said.

“It isn’t.”

Her hand disappeared into the pocket of her dress.

Marcus suddenly leaned toward the screen.

“What is she reaching for?”

Sabina pulled out a small silver key.

Rosamund’s expression became one of pure horror.

“Where did you get that?”

Sabina smiled again.

“From your room.”

Rosamund stood.

“You had no right.”

“I have every right.”

“No. You don’t understand what that opens.”

Sabina’s smile widened.

“Oh, I understand perfectly.”

Chadworth looked at Marcus.

“What key?”

Marcus stared at the image.

“I don’t know.”

Sabina turned toward the staircase.

Rosamund moved after her.

“Sabina, stop.”

But Sabina was already descending.

The cameras followed her through the foyer, down a service corridor and toward the oldest wing of the mansion.

A section Chadworth rarely used.

She stopped in front of a narrow door disguised within the wood paneling.

Chadworth slowly stood.

He knew that door.

It led to a storage passage that had supposedly been sealed during renovations six years earlier.

Sabina inserted the key.

The lock turned.

The door opened.

And behind it was not a storage passage.

There were stairs.

Leading down.

Lights came on automatically.

Rosamund stopped at the top.

“Please,” she said.

For the first time, she sounded desperate.

“Don’t go down there.”

Sabina looked over her shoulder.

“You should have thought about that before you started keeping secrets in my house.”

Chadworth whispered four words.

“It isn’t your house.”

Sabina descended.

Rosamund followed.

Marcus changed camera feeds.

Nothing appeared.

“No cameras?” Chadworth asked.

Marcus typed quickly.

“That section isn’t on the security map.”

Chadworth turned toward him.

“What do you mean?”

“It shouldn’t exist.”

A cold sensation moved through Chadworth’s chest.

The mansion had belonged to his family for fourteen years.

He had paid for every renovation.

Every wall.

Every electrical line.

Every security system.

And yet beneath his own home was a room neither he nor his security chief supposedly knew existed.

From the speakers came a faint metallic sound.

Then Sabina screamed.

Not in anger.

Not in surprise.

In terror.

Every monitor on the wall flickered.

And went black.
