-12

The cloned plate appeared on a traffic camera twelve minutes later.

Northbound.

Then disappeared.

Marcus contacted an analyst he trusted outside Prescott security.

Edmund worked from the mansion.

Chadworth sat in the vehicle staring at a digital map, feeling every passing second like a blade.

“Where would Claire take them?”

Marcus asked.

“Not St. Catherine’s.”

“Agreed.”

“She knows we found it.”

Edmund’s voice came through the secure line.

“I found the vehicle delivery record.”

“Real company?”

“Fake company. But the VIN was recycled from a fleet auction.”

“Original owner?”

A pause.

“Children’s behavioral facility.”

Chadworth’s skin went cold.

“Name.”

“Haven House.”

Marcus looked up.

“I know it.”

“Where?”

“Closed six years ago.”

“Location?”

Edmund sent the coordinates.

A rural property seventy miles north.

Former psychiatric treatment center for minors.

Chadworth remembered the scandal.

Children restrained illegally.

Records falsified.

The facility closed after a lawsuit.

“Who owned it?”

Edmund typed.

Then stopped.

“Voss Family Medical Trust.”

Of course.

They changed direction.

Chadworth called the prosecutor Edmund trusted.

For the first time, he told someone outside the inner circle everything.

The prosecutor’s reaction moved from skepticism to silence.

He requested evidence.

Edmund sent copies of Evelyn’s archive and Daniel’s preliminary statement.

Federal agents began mobilizing.

But Haven House remained more than an hour away.

Chadworth could not wait.

“What did Lorelai mean by Mr. Henry?” he asked.

Marcus shook his head.

“I’m checking staff records.”

“No Henrys.”

“Maybe alias.”

Chadworth thought about Gemma’s birthday.

School events.

Drivers.

Temporary contractors.

Faces wealthy people stopped seeing after a few minutes.

Invisible people.

Claire understood invisibility too.

Thirty minutes later, Marcus found the answer.

A photograph appeared.

Henry Lawson.

Former orderly at Haven House.

Later employed by Voss Medical Group.

Chadworth recognized him.

Not as a driver.

As a waiter.

The man had served at Chadworth and Sabina’s engagement dinner.

He had been inside the mansion repeatedly through temporary hospitality agencies.

Everything suddenly became personal in a new way.

These people had not broken into Chadworth’s life.

They had been invited.

When they reached Haven House, dusk had fallen.

The facility stood beyond rusted gates surrounded by dead trees.

Several windows were broken.

No exterior lights.

Marcus stopped the car half a mile away.

“Federal team is twenty minutes behind.”

“I’m not waiting.”

“I knew you’d say that.”

They approached through the woods.

A gray SUV sat behind the building.

The cloned plate.

Chadworth touched the hood.

Warm.

Inside, Rosamund lay across the rear seat.

Unconscious.

Alive.

Marcus checked her pulse.

“Drugged.”

“The girls?”

No sign.

A side door stood open.

They entered.

The interior smelled of dust and disinfectant.

Old murals of smiling animals covered the walls.

Someone had recently cleaned the central corridor.

Marcus pointed.

Fresh footprints.

Small ones.

Chadworth followed them.

A door marked FAMILY THERAPY stood ajar.

Inside were cameras.

Tripods.

Lights.

A child-sized table.

On one wall hung a backdrop designed to look like an ordinary bedroom.

Chadworth understood immediately.

“They filmed the girls here?”

Marcus looked around.

“Maybe other children.”

Cabinets contained labeled drives.

Dozens of names.

Chadworth opened one.

Video files.

A frightened boy crying while an adult voice offscreen asked whether his father hit him.

Another clip began after an obvious cut.

The boy said, “Yes.”

Chadworth shut it off.

Manufactured evidence.

Children coached, frightened or drugged into creating testimony used in custody and guardianship cases.

The horrific secret beneath Claire’s operation was not only wealthy adults.

It was children.

Entire families could be dismantled with carefully edited recordings.

Marcus opened another cabinet.

Prescription bottles.

Behavioral reports.

Court orders.

“They didn’t just diagnose parents,” Chadworth whispered.

“They diagnosed children too.”

A sound came from upstairs.

Gemma.

Crying.

Chadworth ran.

He reached the second floor.

At the far end, a room glowed with bright camera lights.

Gemma sat in a chair.

Claire stood beside her.

Lorelai was behind a glass partition in another room.

Henry Lawson stood near the door.

Chadworth stopped.

Claire looked toward him.

“I expected Marcus first.”

“Let them go.”

Claire smiled.

“You always say things as though saying them makes them happen.”

Henry reached for Chadworth.

Marcus appeared and struck him hard enough to knock him sideways.

The two men crashed into the wall.

Chadworth moved toward Gemma.

Claire held up a syringe.

“Stop.”

He froze.

Gemma’s face was wet with tears.

“Daddy.”

“I’m here.”

Claire pressed the needle lightly against the child’s sleeve.

“Not another step.”

Chadworth raised his hands.

“What do you want?”

Claire laughed softly.

“That question is four years late.”

“What do you want?”

“I wanted what your family stole.”

“You’re not Sabrina.”

“No.”

“You’re not Arthur’s child.”

“No.”

“Then what did we steal from you?”

Claire’s face changed.

“My mother.”

Chadworth frowned.

“Your mother worked for Arthur Prescott.”

He remembered the settlement.

Marianne Devereaux.

Claire shook her head.

“Marianne was Sabina’s mother.”

“Then who was yours?”

“Dr. Elise Voss.”

Chadworth recognized the name only vaguely.

A psychiatrist.

Dead for nearly thirty years.

Claire’s voice grew colder.

“She developed an early behavioral assessment system for family courts. Your father’s company funded her research.”

“What does that have to do with this?”

“When she discovered her work was being used to manipulate custody cases for wealthy clients, she threatened to expose them.”

Chadworth understood before Claire finished.

“My father silenced her.”

“Yes.”

“How?”

“He destroyed her career.”

Claire’s eyes shone.

“She lost her license. Her reputation. Everything.”

“And then?”

“She killed herself.”

Gemma began crying harder.

Claire looked down at her.

For a moment, something human crossed Claire’s face.

Then vanished.

“My father spent his life proving the system could be perfected.”

“By doing exactly what destroyed your mother.”

Claire’s jaw tightened.

“No. By making the system serve us.”

“You became the thing you hate.”

“Spare me.”

“You’re kidnapping children.”

“I’m correcting power.”

“You’re torturing families.”

“Families like yours created the rules.”

Chadworth looked toward Lorelai.

She stood behind the glass, watching.

Not crying.

Watching.

His daughter had learned something from months of fear.

She was waiting.

Thinking.

Chadworth spoke slowly.

“If this is about Arthur, punish me.”

Claire smiled.

“That’s what Sabrina said once.”

“What?”

“She begged us to leave the girls alone.”

Chadworth stared.

“She tried to protect them?”

“Eventually.”

That complicated everything again.

Sabina had harmed them.

But even she had reached a line Claire was willing to cross.

“Where is Sabina?”

Claire’s expression changed.

“She made her choice.”

“Where?”

“Close.”

A noise sounded inside the ceiling.

Then sprinklers suddenly activated.

Water exploded across the room.

Claire looked upward.

Lorelai smiled.

She had pulled the fire alarm inside the partition.

The distraction lasted two seconds.

It was enough.

Gemma threw herself sideways.

Chadworth moved.

Claire lunged.

The syringe struck his arm instead of Gemma.

Pain flashed.

Marcus crashed through the doorway.

Claire fled.

Chadworth grabbed Gemma.

“Daddy!”

His vision blurred immediately.

Whatever had been inside the syringe worked fast.

Marcus shouted something.

Lorelai appeared.

Chadworth fell to one knee.

Both daughters grabbed him.

He tried to remain conscious.

“Stay together.”

“Daddy!”

“No matter what happens.”

His vision darkened.

“Stay together.”

Then everything disappeared.
