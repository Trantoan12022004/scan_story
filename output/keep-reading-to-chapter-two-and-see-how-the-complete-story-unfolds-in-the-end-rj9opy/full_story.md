## Chapter 1 - THE MOMENT THE MASK FELL

The billionaire pretended to leave for Canada... But what he witnessed on the hidden cameras between his housekeeper and his daughters left him completely frozen.

The billionaire switched off the lights in his mansion, grabbed his suitcase, and said goodbye to his daughters as though the trip were completely real.

“I’ll only be gone for a few days,” he told them with a relaxed smile. “Be good.”

The girls wrapped their arms tightly around him.

They had no idea their father was pretending.

The plane never left the ground.

There was no business trip.

No Canada.

There was no hotel suite waiting for him overseas.

Instead, less than an hour after his car disappeared through the front gates, the most powerful man in the city quietly returned through the mansion’s back entrance, accompanied only by his head of security.

He had not come home to surprise anyone.

He had come back to watch.

Because the seed of doubt had already been planted.

The night before, his fiancée had leaned across the dinner table, lowered her voice, and whispered something that refused to leave his mind.

“You trust that maid far too much,” Sabina had said quietly. “She’s stealing from you. And worse... she’s manipulating your daughters.” Those words stayed with him throughout the entire night.

Not because he immediately accepted them as true.

But because some part of him was afraid they might be.

For years, Chadworth Prescott had trusted the young woman who cleaned his mansion and cared for his daughters whenever he was away. Rosamund had always been quiet, cautious, and respectful. She was the kind of employee wealthy families often overlooked. She moved through the mansion almost like a shadow, never asking for attention and never interfering in matters that did not concern her.

But Sabina had started making little remarks.

At first, they sounded harmless.

Then there were more of them. “I noticed one of my bracelets wasn’t where I left it.”

“The girls seem closer to her than they are to anyone else.”

“She’s getting too comfortable in this house.”

“She knows far too much.”

“She acts like she’s invisible, and those are the ones you have to watch.”

At first, Chadworth brushed the comments aside.

But doubt works in strange ways.

It never needs to kick down the door.

It finds the smallest cracks and slips inside.

And once it gets in, it begins changing the way you see everything.

Before long, Chadworth found himself thinking about moments that had never seemed unusual before.

The way Rosamund always knew exactly how Gemma wanted her sandwiches prepared.

The way Lorelai ran to Rosamund the moment she came home from school.

The way both girls appeared more relaxed around Rosamund than they did around anyone else in the mansion.

Before Sabina started making accusations, those things would have looked like simple affection.

Afterward, they seemed different.

Suspicious.

Dangerous.

Like signs he had somehow missed. So Chadworth made up his mind.

At dinner, he announced that he had to make an unexpected trip to Canada.

“I have to leave tomorrow morning,” he said, barely touching his meal.

Lorelai was the first to look up.

“Again?” She never said the word aloud, but the disappointment in her expression carried more weight than shouting ever could.

Gemma said nothing. She simply tightened her grip around her spoon and stared down at her plate.

For a moment, Chadworth felt something tighten inside his chest.

Perhaps it was guilt.

But he pushed the feeling aside.

“Just a few days,” he promised.

Beside him, Sabina smiled with calm elegance and slipped her hand into his beneath the table, playing the part of the perfect wife-to-be.

Rosamund stood near the kitchen entrance, quietly gathering the dishes, her expression impossible to read.

The next morning, the driver placed Chadworth’s suitcase into the car.

His daughters hugged him at the front door.

“I love you, Dad,” Gemma whispered.

He kissed each of them on the forehead, forced another smile, and climbed into the car.

As the vehicle pulled away, he looked back once through the tinted glass.

The girls remained on the doorstep, watching him disappear.

Behind them, inside the mansion, Rosamund carried a breakfast tray and respectfully lowered her eyes when she realized he was looking at her.

It looked like an ordinary goodbye.

A father leaving for work.

A family returning to its normal routine.

Nothing unusual at all.

Except that none of it was what it appeared to be.

Thirty minutes later, Chadworth was back.

He slipped through a service entrance at the rear of the mansion while the staff still believed he was already on his way to the airport.

No footsteps.

No conversation. No warning.

His head of security escorted him through a private hallway to a locked monitoring room that was normally used only for system inspections and high-level security checks.

Inside, a wall of screens cast a pale glow across the dark room.

The kitchen.

The foyer.

The formal living room.

The upstairs hallway.

The backyard.

The playroom.

The breakfast nook.

Every angle.

Every corner.

Every small, private moment inside the home he had built and paid for, yet somehow never truly understood.

“The cameras are live,” the security chief said quietly.

Chadworth nodded and took a seat.

“I want to see what happens when they believe I’m gone.”

At first, there was nothing unusual.

Rosamund cleared away the breakfast dishes.

The girls finished their milk.

A housekeeper carried a stack of freshly folded towels upstairs.

One of the gardeners walked across the yard. Everything looked painfully ordinary.

For several minutes, Chadworth almost felt embarrassed.

Maybe Sabina had been wrong.

Maybe he had allowed suspicion to turn him into someone he did not want to be.

Maybe he was sitting in a dark room watching an innocent woman because fear had gotten the better of him.

Then the front door clicked shut after the last employee of the morning left the hallway.

And Sabina walked into the living room.

The expression on her face changed instantly.

The warm smile was gone.

The polished elegance disappeared.

So did the sweet, understanding fiancée persona she always showed Chadworth.

It was as if he were watching a mask fall away in real time.

Her entire posture changed.

The sweetness drained from her face and was replaced by something colder. Something sharp. Irritated. Impatient. Cruel.

Chadworth leaned closer to the screens.

Lorelai was sitting on the rug with an open book in her lap. Gemma sat beside her, holding a stuffed rabbit tightly against her chest.

Sabina slowly walked toward them.

“What did I tell you about sitting here?” she snapped.

Both girls immediately jumped.

They weren't simply frightened.

They were conditioned.

That realization was what sent a chill through Chadworth’s blood.

These were not children hearing a raised voice for the first time.

These were children who already knew exactly what was coming next.

Lorelai immediately shut her book. Gemma dropped her eyes to the floor.

Sabina grabbed the stuffed rabbit from her hands and tossed it onto the sofa.

“I’m tired of having to repeat myself,” she said. “When your father isn’t here, you do what I tell you the first time.”

Gemma’s lower lip began to tremble.

Lorelai shifted closer to her little sister.

In the monitoring room, Chadworth stopped breathing for a moment.

Because his daughters did not look like children being disciplined by their future stepmother.

They looked like children who were afraid of her.

Then Rosamund entered the room.

She had most likely heard Sabina’s voice from the hallway.

She approached cautiously, without arguing or creating a confrontation, simply positioning herself between Sabina and the girls in a way that was subtle enough not to escalate the situation.

“Miss Sabina,” Rosamund said gently, “the girls haven’t done anything wrong.”

Sabina spun toward her so quickly that the movement almost looked violent.

“Did I ask for your opinion?”

Rosamund did not move.

“No, ma’am.”

“Then remember your place.”

Silence filled the room.

On the screen, Lorelai reached for Gemma.

Chadworth focused on that tiny movement longer than anything else.

Not the confrontation.

Not Sabina’s expression.

Not even Rosamund stepping in.

It was the way his daughters immediately reached for each other.

As though they had done it before.

As though they already knew how to brace themselves for what was coming. And suddenly, Chadworth felt sick.

Because all those months, Sabina had been whispering into his ear that Rosamund was the dangerous one... Yet he had never stopped to ask why his daughters had grown so quiet.

Why they looked at him with that strange combination of love and distance.

Why the mansion had begun feeling colder long before he was willing to admit it.

Chadworth Prescott had spent twenty years learning how to remain calm when entire companies were collapsing around him.

He had sat through hostile acquisitions, federal investigations, lawsuits that could have destroyed his empire, and boardroom betrayals involving men he once considered brothers.

Nothing had prepared him for the sight of his youngest daughter shrinking away from the woman he intended to marry.

On the largest screen in the monitoring room, Gemma sat pressed against Lorelai’s shoulder, fingers twisted tightly into her sister’s sleeve.

Sabina stood over them.

Rosamund remained between her and the girls.

“Go back to the kitchen,” Sabina said.

Her voice was low now.

That frightened Chadworth more than the shouting.

Rosamund didn’t move.

“I only came because I heard Gemma crying.”

“She cries because people like you encourage weakness.”

Gemma’s face changed.

She was seven years old, yet something about the expression in her eyes belonged to someone much older.

Someone who had learned that crying could make things worse.

Chadworth gripped the edge of the desk.

Beside him, his head of security, Marcus Vale, remained perfectly still.

“Has this happened before?” Chadworth asked.

Marcus hesitated.

The delay lasted less than two seconds.

It was enough.

Chadworth turned toward him.

“I asked you a question.”

Marcus cleared his throat. “I’ve never personally witnessed anything inappropriate, sir.”

“That wasn’t my question.”

“No.”

Chadworth stared at him.

“No, you haven’t witnessed it?”

“No, sir. I don’t know whether it has happened before.”

On the screen, Sabina stepped closer to Rosamund.

“You’re becoming a problem.”

Rosamund lowered her voice.

“The girls are frightened.”

“They’re children.”

“Children know when they’re frightened.”

Sabina smiled.

It was not the smile Chadworth knew.

There was no warmth in it.

“You think because they like you, you matter here?”

Rosamund said nothing.

“You wash sheets,” Sabina continued. “You polish silver. You clean up after people who actually belong in this house.”

Lorelai suddenly stood.

“She does belong here.”

Every muscle in Chadworth’s body locked.

Sabina turned toward her.

“What did you say?”

Lorelai swallowed.

The courage vanished from her face almost instantly, but she forced herself not to sit down.

“I said Rosamund belongs here.”

Sabina walked slowly toward her.

Rosamund moved at the same time.

“Miss Sabina.”

“Stay out of this.”

Chadworth pushed himself away from the desk.

He was already reaching for the door when Rosamund did something unexpected.

She did not challenge Sabina.

She did not raise her voice.

Instead, she looked directly toward the camera hidden inside the molding above the fireplace.

For one second.

Only one.

But Chadworth saw it.

Rosamund knew the camera was there.

His hand froze against the door handle.

“Rewind that,” he said.

Marcus blinked. “Sir?”

“Ten seconds.”

Marcus touched the controls.

The image moved backward.

Sabina stepped away.

Rosamund returned to her original position.

Marcus played the footage again.

There.

Rosamund’s eyes shifted upward.

Directly toward the lens.

Not accidentally.

Not vaguely.

Directly.

Chadworth’s pulse began to pound.

“How does she know where that camera is?”

Marcus did not answer.

“Marcus.”

“The staff know there are cameras in common areas.”

“They know cameras exist. They do not know the exact locations.”

Marcus stared at the screen.

Chadworth suddenly remembered Sabina’s warning.

She knows far too much.

For a terrible second, suspicion returned.

Then Rosamund spoke.

Her voice came through the monitoring speakers clearly.

“Girls, why don’t you go upstairs?”

Neither child moved.

Sabina folded her arms.

“No.”

Rosamund looked at her.

“They should get ready for their lessons.”

“They can stay.”

Lorelai’s hand tightened around Gemma’s.

Sabina saw it.

Something dark moved across her expression.

“You two have been talking again, haven’t you?”

Lorelai immediately looked down.

Rosamund’s face changed.

For the first time, Chadworth saw fear in her.

Not fear for herself.

Fear of what Sabina had just said.

Sabina walked toward the girls.

“What did you tell her?”

“Nothing,” Lorelai whispered.

“What did you tell Rosamund?”

“Nothing.”

Sabina grabbed Lorelai’s wrist.

Chadworth opened the monitoring-room door.

“Sir,” Marcus said sharply.

Chadworth turned.

Marcus was standing now.

Something in his posture was wrong.

“You should wait.”

Chadworth stared at him.

“My daughter is being grabbed.”

“I understand. But if you go out there now, we lose the opportunity to determine the full extent of what’s happening.”

Chadworth looked through the glass panel in the door.

Then back at the screen.

Every instinct in him screamed to cross the mansion and tear Sabina’s hand away from Lorelai.

But Marcus’s logic was cruelly correct.

If Chadworth exposed himself now, Sabina would deny everything.

She would apologize.

She would cry.

She would claim stress.

And if there was something larger happening, it would disappear.

On the monitor, Rosamund stepped forward.

“Let her go.”

Sabina’s eyes sharpened.

“Excuse me?”

“Let go of her wrist.”

There was something different in Rosamund’s voice.

The submissive employee was gone.

Sabina noticed it too.

For several seconds, neither woman moved.

Then Sabina released Lorelai.

“You’re forgetting who signs your paycheck.”

“No,” Rosamund said.

“I’m remembering exactly who does.”

Chadworth frowned.

Sabina did too.

“What is that supposed to mean?”

Rosamund looked once more toward the hidden camera.

This time, Chadworth knew beyond doubt that the movement was intentional.

Then she said something that turned his blood cold.

“It means Mr. Prescott needs to come home.”

Silence filled the monitoring room.

Marcus slowly turned toward Chadworth.

On the screen, Sabina laughed.

“He’s halfway to Canada.”

Rosamund’s gaze remained steady.

“No,” she said quietly.

“I don’t think he is.”

Chadworth stopped breathing.

Sabina’s smile disappeared.

“What?”

Rosamund did not answer.

Instead, she took Gemma’s hand.

“Girls. Upstairs.”

This time they obeyed immediately.

Sabina stared after them, then grabbed Rosamund’s arm.

“How do you know where he is?”

Rosamund looked down at Sabina’s hand.

“Take your hand off me.”

“Answer me.”

“I don’t know where he is.”

“You just said—”

“I said I don’t think he went to Canada.”

Sabina’s face had become pale.

Chadworth stared at the monitor.

Rosamund knew.

Somehow, she had known his trip was false before he had even returned.

That should have made her look guilty.

Instead, Sabina was the one who looked terrified.

Then a sound came from the upstairs hallway.

Three soft knocks.

Pause.

Two more.

Rosamund’s head snapped toward the staircase.

Sabina released her.

“What was that?”

Rosamund said nothing.

Chadworth looked toward Marcus.

“Which camera is upstairs?”

Marcus changed feeds.

The hallway appeared.

Lorelai was standing beside a narrow decorative table near the girls’ bedrooms.

Gemma stood behind her.

Lorelai knocked again.

Three times.

Pause.

Twice.

Rosamund suddenly left the living room and hurried upstairs.

Sabina followed.

“What are they doing?” Chadworth whispered.

Marcus shook his head.

Lorelai knelt beside the decorative table and pulled something from beneath it.

A folded piece of paper.

Rosamund reached her seconds later.

Lorelai handed it to her.

Rosamund opened it.

Her face lost all color.

Sabina appeared at the far end of the hallway.

“What do you have?”

Rosamund crushed the paper in her fist.

“Nothing.”

Sabina began walking toward her.

Rosamund slipped the note into her apron.

Sabina stopped.

“Give it to me.”

“No.”

Chadworth’s eyes narrowed.

He could hear his own heartbeat.

Sabina stepped closer.

“I said give it to me.”

Rosamund backed toward the girls.

Then Lorelai spoke.

“Daddy needs to know.”

Sabina froze.

Chadworth’s stomach dropped.

“Know what?” Sabina asked.

No one answered.

Sabina’s voice became almost a whisper.

“Lorelai.”

The girl began shaking.

Rosamund crouched beside her.

“It’s okay.”

“No,” Sabina said.

“It isn’t.”

Her hand disappeared into the pocket of her dress.

Marcus suddenly leaned toward the screen.

“What is she reaching for?”

Sabina pulled out a small silver key.

Rosamund’s expression became one of pure horror.

“Where did you get that?”

Sabina smiled again.

“From your room.”

Rosamund stood.

“You had no right.”

“I have every right.”

“No. You don’t understand what that opens.”

Sabina’s smile widened.

“Oh, I understand perfectly.”

Chadworth looked at Marcus.

“What key?”

Marcus stared at the image.

“I don’t know.”

Sabina turned toward the staircase.

Rosamund moved after her.

“Sabina, stop.”

But Sabina was already descending.

The cameras followed her through the foyer, down a service corridor and toward the oldest wing of the mansion.

A section Chadworth rarely used.

She stopped in front of a narrow door disguised within the wood paneling.

Chadworth slowly stood.

He knew that door.

It led to a storage passage that had supposedly been sealed during renovations six years earlier.

Sabina inserted the key.

The lock turned.

The door opened.

And behind it was not a storage passage.

There were stairs.

Leading down.

Lights came on automatically.

Rosamund stopped at the top.

“Please,” she said.

For the first time, she sounded desperate.

“Don’t go down there.”

Sabina looked over her shoulder.

“You should have thought about that before you started keeping secrets in my house.”

Chadworth whispered four words.

“It isn’t your house.”

Sabina descended.

Rosamund followed.

Marcus changed camera feeds.

Nothing appeared.

“No cameras?” Chadworth asked.

Marcus typed quickly.

“That section isn’t on the security map.”

Chadworth turned toward him.

“What do you mean?”

“It shouldn’t exist.”

A cold sensation moved through Chadworth’s chest.

The mansion had belonged to his family for fourteen years.

He had paid for every renovation.

Every wall.

Every electrical line.

Every security system.

And yet beneath his own home was a room neither he nor his security chief supposedly knew existed.

From the speakers came a faint metallic sound.

Then Sabina screamed.

Not in anger.

Not in surprise.

In terror.

Every monitor on the wall flickered.

And went black.

## Chapter 2 - THE ROOM BENEATH THE HOUSE

-2

For three full seconds, the monitoring room was completely dark.

Then emergency lights glowed red along the floor.

Chadworth turned on Marcus.

“What happened?”

“I don’t know.”

“Bring the system back.”

“I’m trying.”

Marcus moved to the secondary console.

Chadworth reached for the radio on the desk.

Marcus caught his wrist.

“Don’t.”

Chadworth stared at the hand touching him.

Marcus released him immediately.

“Sir, if someone deliberately killed the feeds, using the internal radios could tell them exactly where you are.”

“Someone?”

Marcus looked toward the black monitors.

“You heard Sabina scream.”

“And my daughters are upstairs.”

“I know.”

Chadworth opened the door.

“Then this conversation is over.”

He moved through the dark service hallway with Marcus following close behind.

The mansion felt different now.

Not like the home Chadworth had lived in for years.

Like a structure hiding things from him.

Every portrait seemed to watch.

Every closed door looked suspicious.

When he reached the main staircase, he could hear Gemma crying upstairs.

He ran.

Lorelai opened the bedroom door before he reached it.

The moment she saw him, her expression changed from fear to complete disbelief.

“Daddy?”

Gemma pushed past her.

Chadworth dropped to his knees as both girls collided with him.

He held them so tightly Gemma squeaked.

“You came back.”

“Yes.”

“You weren’t in Canada?”

“No.”

Lorelai stepped away first.

Her eyes searched his face.

“You were watching.”

It was not a question.

Chadworth looked at her.

“How did you know?”

Lorelai looked toward the hallway.

“Rosamund told us that if things ever got very bad, we should act normal until you saw.”

His stomach tightened.

“Until I saw what?”

Neither girl answered.

Marcus appeared behind him.

Lorelai’s face went blank.

It happened instantly.

Chadworth noticed.

He stood.

“Marcus, downstairs.”

“Sir—”

“Now.”

Marcus hesitated before walking away.

Chadworth closed the bedroom door.

“What is it?”

Lorelai glanced toward the door.

“Is he gone?”

“Yes.”

“Are you sure?”

Chadworth had never heard his daughter speak about Marcus that way.

“Yes.”

She went to the bed and reached beneath the mattress.

From underneath it she removed a tiny plastic pouch.

Inside was a memory card.

Chadworth stared.

“Where did you get that?”

“Rosamund gave it to me.”

“When?”

“Two weeks ago.”

“Why?”

“She said if she disappeared, I had to give it to you.”

The air seemed to leave the room.

“Disappeared?”

Gemma began crying again.

Lorelai looked angry now.

“She said not to tell you until you came home when you weren’t supposed to.”

Chadworth tried to understand the sentence.

“You mean tonight?”

“She said if you ever pretended to go somewhere and came back secretly, it meant you were finally suspicious enough to believe us.”

Believe us.

The words cut deeper than anything Sabina had said.

“You tried to tell me?”

Lorelai’s eyes filled with tears.

“Three times.”

Chadworth remembered fragments.

Lorelai asking whether Sabina had to attend their school recital.

Gemma refusing to stay home when Rosamund took a day off.

A strange conversation at breakfast when Lorelai had said, “Dad, do adults ever lie because they know you’ll believe them?”

He had laughed.

He had kissed her forehead.

He had said, “Everybody lies sometimes.”

Then his phone rang and he walked away.

He felt suddenly ashamed to be standing in front of them.

“What did Sabina do?”

Lorelai shook her head.

“You have to watch the card.”

“What did she do?”

“Daddy.”

Her voice broke.

“Please watch it first.”

Chadworth put the card in his pocket.

“Where is Rosamund?”

Gemma pointed toward the floor.

“Downstairs.”

He knew exactly what she meant.

The hidden room.

“Stay here.”

“No!”

Gemma grabbed him.

He crouched again.

“I’m not leaving the house.”

“That’s what Mommy said.”

Chadworth went still.

Their mother, Evelyn Prescott, had died four years earlier.

A highway accident on a rainy night.

Her car had gone through a barrier.

The police called it tragic.

Unavoidable.

Sabina had entered his life eighteen months later.

“What did you say?”

Gemma looked confused by his reaction.

“Mommy said she wasn’t leaving the house.”

Lorelai turned sharply toward her.

“Gemma.”

“What?”

“When?”

Gemma clutched the rabbit against her chest.

“The night she went away.”

Chadworth could hear nothing except a faint ringing in his ears.

“Your mother was here that night?”

Gemma nodded.

“She was crying with Rosie.”

Rosie.

Rosamund.

Chadworth stood so quickly he almost lost balance.

“Stay together. Lock this door. Open it only for me.”

He left before either could answer.

Marcus was waiting at the bottom of the stairs.

“Sir, the system is partially restored.”

“Good.”

“There’s something else.”

“What?”

“The outage wasn’t random.”

Chadworth stopped.

Marcus continued.

“Someone accessed the old electrical panel beneath the west wing.”

“The hidden room.”

“Possibly.”

Chadworth stared toward the service corridor.

“Then we’re going down.”

Marcus stepped in front of him.

“I strongly recommend waiting for the team.”

“No.”

“We don’t know what’s there.”

“My employee and fiancée are down there.”

“That is exactly why—”

Chadworth pushed past him.

The disguised door remained open.

Lights illuminated the stairway.

He descended.

The temperature dropped with every step.

At the bottom was a narrow corridor with exposed brick walls.

This was not part of the modern renovation.

It was old.

Much older than the mansion’s current interior.

At the end stood a steel door.

It was open.

Inside, Sabina sat against a cabinet, her face white.

Rosamund stood several feet away.

Between them was an open metal trunk.

Hundreds of photographs were scattered across the floor.

Chadworth stepped inside.

Sabina looked up.

Her eyes widened.

“You.”

Rosamund closed her eyes.

Not in surprise.

In relief.

Chadworth looked at the photographs.

They were of his family.

Evelyn pushing Lorelai in a stroller.

Gemma as a baby.

Chadworth leaving the office.

The girls at school.

Rosamund entering the mansion.

Sabina standing outside a restaurant.

Some photographs were recent.

Others were almost ten years old.

“What is this?” Chadworth asked.

Sabina pointed at Rosamund.

“Ask her.”

Rosamund looked at him.

“It isn’t what you think.”

“That sentence rarely helps.”

She swallowed.

“I can explain.”

“Start.”

Rosamund looked toward Marcus.

“No.”

Chadworth followed her gaze.

Marcus stood in the doorway.

His expression unreadable.

“What?” Chadworth asked.

Rosamund did not take her eyes off him.

“Not while he’s here.”

Marcus almost laughed.

“Excuse me?”

Rosamund stepped backward.

“Mr. Prescott, you need to send him away.”

Marcus shook his head.

“She’s clearly involved in something.”

Rosamund’s voice sharpened.

“He knows this room exists.”

Silence.

Chadworth slowly turned.

Marcus stared back at her.

“That’s absurd.”

“You disabled the original camera in this corridor four years ago.”

“I wasn’t even head of security four years ago.”

“No.”

Rosamund looked at Chadworth.

“He was your wife’s private protection officer.”

Chadworth knew that.

Marcus had been promoted after Evelyn died.

Rosamund continued.

“And he was the last member of your security staff to see her alive.”

Marcus’s face changed.

Barely.

But Chadworth saw it.

Rosamund pointed to the metal trunk.

“That belongs to Mrs. Prescott.”

Chadworth’s heart stopped.

“She knew someone was following her. She started collecting evidence.”

Sabina laughed nervously.

“You expect him to believe you?”

Rosamund ignored her.

“Three days before she died, Mrs. Prescott told me that if anything happened to her, I was to keep this room hidden until I could prove who was involved.”

Chadworth looked at the photographs again.

“Why didn’t you tell me?”

“Because she told me not to.”

“My wife died.”

“And she believed someone close to you helped make that happen.”

Marcus stepped forward.

“That’s enough.”

Rosamund immediately moved away from him.

Chadworth raised one hand.

Marcus stopped.

“What exactly did Evelyn suspect?”

Rosamund looked down at the open trunk.

“She discovered money disappearing from a foundation account.”

Chadworth frowned.

“Which foundation?”

“The Prescott Children’s Trust.”

He knew it well.

It held hundreds of millions in assets designated primarily for Lorelai and Gemma.

“Who was taking it?”

“She never found the whole network.”

“Who did she suspect?”

Rosamund looked directly at Marcus.

Then at Sabina.

And said two names.

“Marcus Vale.”

Sabina’s breathing changed.

“And Sabrina Devereaux.”

Chadworth frowned.

“Who?”

Rosamund looked at Sabina.

“Ask your fiancée what her real name is.”

Sabina stood suddenly.

“That woman is insane.”

Rosamund reached into the trunk.

She pulled out an old newspaper clipping sealed inside plastic.

The photograph showed a younger woman outside a courthouse.

Dark hair.

Different makeup.

Different clothes.

But unmistakable eyes.

Sabina’s eyes.

Beneath the photograph was the name:

SABRINA DEVEREAUX.

Chadworth looked up.

Sabina had begun moving toward the door.

Marcus stood in her path.

For one second, Chadworth expected him to stop her.

Instead, Marcus stepped aside.

And that was when Chadworth finally understood that the danger inside his house was far greater than one cruel woman.

## Chapter 3 - THE WOMAN SABINA USED TO BE

-3

“Don’t let her leave.”

Chadworth’s command echoed off the brick walls.

Sabina did.

She darted through the doorway.

“Let her go.”

“She’s escaping.”

“No. She wants you to follow her.”

“What?”

Rosamund pointed toward Marcus.

The security chief remained in the doorway.

Too calm.

Far too calm.

Chadworth looked from one to the other.

“Marcus?”

Marcus exhaled.

“You should listen to her.”

The words were so unexpected that even Rosamund looked confused.

Then Marcus reached behind his jacket.

Chadworth stepped protectively in front of Rosamund.

Marcus removed a handgun.

“I’m not the person you think I am.”

“At the moment, I don’t know who anyone is.”

Rosamund shook her head.

“You worked with Sabrina.”

“I investigated her.”

“You followed Mrs. Prescott.”

“Because she asked me to.”

Marcus looked at Chadworth.

“Your wife suspected someone was accessing the girls’ trust almost five years ago. She came to me privately because she believed someone in your corporate office was involved.”

“Because she didn’t know who you would tell.”

“You’re saying Evelyn didn’t trust me?”

Marcus’s expression hardened.

“And she believed that made you vulnerable.”

Chadworth looked toward the doorway where Sabina had disappeared.

“Explain.”

“Evelyn discovered a shell company receiving consulting payments from three charities tied to the Prescott Foundation. Every payment was small enough to avoid automatic review. But together, over several years, they amounted to almost eight million dollars.”

Chadworth felt nauseated.

“Why didn’t the auditors catch it?”

“They did.”

“What?”

“One auditor did.”

“What happened to him?”

“He disappeared.”

Rosamund whispered, “Daniel Harker.”

Marcus nodded.

At least that was what Chadworth had been told.

“Where is he?”

“No one knows.”

Chadworth’s gaze moved to the clipping.

“And Sabina?”

That she had studied art history.

That she had spent several years managing private events in Europe.

Every detail suddenly felt poisonous.

“She changed her identity?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Marcus looked at Rosamund.

“That’s the part I never proved.”

“Mrs. Prescott did.”

Bank transfers.

Employee lists.

And a birth certificate.

Chadworth took it.

Sabrina Elise Devereaux.

Mother: Marianne Devereaux.

Father: blank.

“What am I looking at?”

Rosamund handed him another document.

An old legal settlement.

Prescott Holdings.

Twenty-nine years earlier.

Chadworth read quickly.

Then stopped.

The company had paid Marianne Devereaux $400,000 under a confidential settlement.

The authorization signature belonged to Chadworth’s father.

Arthur Prescott.

“What was this for?”

Rosamund shook her head.

“Mrs. Prescott never discovered the original complaint.”

Marcus spoke quietly.

“I did.”

Chadworth looked at him.

“Marianne Devereaux worked for your father.”

“As what?”

“A secretary.”

Something inside Chadworth twisted.

Marcus continued.

“She claimed Arthur Prescott had fathered her child.”

Chadworth looked down at the birth certificate.

Then at the photograph.

Then toward the doorway where Sabina had disappeared.

“No.”

Rosamund’s face showed pity.

“Chadworth—”

“Don’t.”

He had spent fifty-one years believing himself to be Arthur Prescott’s only surviving child.

His father had been cold, ruthless, brilliant.

But never once had he mentioned another daughter.

Marcus said, “The settlement included a nondisclosure agreement and an agreement that Marianne would never contact the Prescott family.”

Chadworth lowered himself onto the edge of a cabinet.

“Was the claim true?”

“We don’t know.”

Rosamund looked at the trunk.

“Mrs. Prescott believed it was.”

That changed everything.

Sabina was not simply a con artist who had targeted a billionaire.

She might be his half-sister.

The thought was so grotesque that Chadworth nearly laughed.

But another detail made the situation worse.

“She planned to marry me.”

Marcus nodded slowly.

“If the paternity claim is true, I don’t think marriage was ever the real goal.”

“Then what was?”

Rosamund opened another envelope.

Inside was a copy of Chadworth’s will.

He recognized it immediately.

But it was not the current version.

It was an unsigned draft prepared three years earlier.

A draft only his attorney, personal assistant and fiancée should have seen.

Sections were highlighted.

Upon the incapacity or death of Chadworth Prescott prior to marriage, all controlling shares would transfer temporarily to the trustees overseeing his daughters’ inheritance.

If he married, certain spousal rights would complicate the structure.

Yet the girls remained primary beneficiaries.

Chadworth read the highlighted notes in the margins.

Handwritten.

Remove minors through incapacity proceeding.

Secure medical guardianship.

Accelerate controlling transfer.

He stopped.

“What does ‘remove minors’ mean?”

Rosamund’s eyes filled with something close to dread.

“She planned to have Lorelai and Gemma declared psychologically unfit.”

Chadworth looked at her.

“They’re children.”

“Exactly.”

Marcus stepped closer.

“If Sabina could establish a documented history of behavioral instability, self-harm risk, violent outbursts, or severe emotional disturbance, she could argue they required institutional care and controlled guardianship.”

“She has no authority.”

“Not yet.”

Rosamund whispered, “She would after she became your wife.”

The cruelty toward the girls suddenly took on another meaning.

It was not random.

It was training.

Conditioning.

Creating fear.

Creating breakdowns.

Creating evidence.

Gemma’s nightmares.

Sabina offering to arrange a private child psychologist.

Sabina telling him the girls were “struggling more than they admitted.”

He had believed she was concerned.

“What has she done to them?”

Rosamund looked away.

“Tell me.”

“She recorded them.”

“When?”

“When they cried.”

Chadworth felt his hands curl into fists.

“She would provoke them, then turn on her phone. Sometimes she locked away things Gemma loved until Gemma screamed. Sometimes she told Lorelai you might send her away because she was becoming difficult.”

Chadworth’s face went numb.

“She told my daughter I would abandon her?”

“Yes.”

“And you knew?”

“I knew some of it.”

“Why didn’t you come to me?”

“I tried.”

“When?”

“Your office.”

He remembered.

Eight months earlier.

Rosamund had asked for a private conversation.

Sabina had appeared in the doorway.

Rosamund immediately changed the subject and asked about Gemma’s dietary restrictions.

Chadworth had thought nothing of it.

Rosamund continued.

“The next day Marcus told me that if I made accusations without evidence, Sabina could have me removed from the property.”

Chadworth looked at Marcus.

Marcus shook his head.

“I told her that because it was true. At that point we had nothing admissible.”

“You had cameras.”

“Not inside private family spaces.”

“Apparently someone had plenty of cameras.”

Marcus’s expression darkened.

“The system you were watching today is the legal security system.”

“What does that mean?”

Rosamund answered.

“There’s another one.”

Silence.

Chadworth stared at her.

“Another what?”

“Camera network.”

“Where?”

“Bedrooms.”

His stomach turned.

“The girls?”

Rosamund nodded.

Chadworth stood.

“Show me.”

“I can’t.”

“Why?”

“I never found the recording station.”

Marcus looked disturbed.

“This is the first I’ve heard about a second system.”

Rosamund gave him a skeptical look.

“I found a lens behind the smoke detector in Lorelai’s room three months ago.”

“Why didn’t you remove it?”

“Because I wanted to know who came to collect the footage.”

Chadworth closed his eyes.

Every instinct screamed at him to find Sabina.

But now he understood something terrifying.

Running after her would not expose the entire structure.

It would only frighten it into hiding.

He looked at Marcus.

“Lock the property.”

Marcus reached for his radio.

“Quietly.”

Marcus nodded.

“No cars in or out. No alarms.”

Rosamund suddenly looked toward the ceiling.

A faint sound traveled through the pipes.

A door closing.

Then footsteps.

“Sabina,” Chadworth said.

Rosamund shook her head.

“No.”

“How do you know?”

“Because she would run.”

The footsteps stopped directly above them.

A moment later, a male voice came through the old ventilation shaft.

“Chadworth?”

He recognized it immediately.

His younger daughter’s godfather.

His oldest friend.

And the chief financial officer of Prescott Holdings.

Edmund Carlisle.

Chadworth looked at Marcus.

“Why is Edmund in my house?”

Marcus’s face had gone pale.

No one answered.

Then Edmund’s voice came again.

“Chadworth, I know you’re down there.”

A pause.

“And before you come upstairs, there’s something you need to understand about Evelyn’s death.”

## Chapter 4 - THE LAST NIGHT OF EVELYN PRESCOTT

-4

Chadworth climbed the stairs with the sensation that he was walking toward the end of his old life.

Edmund Carlisle waited in the service corridor.

He looked almost exactly as he had that morning at breakfast meetings for the past fifteen years: perfectly tailored gray suit, silver hair, calm posture.

Except there was blood on his left cuff.

Chadworth stopped.

“Whose blood?”

Edmund looked down.

“Sabina’s.”

Rosamund appeared behind Chadworth.

“What did you do?”

“Nothing she didn’t bring upon herself.”

Chadworth stepped forward.

“If you touched her—”

“She attacked me.”

“Where is she?”

“Gone.”

Marcus arrived behind Rosamund.

Edmund’s expression changed.

“You brought him?”

Chadworth laughed once.

It contained no humor.

“Apparently everyone in my life has a secret opinion about everyone else in my life.”

Edmund glanced at the hidden door.

“You found Evelyn’s archive.”

“Yes.”

“Then we’re out of time.”

“For what?”

Edmund looked toward the main house.

“Sabina didn’t come here alone.”

Chadworth stared at him.

“Who is she working with?”

“That depends on how much you already know.”

“I know enough to understand that my fiancée has been terrorizing my daughters, using a false identity, stealing information about their trust, and possibly spying on them in their bedrooms.”

Edmund’s calm expression broke slightly.

“Bedrooms?”

“You didn’t know?”

“No.”

Rosamund folded her arms.

“Convenient.”

Edmund ignored her.

“Where are the girls?”

“Safe.”

“Are you sure?”

Chadworth moved closer.

“You have thirty seconds to tell me why you came into my home uninvited and started talking about my dead wife.”

Edmund looked at him.

“Because Evelyn called me the night she died.”

The hallway seemed to narrow.

“What did she say?”

“She asked me to meet her.”

“Where?”

“Your old downtown office.”

“She died thirty miles north of the city.”

“I know.”

“What did she want?”

Edmund hesitated.

“She had discovered that someone had been manipulating trust distributions.”

“I know that part.”

“She thought it led to your father.”

“My father had been dead almost eight years.”

“Yes.”

“Then explain.”

Edmund removed something from his jacket.

A small recorder.

Old.

Damaged.

“She gave me this.”

Chadworth stared.

“What is it?”

“Her insurance.”

Rosamund looked shocked.

“You had that all these years?”

“I didn’t know what was on it until six months ago.”

Marcus said, “Why?”

“It was encrypted.”

“With technology from four years ago?”

“With a physical access key.”

Edmund looked at Chadworth.

“The key was hidden inside the silver locket Evelyn left to Lorelai.”

Chadworth remembered the necklace.

Lorelai wore it only on special occasions.

“How did you get the key?”

“I didn’t. Someone else did.”

“Who?”

“Sabina.”

Rosamund whispered a curse.

Edmund continued.

“She copied the key without realizing what it opened. One of my investigators intercepted the copy.”

Chadworth’s patience broke.

“Play it.”

“Not here.”

“Play it now.”

Edmund pressed the button.

Static filled the corridor.

Then Evelyn’s voice.

Chadworth’s entire body went cold.

He had not heard it in years.

Not clearly.

Not unexpectedly.

“November seventeenth,” Evelyn said on the recording. “If Chadworth hears this, it means I was right to be afraid.”

Chadworth’s knees nearly weakened.

The recording continued.

“The theft from the children’s trust is not about money. The money is camouflage. Someone has been creating false beneficiary structures inside Prescott Holdings.”

Papers rustled.

“I found six names. Five are dead. The sixth is Sabrina Devereaux.”

Chadworth looked toward Rosamund.

Evelyn continued.

“I believe Sabrina is Arthur Prescott’s daughter.”

A long pause.

“But that is not the secret.”

Chadworth whispered, “What secret?”

The recording answered.

“She is not trying to prove she is his daughter. She is trying to prove Chadworth isn’t his son.”

Silence.

Edmund stopped the recording.

Chadworth stared at him.

“Keep playing.”

“There’s more.”

“Then play it.”

Edmund pressed the button.

Evelyn’s voice returned.

“Arthur changed the family trust shortly before his death. There is a dormant clause. If Chadworth’s biological relationship to Arthur is successfully challenged, control of several private holdings transfers to the eldest surviving biological child of Arthur Prescott.”

Rosamund whispered, “Sabina.”

Chadworth shook his head.

“That’s impossible.”

Edmund said nothing.

“My father would never create a clause like that.”

“He did.”

“You knew?”

“I learned after Evelyn died.”

Chadworth looked at him with disgust.

“And you stayed silent?”

“I was trying to protect you.”

“Everyone who betrays someone eventually says that.”

Edmund’s jaw tightened.

“Would you have believed me?”

Chadworth opened his mouth.

Then stopped.

Four years earlier, immediately after losing Evelyn, he had been impossible.

Angry.

Numb.

Obsessed with work.

If Edmund had appeared with a conspiracy involving hidden children and bloodlines, Chadworth probably would have thrown him out.

That did not excuse anything.

But it made the lie understandable.

Almost.

“Did Evelyn believe my father wasn’t my biological father?”

“No.”

“Then why was she afraid?”

“Because someone had manufactured evidence.”

Edmund restarted the recording.

“I located a laboratory report claiming Chadworth’s DNA does not match Arthur’s preserved medical samples. I believe the report is fraudulent, but I cannot prove it. The lab technician who signed it died three weeks later.”

Chadworth closed his eyes.

A pattern.

Always someone dead.

Someone missing.

Someone silenced.

Evelyn continued.

“I think Sabrina has been planning this for years. But I no longer believe she is the person in charge.”

The recording crackled.

A door sounded somewhere in the background.

Evelyn whispered quickly.

“If something happens to me, do not trust the person who benefits from everyone blaming Sabrina.”

Then the recording abruptly stopped.

Chadworth opened his eyes.

“That’s it?”

Edmund nodded.

“That was the final file.”

“Who benefited?”

“That’s what I’ve been trying to determine.”

Marcus spoke.

“Why show up today?”

Edmund looked at him coldly.

“Because Sabina called me thirty-eight minutes ago.”

Everyone became still.

“What did she say?” Chadworth asked.

“She said Chadworth had found the basement archive.”

“How would she know you cared?”

Edmund’s expression became grim.

“Because she believes I’m on her side.”

Rosamund laughed bitterly.

“Are you?”

“No.”

“How convincing.”

Edmund looked at Chadworth.

“For the last eighteen months, I’ve been letting her think I wanted control of Prescott Holdings.”

Chadworth’s face hardened.

“You’ve been meeting with my fiancée behind my back?”

“Yes.”

“How many times?”

“Twenty-three.”

Rosamund stared.

Marcus said, “Christ.”

Chadworth took one step forward.

“What happened when she came upstairs?”

“She demanded that I get her out of the mansion. I refused. She panicked. Then she attacked me with a letter opener.”

He lifted his cuff.

A shallow cut crossed his wrist.

“Where did she go?”

“East corridor.”

“That leads to the garage.”

“The garage doors are locked,” Marcus said.

Edmund looked at him.

“She doesn’t need the garage.”

Everyone turned.

“What are you talking about?”

Edmund looked toward the floor.

“There’s an old tunnel beneath the estate.”

Chadworth almost laughed again.

“How many hidden structures are in my house?”

“Your grandfather built it during the kidnapping scares of the 1970s. It runs from the wine cellar to a stone shed beyond the eastern wall.”

Marcus stared.

“That passage isn’t on any current security plan.”

“I know.”

“How does Sabina know?”

Edmund’s answer was simple.

“Evelyn showed her.”

Chadworth went cold.

“My wife knew Sabina?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

Edmund’s eyes dropped.

“They met before you did.”

Rosamund stepped closer.

“When?”

“Six months before Evelyn died.”

Chadworth felt something break open inside him.

“Why?”

“Because Evelyn went looking for Arthur Prescott’s secret daughter.”

Edmund paused.

“And she found her.”

“Why didn’t she tell me?”

“She was going to.”

“When?”

“The night she died.”

Chadworth could no longer feel his hands.

Evelyn had not been driving aimlessly.

She had not been on the highway because of some spontaneous errand.

She had been trying to reach him.

“What happened that night?”

Edmund looked toward the distant windows.

“It was raining. Evelyn met Sabrina at a restaurant outside the city. She believed Sabrina might be another victim of Arthur’s secrecy.”

“And was she?”

“At first.”

“What changed?”

“She discovered Sabrina had already been accessing the trust.”

Chadworth’s throat tightened.

“Then?”

“Evelyn called me at 9:14 p.m. She said she had proof. She was scared.”

“When did her car crash?”

“10:02.”

“Who saw her last?”

Edmund looked at Marcus.

Marcus’s face hardened.

“I followed her from the restaurant.”

Chadworth stepped toward him.

“Why?”

“Because she asked me to.”

“Did you see the crash?”

“No.”

“Then what did you see?”

Marcus hesitated.

“A black sedan.”

“What sedan?”

“It followed her onto the highway.”

“Who was driving?”

“I couldn’t see.”

“Why wasn’t this in the police report?”

“Because I never reached the scene.”

Chadworth stared.

Marcus looked ashamed for the first time.

“I got a call telling me Gemma had been rushed to the hospital.”

Chadworth remembered.

Gemma had developed a sudden breathing problem that night.

A nanny had called emergency services.

Chadworth had been at the hospital when police arrived to tell him Evelyn was dead.

He turned slowly toward Rosamund.

“You were working that night.”

Rosamund nodded.

“Was Gemma actually sick?”

Tears appeared in her eyes.

“No.”

The answer was barely audible.

Chadworth’s face went empty.

“What happened?”

“Someone put something in her milk.”

Edmund cursed under his breath.

“Who?”

Rosamund looked terrified.

“I never knew.”

Gemma’s emergency had pulled Marcus away.

It had kept Chadworth occupied.

It had emptied the house of attention.

And Evelyn had died alone on a dark highway.

Chadworth felt the conspiracy rearrange itself inside his mind.

That night had not contained one tragedy.

It had contained several coordinated events.

Then Marcus’s radio crackled.

A guard’s voice came through.

“Mr. Vale?”

Marcus answered.

“Go.”

“We found Ms. Laurent.”

Sabina.

“Where?”

A pause.

“East grounds.”

Marcus looked at Chadworth.

The guard continued.

“She’s not alone.”

“Who is with her?”

Another pause.

Then:

“Lorelai Prescott.”

Chadworth ran.

## Chapter 5 - THE GIRL AT THE EASTERN GATE

-5

Chadworth did not remember crossing the foyer.

He did not remember pushing open the side doors.

He remembered only rain.

A light autumn rain had begun falling over the estate, turning the stone pathways dark and shining beneath the security lights.

Somewhere beyond the hedges, a guard shouted.

Chadworth ran toward the eastern wall.

“Lorelai!”

No answer.

Marcus was behind him.

Edmund farther back.

Rosamund had gone upstairs to check on Gemma.

Chadworth reached the old stone shed near the perimeter just as one of the security guards emerged from behind it.

“Sir!”

“Where is she?”

The guard pointed.

“Inside.”

Chadworth entered.

The shed smelled of wet earth and old wood.

A trapdoor stood open in the floor.

Beside it lay one of Lorelai’s slippers.

Chadworth picked it up.

His hand shook.

“How did she get here?”

“We don’t know.”

“You said you found them.”

“We saw Ms. Laurent entering. There was a smaller figure with her.”

Marcus took a flashlight.

“Tunnel.”

Chadworth stepped onto the ladder.

“Sir, I should go first.”

“You can follow.”

He descended.

The tunnel was narrow and damp.

Old electrical bulbs cast weak circles of yellow light every fifteen feet.

Ahead, footsteps echoed.

“Lorelai!”

This time a voice answered.

“Daddy!”

Chadworth ran.

He rounded a corner.

Lorelai stood alone beneath one of the lights.

He reached her and dropped to his knees.

“Are you hurt?”

She shook her head.

“What happened?”

“She told me Rosamund was in trouble.”

“Sabina?”

“Yes.”

“Where is she?”

Lorelai pointed farther down the tunnel.

“She ran.”

Chadworth pulled his daughter into his arms.

Marcus arrived.

“Take her back.”

Lorelai grabbed Chadworth’s jacket.

“No.”

“You’re going upstairs.”

“No, Daddy.”

“Lorelai.”

“She wanted me to open something.”

Chadworth stopped.

“What?”

Lorelai reached into her pocket.

She removed the silver locket Evelyn had left her.

Chadworth stared.

“The key.”

Edmund had said Sabina copied the key hidden inside it.

Lorelai opened the locket.

Inside, behind Evelyn’s photograph, was a tiny metal strip.

“She said Mommy left something down here that belonged to me.”

“Did she touch the locket?”

“No. I ran when I heard the guards.”

Marcus looked down the tunnel.

“She may still be inside.”

Chadworth stood.

“You’re taking Lorelai back.”

Marcus shook his head.

“I’m not leaving you unprotected.”

Edmund finally caught up.

“I’ll take her.”

Lorelai recoiled from him.

Chadworth noticed.

“Why are you afraid of Uncle Edmund?”

Edmund looked wounded.

“I don’t know.”

Lorelai whispered, “Sabina said he killed Mommy.”

Silence.

Edmund’s face changed.

“That’s a lie.”

“Why would she tell you that?” Chadworth asked.

“She said if I ever saw you talking to Uncle Edmund about Mommy, I should run away because he’d hurt me too.”

Edmund closed his eyes.

The manipulation was extraordinary.

Sabina had planted different fears in everyone.

Rosamund was dangerous.

Edmund was a murderer.

The girls were unstable.

Chadworth was paranoid.

Each person had been given a separate version of reality.

Enough contradictions to keep them from ever comparing stories.

“We go together,” Chadworth said.

Marcus frowned.

“With Lorelai?”

“I’m not handing her to anyone until I know exactly who I can trust.”

They moved deeper into the tunnel.

Lorelai held Chadworth’s hand.

After several minutes, the passage widened.

A steel door stood ahead.

Unlike the rest of the tunnel, it was modern.

Electronic keypad.

Biometric reader.

Chadworth stared.

“This was not built in the seventies.”

Edmund shook his head.

“No.”

Lorelai held up the locket.

“Sabina said Mommy’s key opens it.”

Marcus examined the lock.

“There’s a slot.”

Chadworth took the metal strip from the locket.

It fit perfectly.

The keypad illuminated.

A recorded voice spoke.

“Secondary verification required.”

Lorelai gasped.

Chadworth knew the voice.

Evelyn.

A small microphone activated.

Then another recording:

“Lorelai, if you’re old enough to understand this, tell me the name of the story I read you every night when Gemma was born.”

Lorelai began crying.

Chadworth knew the answer too.

But the system was clearly waiting for her.

She whispered, “The Moon Garden.”

The lock clicked.

The door opened.

Inside was a room filled with filing cabinets.

Computer equipment.

Boxes.

A desk.

And a wall covered with photographs connected by lines.

Arthur Prescott.

Marianne Devereaux.

Sabrina.

Edmund.

Marcus.

Several board members.

Lawyers.

Doctors.

And Chadworth himself.

At the center was a photograph of Evelyn.

Lorelai stared.

“Mommy made this?”

Marcus walked toward the computers.

“Looks like it.”

Edmund stood frozen before one section of the wall.

“What?”

Chadworth asked.

Edmund pointed at a photograph.

A man in his late sixties.

Chadworth recognized him.

Dr. Theodore Voss.

A respected neurologist.

The same doctor who had treated Arthur Prescott during the final year of his life.

And the same doctor Sabina had recommended for Gemma’s anxiety.

Chadworth’s stomach turned.

“There’s a note.”

Edmund read aloud.

“Voss altered medical samples.”

Marcus opened a folder beneath the photograph.

Inside were lab reports.

DNA records.

Payment receipts.

Arthur Prescott’s preserved samples.

Chadworth read the dates.

Several had been created after Arthur was already dead.

“Fabricated.”

“Yes,” Marcus said.

Lorelai walked toward Evelyn’s desk.

“Daddy.”

A red envelope rested beside an old computer.

Written across the front:

FOR CHADWORTH ONLY.

He opened it.

Inside was a handwritten letter.

Chad,

If you are reading this, then I did not get the chance to explain everything myself.

The most important thing I discovered is that Sabrina Devereaux is not your enemy in the way you think.

She is dangerous.

She has lied.

She has stolen.

And I believe she has done terrible things.

But someone spent years turning her hatred toward you.

Someone taught her that your family stole her life before she was even born.

Someone funded her.

Someone gave her access.

And that person is inside Prescott Holdings.

Do not follow the money.

Follow the medical records.

Chadworth looked at Dr. Voss’s photograph.

Edmund opened another drawer.

“Chad.”

Inside were medical charts belonging to Gemma and Lorelai.

Dozens.

Most Chadworth had never seen.

Some were stamped with Voss’s private clinic.

Gemma: anxiety.

Lorelai: oppositional behavior.

Gemma: hallucinations.

Lorelai: violent ideation.

None of it was true.

His daughters were being given fake medical histories.

Exactly what Rosamund had warned.

Then Marcus found another folder.

This one bore Chadworth’s name.

Inside were blood tests.

Brain scans.

Psychiatric assessments.

His face hardened.

“I’ve never had these tests.”

Edmund took one.

The conclusion at the bottom read:

Early-onset frontotemporal degeneration.

Impaired executive judgment.

Progressive loss of capacity expected.

Chadworth read it twice.

Someone was building a medical case to declare him mentally incompetent.

Not dead.

Incompetent.

That meant they did not need to murder him.

They only needed to convince a court he could no longer control his company or his daughters.

“Sabina wanted guardianship,” Rosamund had said.

Now Chadworth understood.

Not merely of the children.

Of everything.

Lorelai opened a small box on Evelyn’s desk.

Inside were prescription bottles.

Chadworth recognized one.

The sound still felt unfamiliar inside the mansion.

Rosamund approached from the terrace carrying an envelope.

“This came for you.”

“No return address?”

“No.”

For a second, the old fear returned.

Hidden messages.

Secret threats.

Another layer.

He opened it.

Inside was a photograph.

Evelyn.

Standing beside the girls years earlier.

Chadworth had never seen it.

Written on the back in her handwriting were the words:

A house is only worth protecting if the people inside it feel safe.

He stared at the sentence.

Rosamund smiled.

“I found it inside Daniel’s old files.”

Chadworth looked toward his daughters.

For decades, he had believed protection meant walls.

Money.

Guards.

Cameras.

Control.

But cameras had watched his children suffer while he was too absent to see them.

Money had attracted people who wanted to own his life.

Security had created rooms where secrets grew.

The thing his daughters had needed most was embarrassingly simple.

A father who noticed.

He folded the photograph carefully.

Gemma climbed into his lap.

“Are you going away again?”

He looked at her.

“Eventually. For work.”

Her face fell slightly.

“But not today.”

She smiled.

“Tomorrow?”

“No.”

“Next week?”

“Probably not.”

Lorelai looked suspicious.

“What about Canada?”

Chadworth laughed.

“Definitely not Canada.”

Both girls laughed with him.

Then Lorelai became serious.

“Daddy?”

“Yes?”

“Why did you pretend to leave that day?”

Chadworth looked toward the mansion.

He could have said he wanted evidence.

He could have said he was protecting them.

He could have said Sabina manipulated him.

All of those things were partly true.

Instead, he gave her the answer she deserved.

“Because I didn’t trust the right people.”

Lorelai thought about that.

“Do you now?”

Chadworth looked at Rosamund.

At Gemma.

At Lorelai.

Then at Evelyn’s photograph.

“I’m learning.”

That evening, when darkness settled over the estate, the mansion looked almost exactly the way it had one year earlier.

Same windows.

Same stone walls.

Same iron gates.

But the lights stayed on.

Chadworth stood at the upstairs landing while his daughters argued cheerfully about which movie to watch.

For once, he did not feel the need to check a camera.

He simply walked toward their voices.

Far beneath the mansion, behind a sealed steel door, one final piece of Evelyn’s archive remained undiscovered.

A tiny encrypted drive hidden inside the wall.

Investigators would find it three weeks later.

It contained only one video.

Evelyn sat alone before the camera.

The recording had been made two days before her death.

She looked exhausted.

Frightened.

But calm.

“If Chadworth ever finds this,” she said, “then he probably already knows what happened.”

She paused.

“There is one thing I never told him.”

Chadworth, watching the recovered recording weeks later, leaned closer.

Evelyn continued.

“Arthur Prescott was not Sabrina Devereaux’s father.”

Chadworth’s heart stopped.

“The DNA evidence proving that was real.”

A pause.

“But Sabrina was still connected to the family.”

Chadworth stared at the screen.

Evelyn looked directly into the camera.

“Marianne Devereaux’s child was fathered by Jonathan Crane.”

The revelation hit harder than Chadworth expected.

Sabina had spent her entire life chasing the wrong father.

Crane had known.

Of course he had known.

He had taken his own abandoned daughter’s anger and aimed it at the Prescott family.

He had convinced her that Arthur had rejected her.

He had manufactured DNA evidence.

He had transformed his own child into a weapon while never telling her who she really was.

Evelyn’s final words came quietly.

“That is why Crane never allowed Sabrina to die.”

The recording ended.

Chadworth sat alone for a long time.

Then he requested permission through Sabina’s attorney to send her one document.

No message.

No explanation.

Only the verified paternity report.

Weeks later, her attorney told him she had opened the envelope and stared at the first page for almost an hour.

She did not cry.

She did not speak.

She simply read the name of her biological father again and again.

Jonathan Crane.

The man who had spent decades teaching her to hate the Prescott family.

The man who had promised to help her reclaim what had been stolen.

The man who had told her she was Arthur Prescott’s forgotten daughter.

Her real father had never been the billionaire who supposedly abandoned her.

It had been the man standing beside her the entire time.

And that was the final cruelty of the ARGENT system.

It did not merely falsify documents.

It falsified lives.

Chadworth eventually shut down the old Prescott family office completely.

ARGENT was dismantled under federal supervision.

The original source code was preserved only as evidence.

Arthur’s recordings were sealed.

The underground server vault was emptied.

When reporters asked Chadworth whether destroying his father’s succession system endangered the future of Prescott Holdings, he gave an answer none of them expected.

“Companies are supposed to serve people.”

He paused.

“Not the other way around.”

Months later, Lorelai stood beside him during the dedication of Evelyn House, a legal and medical advocacy center funded using assets recovered from the corrupted trust network.

Gemma held Rosamund’s hand.

Daniel stood nearby.

Marcus remained at the edge of the crowd, watching everyone the way security men always did.

But this time there were no hidden motives behind the watching.

Chadworth looked at his daughters.

They looked different.

Not completely healed.

Healing did not work like that.

Gemma still woke from nightmares sometimes.

Lorelai still became tense when adults argued.

Both girls attended therapy with doctors independently selected and reviewed.

Chadworth attended therapy too.

That surprised the business press more than the criminal investigation.

He did not care.

He had spent years believing vulnerability was something wealthy men paid other people to handle.

He knew better now.

That night, back at the mansion, Gemma fell asleep on the sofa.

Lorelai curled up beside her.

Chadworth carried Gemma upstairs.

When he returned, Lorelai was awake.

“Dad?”

“Yes?”

“Do you ever think about what would have happened if you actually went to Canada?”

He stopped.

The question had visited him many nights.

If the plane had taken off.

If he had ignored Sabina’s accusation.

If he had trusted her completely.

Gemma might have disappeared.

He might have continued swallowing the drugs.

Rosamund might have been framed.

Lorelai might have been placed under Sabina’s guardianship.

Daniel might have died underground.

Claire might still be controlling families.

Crane might have continued operating ARGENT.

And Chadworth might eventually have become another tragic billionaire whose decline seemed obvious only in retrospect.

“Yes,” he said.

“What do you think would have happened?”

He sat beside her.

“Something terrible.”

“Then pretending was good?”

He considered the question.

“No.”

Lorelai frowned.

“But it saved us.”

“It helped me discover the truth.”

“That sounds good.”

“It was.”

He smiled.

“But I wish I had discovered the truth because you knew you could tell me.”

Lorelai became quiet.

Then she leaned against him.

“You know now.”

“Yes.”

Outside, rain began touching the windows.

For a moment, the sound reminded Chadworth of Evelyn’s final night.

The highway.

The black sedan.

The dark road.

But the memory no longer ended only with death.

It led here.

To the daughters she had tried to protect.

To Rosamund, whom she had trusted.

To Daniel, whom she had refused to forget.

To evidence hidden behind walls because Evelyn understood that truth sometimes needed years to survive.

Chadworth kissed Lorelai’s forehead.

“Bed.”

She stood.

At the staircase, she turned.

“Dad?”

“Yes?”

“Leave the hallway light on.”

He looked toward the switch.

Once, the Prescott mansion had been designed to become dark automatically after midnight.

Efficient.

Elegant.

Controlled.

Chadworth smiled.

“All night.”

Lorelai disappeared upstairs.

Chadworth switched on the hallway light.

Then another.

And another.

Until the corridor glowed warmly from one end of the mansion to the other.

A year earlier, he had switched off the lights, carried a suitcase through the front door, and pretended to leave because he believed the greatest danger in his home was a quiet housekeeper.

Instead, the cameras had shown him the truth.

The woman he mistrusted had been protecting his daughters.

The woman he intended to marry had been terrifying them.

The doctor he trusted had been poisoning him.

The friend he doubted had been manipulated.

The father whose legacy he protected had created the foundation of a system that nearly destroyed his family.

And the elderly attorney everyone respected had turned generations of fear into an invisible empire built from forged realities.

Chadworth Prescott had spent his entire life believing power meant knowing everything that happened inside the walls he owned.

He finally understood.

Real power was making sure the people inside those walls never had to be afraid of telling you the truth.

He stood alone beneath the warm hallway lights for another moment.

Then he heard Gemma call from upstairs.

“Daddy!”

A year earlier, he might have answered while checking his phone.

He might have sent Rosamund.

He might have promised to come in a minute and forgotten.

This time, he went immediately.

And somewhere behind him, for the first time in years, not a single hidden camera was watching.
