The air inside Courtroom 3B smelled of damp wool, decades of floor wax, and the quiet desperation of everyone waiting on the wooden benches. Outside, the morning traffic on the county loop crept past in a gray drizzle, but in here, the fluorescent tubes hummed a steady, flat C-note that seemed to press directly against my temples.

Sarah Vance slid a manila folder across the oak table, her movements crisp and economical. She didn't look up from her legal pad, where her fountain pen was sketching small, precise geometric shapes in the margin. "He brought two corporate litigators from the city," she murmured, her voice pitched low beneath the drone of the clerk reading docket numbers. "They’re going to argue jurisdiction, and they’re going to hammer the hallway incident until the paint peels off the walls."

"It wasn't an incident," I said, my jaw tightening as I remembered the hollow slap of my hand against Richard’s cheek. "It was an intervention."

"To the law, Marcus, it's a battery. A minor one, but right now, Richard has a clean medical report from the county hospital documenting a bruised zygoma, and you have a handful of tactical officers who would rather be anywhere else than testifying about a basement door." She finally turned her sharp, dark eyes toward me. "So we keep the focus off your temper and keep it entirely on the chain."

When Judge Callahan took the bench, the room fell into a heavy, respectful hush. Callahan was a graying man with deep lines around his mouth, the kind of judge who had seen every permutation of human misery and had little patience for theatricality. He adjusted his half-moon glasses, scanned the emergency petition Sarah had filed at dawn, and fixed his gaze on Richard’s legal team.

Richard sat three tables down, wearing a charcoal suit that cost more than my first car. The red mark on his cheek had faded to a dull, yellowish bruise, artfully half-hidden under stage makeup or careful lighting. He didn't look back at me, but his posture was rigid, projecting the immaculate, aggrieved innocence of a pillar of the community whose home had been unjustly invaded by overzealous neighbors and rogue officers.

"Mr. Vance," Judge Callahan said, his voice gravelly and unhurried. "Your counsel has filed for an immediate dismissal of the protective custody order, citing procedural overreach and an unlawful breach of private residence. Care to summarize?"

Richard stood, buttoning his jacket with slow, deliberate grace. He looked every inch the successful foundation director, his white shirt blindingly crisp against his dark lapels. "Your Honor, what occurred yesterday afternoon was nothing short of a home invasion carried out under the influence of a deeply disturbed individual—Mr. Marcus here—who has harbored an irrational obsession with my family." He paused, letting a flicker of genuine sorrow cross his features. "My daughter Lily has been subjected to severe emotional manipulation. She was frightened by armed men bursting into our home, all because of wild, baseless accusations regarding a basement storage room that happens to require a heavy security latch to protect hazardous tools from a child."

A murmur rippled through the gallery. Sarah didn't flinch. She simply waited for Richard to finish before rising with her exhibits.

"Your Honor, if I may direct the court’s attention to Exhibit A," Sarah said, her voice cutting cleanly through the courtroom acoustics. She handed up a series of high-resolution photographs taken by the responding tactical unit.

The color prints showed the macro view of the heavy iron chain threaded tightly through the brass padlock securing the metal basement door, alongside wide-angle shots revealing the interior deadbolts, the lack of any light source, and the small, scratched-up cot pushed into the corner of the damp concrete floor.

Judge Callahan stared down at the photographs for a long, silent minute. The hum of the fluorescent lights seemed to grow louder.

"Tools, Mr. Vance?" the judge asked quietly, not looking up from the images.

Richard’s defense counsel was on his feet instantly. "Objection, Your Honor, those photographs lack proper chain-of-custody authentication from an accredited forensic structural engineer—"

"Overruled, Counsel," Callahan snapped, finally lifting his eyes to fix Richard with a cold, level stare. "I've seen tool lockers in my forty years on the bench, and I've seen prison cells. This court is not inclined to dissolve the protective custody order for the minor child, Lily Vance, while these questions remain entirely unanswered."

A sharp exhale hissed out of Richard’s teeth, though his expression remained smooth.

"However," Callahan continued, turning his gaze toward Sarah and me, "Mr. Marcus took the law into his own hands in the hallway. That kind of vigilantism will not be tolerated in this jurisdiction. I am upholding the temporary restraining order keeping Mr. Marcus away from the child pending a full psychological evaluation and a formal child services report. Furthermore, supervised visitation is the only avenue this court will entertain moving forward."

Sarah gave a minimal nod, accepting the compromise without argument. "Thank you, Your Honor."

As the gavel came down, signaling the end of the morning docket, Richard gathered his leather portfolio. As he walked past our table toward the aisle, he paused for just a fraction of a second. His eyes met mine, cold and entirely devoid of the warmth he projected to the public.

"You haven't won anything," he whispered, low enough for only Sarah and me to hear. "The clinic records are sealed. Elena is gone. You’re tilting at windmills, Marcus."

He walked out flanked by his high-priced attorneys, leaving Sarah and me sitting in the emptying courtroom.

"He's rattled," Sarah said, packing her pens back into her leather case. "He wouldn't have brought up the clinic if he didn't think we were getting close."

"What clinic?" I asked, watching the heavy oak doors swing shut behind Richard's retreating back.

"The one he mentioned just now," Sarah said, freezing mid-motion. "He just told us Elena isn't dead, Marcus. He just told us she's somewhere he put her."
