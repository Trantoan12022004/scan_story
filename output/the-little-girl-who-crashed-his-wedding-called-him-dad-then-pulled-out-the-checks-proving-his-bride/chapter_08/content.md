Not bridal white.

Not mourning black exactly.

Something between widow and victim.

Her hair was pulled back. Her makeup was soft. A small bandage showed at her wrist from the arrest. She looked fragile enough for cameras and expensive enough for donors.

Three microphones stood before her outside Hale Tower.

Her lawyer stood at her right.

Her father at her left.

Victoria looked into the cameras and began to cry on command.

“Yesterday should have been the happiest day of my life.”

Ethan watched from Olivia’s hospital room with Lucy beside him and Daniel standing behind them.

Olivia refused to watch, but she could not stop listening.

Victoria continued.

“Instead, I was publicly attacked by a troubled young girl and a woman from Ethan’s past who has spent years attempting to exploit his family.”

Lucy stiffened.

Ethan turned off the sound.

“No,” Lucy said. “I want to hear.”

“You don’t need to.”

“I already know what lies sound like.”

Ethan turned the volume back on.

Victoria’s voice trembled beautifully.

“I have made mistakes. Years ago, Olivia Bennett came to me asking for financial help. She was pregnant, frightened, and alone. I helped her quietly because I believed it was the compassionate thing to do.”

Olivia let out a bitter laugh from the bed.

“She calls checks compassion now.”

Victoria dabbed her eyes.

“Those payments were not bribes. They were support. Any claim that I forced Ms. Bennett to disappear is false. As for the accusations of kidnapping and violence, I trust the legal process.”

A reporter shouted, “Did you sign the checks?”

Victoria paused perfectly.

“Yes. I did. Because Olivia threatened to destroy Ethan if I didn’t pay.”

Ethan’s face hardened.

Another reporter shouted, “Is Lucy Ethan Pierce’s daughter?”

Victoria inhaled as if wounded.

“That is a private family matter, and I will not exploit a child for headlines.”

Lucy whispered, “She already did.”

Then Charles Hale stepped forward.

“My daughter has been the target of emotional manipulation. We ask the public to withhold judgment.”

Ethan muted the television.

Daniel swore softly.

“She’s good.”

“She’s desperate,” Ethan said.

Olivia looked at him.

“Desperate people are dangerous.”

Ethan’s phone rang.

His lawyer.

He answered.

“Tell me.”

The lawyer’s voice was tense.

“Victoria’s team filed an emergency petition questioning Olivia’s fitness as a parent.”

Ethan looked at Olivia.

“What?”

“They’re claiming Olivia endangered Lucy by bringing her into a public confrontation, exposing her to criminal activity, and living without stable housing. They’re asking for temporary protective review.”

Lucy’s face went blank.

Olivia tried to sit up.

“No. No, they can’t.”

Ethan’s voice went deadly calm.

“What are they really asking for?”

A pause.

“They’re trying to get Lucy removed from Olivia’s custody before the criminal case advances.”

Lucy grabbed Olivia’s hand.

“I won’t go.”

Ethan looked at Detective Klein.

Klein had heard enough.

“They’re using family court to pressure a witness.”

Ethan spoke into the phone.

“File whatever stops it.”

“We need a stable placement option.”

Ethan did not hesitate.

“Me.”

Olivia stared at him.

His lawyer paused.

“Ethan, you understand what that means?”

“It means my daughter comes home with me if her mother can’t leave the hospital.”

Lucy looked at him like she was afraid to believe it.

Olivia whispered, “Ethan…”

He looked at her.

“No one is taking her.”

The hearing happened the next morning.

Family court was smaller than Ethan expected and crueler in quiet ways. No cameras. No shouting guests. Just polished wood, tired clerks, and adults speaking about Lucy as if she were a file.

Victoria did not attend.

Her attorney did.

He painted Olivia as unstable. Transient. Unreliable. He spoke of motel rooms and unpaid bills. He suggested Lucy had been coached.

Ethan listened until his hands hurt from clenching.

Then his lawyer stood.

“We have DNA confirmation that Mr. Pierce is Lucy’s biological father. We have an active criminal investigation involving Ms. Hale’s alleged threats against both mother and child. We have hospital records showing Ms. Bennett was likely assaulted and possibly poisoned. Removing Lucy from her mother and father at this stage would reward the very intimidation this court should prevent.”

The judge looked at Ethan.

“Mr. Pierce, are you prepared to assume temporary guardianship responsibilities if Ms. Bennett remains hospitalized?”

Ethan stood.

“Yes, Your Honor.”

“Do you understand this is not symbolic?”

“Yes.”

“You will be responsible for schooling, medical care, housing, supervision, and emotional support.”

Ethan glanced at Lucy.

She sat beside Olivia’s court-appointed advocate, shoulders small beneath Daniel’s oversized jacket.

“I understand I’m twelve years late,” Ethan said. “I intend not to be late again.”

The courtroom went quiet.

The judge granted joint temporary custody to Olivia and Ethan, with Lucy to remain with Ethan while Olivia received medical treatment. Victoria’s petition was denied.

Lucy did not react until they were outside.

Then she walked up to Ethan and asked, “Do I have to call your house my house?”

He crouched.

“Only when you’re ready.”

“What if I’m never ready?”

“Then it can just be a place where your toothbrush lives.”

She thought about that.

“Do you have cereal?”

“Yes.”

“Good cereal?”

“I’m rich, Lucy.”

For the first time, she laughed.

It was small.

It was everything.

That evening, Ethan brought Lucy to his penthouse.

The elevator opened into marble floors, glass walls, and a city view glittering beneath the rain.

Lucy stepped inside and froze.

“This is bigger than our motel.”

Ethan tried to smile.

“Most things are.”

She wandered carefully, touching nothing.

Her backpack had been returned by police, cleaned but still stained. She carried it into the guest room Ethan had prepared in six frantic hours.

There were new clothes, books, art supplies, and a bed with a yellow blanket because Daniel had said yellow felt less sad than white.

Lucy stood in the doorway.

“Is this mine?”

“Yes.”

“For how long?”

“As long as you need.”

She looked at the bed.

Then at him.

“Can I sleep on the couch?”

Ethan blinked.

“Why?”

“Rooms with doors feel weird.”

His chest tightened.

“Then we’ll put blankets on the couch.”

She nodded, relieved.

At midnight, after Lucy finally fell asleep under three blankets with every light in the living room on, Ethan stepped onto the balcony.

His phone buzzed.

A message from Victoria.

No number blocked it this time.

Just six words.

You have no idea what Olivia did.

Then a video file appeared.

Ethan opened it.

The screen showed Olivia twelve years earlier, standing outside Victoria’s house, crying.

Her voice shook.

“I’ll take the money. I’ll disappear. Just promise Ethan never finds out the baby lived.”

The video ended.

Ethan stared at the dark screen.

Behind him, Lucy slept peacefully for the first time in days.

And Ethan realized Victoria’s next move was not to deny the checks.

It was to destroy Olivia with them.
