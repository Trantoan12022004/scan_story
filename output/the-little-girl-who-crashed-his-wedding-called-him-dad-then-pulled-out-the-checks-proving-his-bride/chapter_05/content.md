It had been Ethan’s childhood home, though it had never felt warm enough to deserve the word home. Every room was tasteful. Every portrait was expensive. Every silence had rules.

Margaret greeted him in the front sitting room with tea already poured.

That was how Ethan knew she had expected him.

She wore pearls, a cream cardigan, and the expression of a woman prepared to survive disappointment by pretending it was inconvenience.

“Ethan,” she said. “You look terrible.”

He placed Olivia’s letter on the table between them.

“You knew.”

Margaret’s face did not change.

Daniel stood near the door. Detective Klein had insisted on coming, but Margaret’s attorney had arrived faster. Lucy waited in the car with a female officer because Ethan could not bear for her to hear this part.

Margaret glanced at the letter.

“Olivia always was dramatic.”

Ethan felt something in him snap.

“She was pregnant.”

“I know.”

The room fell silent.

There it was.

Not denial.

Not confusion.

Just confession spoken like a weather report.

Ethan stared at his mother.

“You knew Lucy existed?”

Margaret lifted her teacup.

“I knew Olivia claimed the baby was yours.”

“She was mine.”

“So it appears.”

Ethan laughed, but there was no humor in it.

“So it appears? That’s all you have?”

Margaret set the cup down.

“You were twenty-six. Reckless. Emotional. Olivia was sweet, yes, but completely unsuitable for the life ahead of you.”

“She was the woman I loved.”

“You loved many things that would have ruined you.”

Ethan stepped toward her.

“My daughter grew up hiding in motels.”

Margaret’s lips tightened.

“I did not arrange that.”

“No. You just looked away.”

Her eyes sharpened.

“You think love is enough because no one has ever made you carry the weight of a family name. Your father was dying. Pierce Holdings was vulnerable. The Hales were offering stability, political connections, capital. Olivia Bennett was a waitress with debts and no protection.”

“She needed protection from you.”

Margaret’s hand trembled slightly.

For the first time, Ethan saw it.

Not guilt.

Fear.

“What did Victoria tell you?” he asked.

Margaret looked toward the windows.

“That Olivia intended to trap you. That she had admitted the child was not yours. That she wanted money.”

“And you believed her?”

“I believed what was convenient.”

The honesty was uglier than a lie.

Ethan looked at the woman who had raised him.

“You let me hate Olivia.”

“I let you recover.”

“No. You let me become useful.”

Margaret stood.

“Be careful.”

“Why? Afraid I’ll embarrass the family?”

“I am afraid you will burn your life down for a woman who has returned at the perfect moment with a child and a box of accusations.”

Ethan moved so close she had to look up at him.

“Say one more word about Olivia using me, and I will forget you are my mother.”

The attorney cleared his throat.

“Mrs. Pierce, I advise—”

“Shut up,” Margaret said.

Everyone froze.

Her perfect mask finally cracked.

She looked at Ethan with eyes suddenly wet.

“You have no idea what Charles Hale is capable of.”

Ethan went still.

“What does Charles have on you?”

Margaret sank slowly back into her chair.

For the first time in Ethan’s memory, she looked old.

“After your father died, the company was nearly insolvent. He had hidden losses. Bad loans. Quiet settlements. Charles offered help before the board found out.”

“And the price was Victoria?”

“The price was alliance.”

“The price was my child.”

Margaret closed her eyes.

“I didn’t know about Lucy then.”

“But later?”

She said nothing.

That was enough.

Ethan picked up the letter.

“You will give Detective Klein everything. Every email. Every transaction. Every conversation you remember.”

Margaret looked up.

“And if I don’t?”

Ethan’s voice turned cold.

“Then I will make sure Lucy knows exactly which grandmother chose money over her life.”

Margaret flinched as if struck.

Outside, thunder rolled.

When Ethan returned to the car, Lucy was drawing on the fogged window with her fingertip. She quickly wiped it away, but not before he saw the word.

Dad.

His chest tightened.

She looked at him.

“Was she mean?”

“Yes.”

“Did she know about me?”

He sat beside her.

“For part of it.”

Lucy looked down at her shoes.

“Oh.”

That small word contained more pain than crying would have.

Ethan reached out slowly, giving her time to pull away. She did not.

He took her hand.

“That is her failure. Not yours.”

Lucy’s mouth trembled.

“Did she hate me?”

“No.” Ethan swallowed. “She didn’t know how to love anything more than power.”

Lucy thought about that.

“Mom knows how.”

“Yes,” Ethan whispered. “She does.”

His phone buzzed.

A text from an unknown number.

There was no message.

Only a photo.

Ethan opened it and went rigid.

It showed Olivia’s hospital room door.

Taken from inside the hallway.

A second text arrived.

NO POLICE CAN WATCH EVERY DOOR.

Ethan threw open the car door.

“Drive!”

The police escort followed as they sped back toward the hospital, but traffic had thickened and rain had flooded two intersections.

Ethan called Daniel.

No answer.

He called the nurses’ station.

Busy.

He called Detective Klein.

She answered on the first ring.

“I just got a threat from inside the hospital,” he said.

“We’re two minutes behind you.”

“Olivia’s room. Now.”

Lucy’s face went white.

“Mom?”

Ethan drove through a red light.

By the time they reached the hospital, alarms were sounding.

A nurse ran down the hallway.

A security guard lay on the floor, groaning.

Ethan sprinted past him.

Room 312 was empty.

The bed sheets were torn away.

The IV stand had fallen.

Olivia was gone.

Lucy screamed.

Ethan stood in the middle of the room, staring at the empty bed.

Then he saw something on the pillow.

A white rose.

From the wedding bouquet.

And beneath it, a note written in Victoria’s perfect hand.

Now you know how it feels to lose her.
