Detective Klein shouted into her radio from the passenger seat, demanding units at Pierce Tower immediately. Ethan barely heard her.

The only thing in his mind was Lucy asleep on his couch, trusting a home that had barely become safe.

The elevator ride to the penthouse felt endless.

Klein held her weapon ready.

Ethan held nothing but terror.

When the doors opened, the first thing he saw was Daniel on the floor.

Blood on his forehead.

One officer kneeling beside him.

The living room lights blazed.

The couch blankets were thrown aside.

Lucy was gone.

Ethan’s heart stopped.

“No.”

The injured officer looked up.

“Mr. Pierce, she ran.”

“What?”

“She wasn’t taken. She ran out the service corridor before we could stop her.”

Ethan turned toward Daniel.

Daniel groaned.

“She hit me with a lamp.”

Despite everything, Ethan stared.

“Lucy?”

Daniel touched his bleeding forehead.

“Kid’s got survival instincts.”

Klein lowered her weapon but not her guard.

“Why would she run?”

Ethan saw the tablet on the floor.

The leaked video was still frozen on the screen.

Olivia crying.

Agreeing to disappear.

Lucy had watched her mother being destroyed by strangers.

Then she had heard Ethan leave.

And fear had done the rest.

Ethan checked the service corridor camera.

Lucy had left wearing Daniel’s jacket and carrying her backpack.

Alone.

Again.

This time into a city that now knew her face.

Klein organized a search, but Ethan knew Lucy better than the police did in one terrible way.

She ran toward proof.

“Storage unit,” he said.

Klein looked at him.

“Maybe.”

“No. She thinks evidence fixes things.”

Daniel sat up with help from a paramedic.

“She took the blue folder.”

Ethan closed his eyes.

Lucy was going to defend Olivia herself.

At eleven years old.

In the middle of a media war.

They found her three hours later outside Hale Tower.

Not inside.

Not at the police station.

Outside the press entrance, standing in Daniel’s oversized jacket while reporters shouted questions around her.

Ethan pushed through the crowd with Klein and two officers.

“Lucy!”

She turned.

Her face was pale, but her eyes were fierce.

She held up the blue folder.

“My mom didn’t sell me!” she shouted at the cameras.

The reporters surged.

“Lucy, did Olivia coach you?”

“Are you really Ethan’s daughter?”

“Do you blame Victoria Hale?”

Ethan reached her and pulled her behind him.

“Back off!”

One camera hit his shoulder.

Klein barked orders and officers forced space around them.

Lucy fought Ethan’s hold.

“They have to know!”

“They will,” he said. “But not like this.”

“No one listens unless you scream!”

The words stunned him.

Because she was right.

The world had ignored Olivia’s quiet truth for twelve years. It had only listened when Lucy screamed in a chapel.

Ethan knelt in front of her amid flashing cameras.

“I’m listening now.”

Her anger broke.

“You left.”

“I went to get evidence.”

“You left like everyone leaves.”

He took the accusation because he deserved part of it.

“I came back.”

She shook her head.

“What if next time you don’t?”

“Then you call me. You yell. You throw another lamp at Daniel if you must. But you don’t run alone into a crowd of people who want to turn your pain into entertainment.”

Daniel, arriving behind them with a bandage on his forehead, muttered, “Let’s not encourage the lamp thing.”

Lucy almost laughed through tears.

Then the crowd went strangely quiet.

Ethan turned.

Victoria Hale stood at the top of the building steps.

She had not expected Lucy.

For once, her face was unprepared.

Lucy saw her and pulled away from Ethan.

“You stole my mom’s life!”

Victoria looked around at the cameras, quickly rebuilding herself.

“Lucy, sweetheart—”

“I’m not your sweetheart.”

The cameras captured every word.

Victoria smiled sadly.

“You’re confused. Adults have used you.”

Lucy opened the folder and pulled out the newborn photo.

“You took me from the shelter.”

Victoria’s smile froze.

“I have no idea what you’re talking about.”

“Yes, you do.” Lucy’s voice shook but carried. “Mom said you told her babies have accidents.”

A reporter shouted, “Ms. Hale, did you remove Lucy from a shelter?”

Victoria’s lawyer rushed forward.

“No questions!”

But the damage had begun.

Then Margaret Pierce stepped out of a car across the street.

Ethan stared.

His mother walked through the crowd slowly, pearls shining under streetlights, face pale but determined.

She stopped beside Lucy.

“I heard Charles Hale admit the child was a problem,” Margaret said clearly. “I heard him say Reed should have taken care of Olivia Bennett years ago.”

The reporters exploded.

Victoria’s eyes widened.

“Margaret, don’t.”

Margaret looked at her.

“I already did too little.”

Charles Hale appeared behind Victoria.

His face was thunder.

“You foolish woman.”

Detective Klein moved immediately.

“Charles Hale, we need you to come with us.”

He laughed.

“For what?”

Klein held up her phone.

“For starters? Conspiracy, witness intimidation, and obstruction. We’ll build from there.”

Charles looked at Victoria.

For the first time, father and daughter did not stand united.

They looked like two drowning people deciding who to push under first.

Charles spoke first.

“She planned it.”

Victoria turned slowly.

“What?”

He adjusted his cufflinks.

“My daughter developed an obsession with Ethan Pierce. I tried to contain the situation.”

Victoria’s face transformed.

“You coward.”

He did not blink.

“You were always emotional.”

She stepped toward him.

“I did everything for the family.”

“No,” Charles said. “You did everything because you could not stand losing to a waitress.”

The cameras caught it all.

Victoria realized too late that the world was finally seeing her without editing.

Her voice dropped.

“If I go down, Daddy, I won’t go alone.”

Charles’s expression flickered.

Then a black SUV screeched to the curb.

The back door opened.

A man Ethan did not recognize shouted, “Victoria!”

She ran.

Klein yelled.

Ethan grabbed Lucy and pulled her back as officers rushed forward.

Victoria jumped into the SUV.

But before the door closed, Lucy slipped from Ethan’s arms and threw something through the open window.

Her sketchbook.

It hit Victoria in the chest.

Pages burst loose.

Drawings scattered inside the vehicle.

Victoria looked down at them, stunned.

The faceless bride.

The black car.

The motel door.

The child’s memory of every monster.

Then the SUV sped away.

Ethan caught Lucy and held her tight.

Klein shouted into her radio.

But Lucy was staring at the road.

“She’ll come back,” she whispered.

Ethan looked at the scattered pages blowing across the sidewalk.

“No,” he said.

This time, he meant it.

“We’re going to her.”
