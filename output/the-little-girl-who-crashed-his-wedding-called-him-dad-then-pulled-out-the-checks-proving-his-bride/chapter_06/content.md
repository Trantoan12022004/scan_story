He had been wrong.

Grief was the sound Lucy made when she saw the empty hospital bed.

It was not a scream exactly. It was something smaller and worse, like the world had reached into her chest and pulled too hard.

Ethan caught her before she hit the floor.

“No,” she gasped. “No, no, no, no.”

He held her against him while nurses rushed in and Detective Klein shouted orders into her radio.

Security footage.

Lock down exits.

Check elevators.

Find every black vehicle within six blocks.

But Ethan heard almost none of it.

His eyes stayed on the white rose.

Victoria had wanted him to know.

Not just that she could take Olivia.

That she could still reach into his life whenever she wanted.

Daniel arrived ten minutes later, breathless and furious.

“I was downstairs with the police giving another statement. Someone pulled the fire alarm near the west stairwell. Whole floor went crazy.”

Detective Klein came back into the room.

“Camera near this hallway went dark for four minutes.”

“Convenient,” Ethan said.

“Very.”

“Who accessed the system?”

“We’re checking.”

Ethan picked up the rose with a tissue.

Klein stopped him.

“Careful.”

He looked at her.

“Then move faster.”

Her eyes hardened.

“I know you’re angry.”

“You don’t.”

“No,” she said quietly. “I know what it looks like when rich people think consequences are optional. And I know how often they get sloppy when they think they’ve already won.”

That got through.

Ethan forced himself to breathe.

“What do we do?”

“First, you keep Lucy alive.”

Lucy had stopped crying.

She stood in the corner with her sketchbook clutched flat against her chest. Her face was blank again.

Ethan hated that blankness.

Klein crouched in front of her.

“Lucy, did your mother ever mention another safe place? Somewhere she might be taken? Somewhere Victoria knew about?”

Lucy shook her head.

Then stopped.

“What?” Ethan asked.

Lucy opened the sketchbook, flipping pages fast. Near the back, she found a drawing of a small white house beside a lake.

“Mom used to dream about this place.”

Ethan’s pulse changed.

He knew that house.

“My father’s lake cabin.”

Daniel frowned.

“The one near Briar Lake?”

Ethan nodded.

“I took Olivia there once. Before everything.”

Lucy looked up.

“Mom said it was the last place she felt safe.”

Detective Klein stood.

“Would Victoria know about it?”

Ethan’s jaw tightened.

“She knew everything I ever told her.”

They left within minutes.

Klein could not officially authorize Ethan to join a police operation, so Ethan did what men like him had always done.

He called lawyers.

He called the board.

He called every private security contact he trusted more than the Hale family.

Then he drove behind the police convoy anyway.

Briar Lake was forty miles north, hidden behind pine roads and old money gates. The cabin had belonged to Ethan’s father, then to Ethan, though he had not visited in years.

The closer they got, the more memories returned.

Olivia barefoot on the dock.

Olivia laughing under his jacket.

Olivia whispering that she was late, and him spinning her around because he already knew he wanted every possible future with her.

Then the future had been stolen.

Lucy sat in the back seat beside a female officer, silent.

Ethan watched her in the mirror.

“Lucy?”

She looked up.

“When we find her, I need you to stay back until Detective Klein says it’s safe.”

“No.”

“Lucy.”

“No,” she repeated, louder. “Every time I stay back, someone takes her.”

The officer touched her shoulder gently.

“Your dad is trying to protect you.”

Lucy looked at Ethan.

“He’s new at it.”

The words hurt because they were true.

Ethan nodded.

“I am. But I’m trying.”

She looked away, but he saw her wipe her eyes.

They reached the private road at 10:42 p.m.

The gate was open.

Ethan’s blood went cold.

“That gate requires a code.”

Detective Klein drew her weapon.

“No one moves until we clear the property.”

They parked a quarter mile down and approached on foot through the trees.

Rain had softened to mist. The lake appeared between black branches, silver under the moon.

The cabin lights were on.

Klein signaled two officers around the back.

Ethan stayed behind a tree with Lucy, every muscle screaming to run forward.

Then they heard a crash inside.

A woman shouted.

Not Olivia.

Victoria.

“You should have stayed grateful!”

Ethan moved before anyone could stop him.

Klein hissed his name, but he was already across the wet yard and onto the porch.

Through the window, he saw Olivia on the floor near the fireplace, wrists tied, face pale but eyes open.

Victoria stood above her in a dark coat, holding a pistol with both hands.

Charles Hale stood near the door, arguing in a low voice.

“This is over,” Charles said. “Leave her. We need to go.”

Victoria laughed wildly.

“Over? She walked into my wedding from a grave.”

“She is evidence now.”

“She is nothing.”

Olivia lifted her head.

“My daughter found him.”

Victoria’s face twisted.

“Yes. And now your daughter can lose him too.”

Ethan kicked the door open.

Everything happened at once.

Victoria turned.

Charles shouted.

Klein yelled, “Police!”

The gun swung toward Ethan.

He saw Victoria’s finger tighten.

Then Olivia, bound and weak, threw herself sideways into Victoria’s legs.

The gun fired.

The shot shattered the window behind Ethan.

He lunged.

Victoria screamed as he tackled her against the wall. The gun slid across the floor. Officers flooded the room.

Charles tried to run through the back door and slammed directly into Daniel, who had circled around with police.

Lucy rushed toward Olivia.

“Mom!”

An officer caught her, but Ethan shouted, “Let her go!”

Lucy dropped beside Olivia, sobbing into her shoulder.

Olivia’s bound hands trembled as she tried to touch her daughter’s hair.

“I’m here,” she whispered. “I’m here.”

Victoria was on the floor beneath Ethan, laughing and crying at the same time.

“You think this makes you noble?” she spat. “You think she came back because she loves you?”

Ethan looked at her with disgust.

“She came back because you failed to kill her.”

Victoria smiled.

“Ask her about the second test.”

Ethan froze.

Olivia’s face changed.

Victoria saw it and laughed harder.

“Oh, you didn’t tell him? Sweet Olivia. Always saving the worst truth for last.”

Detective Klein hauled Victoria to her feet.

“Victoria Hale, you are under arrest for kidnapping, attempted murder, witness intimidation—”

Victoria leaned toward Ethan as officers dragged her away.

“Ask her why she really ran the first time.”

Then she was gone into the flashing red and blue lights.

Ethan turned slowly toward Olivia.

Lucy held her mother tightly.

Olivia looked at him with tears in her eyes.

And Ethan knew.

Victoria had not been lying about everything.
