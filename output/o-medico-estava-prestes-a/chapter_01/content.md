**The doctor was about to amputate his leg when his daughter screamed, “Stop!”**

Michael Carter stared at his six-year-old daughter, Emma.

The surgeon froze.

Emma pointed to the bandage covering her father's crushed leg.

“That piece of metal shouldn’t be there.”

The doctor carefully removed it.

His expression changed.

“That’s not part of the orthosis.”

Michael's blood ran cold.

“So what is this?”

The surgeon shook his head.

“I don't know.”

Three weeks earlier, Michael had survived a serious car accident. His left leg had been crushed, but the first surgery had gone well.

Then everything changed.

The swelling returned. The pain became unbearable. He started to lose sensation in his foot.

Doctors believed the tissue was dying.

They had scheduled an amputation.

But now, hidden under the bandage, they found a small metal device that shouldn't have been there.

Michael stared at him.

Someone had placed him inside his brace.

Someone was trying to make him lose his leg.

And suddenly he began to wonder if the accident had really been an accident.

Michael was a wealthy businessman and had many enemies. But he always believed that his home was the only place he was safe.

This certainty lasted until that night.

He ordered his security team to completely lock down the house.

Ethan, his head of security, reviewed the camera recordings.

Two hours later, he entered Michael's office.

“There’s something strange.”

He showed the recordings to Michael.

Every night, at exactly **2:17 in the morning**, the camera outside Michael's room would shut off for eight minutes.

“Who has access to the system?” Michael asked.

“Me, Ryan and your brother Alex.”

Michael stared at the screen.

“So one of them is helping whoever did this.”

That night, Michael stayed awake.

At 2:17 a.m., the hallway camera went black.

A few seconds later, the door to his room slowly opened.

Someone entered.

Michael reached toward the gun next to the bed.

But before he could react, the figure disappeared.

When Ethan arrived, there was no one there.

Only an envelope had been left on Michael's pillow.

Inside was a photograph of Michael next to his car the night of the accident.

He didn't remember that photo being taken.

On the back were written five words:

**YOU SURVIVED THE WRONG NIGHT.**

The next morning, Michael confronted Ryan.

“You were in my room three nights ago.”

Ryan went pale.

“It wasn’t me.”

Michael showed the security camera image.

Ryan stared at her.

“That’s not me.”

Suddenly, the fire alarm started ringing.

Smoke filled the downstairs hallway.

“Where’s Emma?”

The nanny was found unconscious.

Emma had disappeared.

Michael forced himself to stand despite the excruciating pain in his leg.

They searched the house until they heard a faint sound coming from the basement.

Touch.

Touch.

Touch.

It was Emma's wooden toy.

Ethan broke down the door of a locked storage room.

Emma was inside, scared but unharmed.

Michael pulled her into his arms.

“Who brought you here?”

“I don't know.”

“What was he like?”

“He was wearing daddy’s black coat.”

Michael froze.

He only had a black coat.

It was hanging in his room.

Then Emma whispered:

“He smelled like Alex.”

Alex was Michael's younger brother.

But Alex was gone.

His cell phone was still in the kitchen.

His car was still outside.

Then Ethan discovered a room hidden behind a basement wall.

Inside were photographs of Michael.

Your schedule.

Your medical records.

Your family.

Even photos from your childhood.

But one photograph made Michael freeze.

It showed **two identical boys**.

The same face.

The same height.

The same scar above the eyebrow.

Michael stared at the photo.

“My parents said I was an only child.”

Ethan turned the photograph over.

On the back were written the words:

**THE ORIGINAL DIED THAT NIGHT.**

Suddenly, the lights in the basement went out.

A voice came out of the darkness.

“Finally you found it.”

Alex stepped out of the shadows.

Next to him was another man.

Michael's face.

Michael's height.

Michael's scar.

Michael couldn't breathe.

“You should never have survived the accident,” Alex said.

Michael pointed to the stranger.

“Who is he?”

Alex smiled.

“Your replacement.”

The stranger took a step forward.

Then Emma held Michael's hand.

“Daddy…”

She faced the man.

“He’s not the one who looks like you.”

Michael frowned.

“What?”

“He looks like the man in your old photos.”

Michael looked again.

She was right.

The stranger was not his replacement.

He was the man in the childhood photographs.

The **real Michael Carter**.

Suddenly, Michael's entire life turned into a lie.

Your name.

Your family.

Your fortune.

Everything.

Alex looked at him and whispered:

“You were never Michael Carter.”

And for the first time, Michael realized that the person whose identity he had stolen for twenty-two years…

was right in front of him.
