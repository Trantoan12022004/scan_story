6

We did not run toward freedom.

That would have been the obvious move.

Marcus expected obvious moves.

Instead, Daniel called federal investigators while Adrian and I headed through the service corridor toward the lake.

The cellar countdown continued remotely on my phone.

Twenty-seven minutes.

Marcus had to access the physical server before zero.

He could not cancel from wherever he held Celeste and Vanessa.

That meant he had two choices.

Return himself.

Or send someone.

The boathouse lights were on.

A motorboat rocked against the pier.

One door stood open.

Adrian entered first.

I followed despite every rational instinct telling me not to.

The room was empty.

One overturned chair.

Plastic restraints.

Celeste’s handbag.

A drop of blood on the floor.

Not enough to indicate serious injury.

Vanessa’s phone lay smashed beside the wall.

Outside, an engine started.

Adrian ran toward the rear doors.

A black SUV disappeared along a maintenance road.

“We missed them.”

“No.”

I looked at the countdown.

“Marcus has to come back.”

Daniel reached us.

“Federal agents are fifteen minutes away.”

“Too slow.”

“Eleanor, stop saying that like we’re supposed to chase these people ourselves.”

He was right.

So we returned toward the house.

Halfway there, Adrian stopped.

“What?”

“My mother’s folder.”

“What about it?”

“She kept looking down left.”

“At something in the room.”

“No. She was telling us something.”

Adrian turned.

“Left dock.”

Bellwether had two docks.

The boathouse sat on the right side of the lake.

A smaller maintenance dock lay west, hidden behind trees.

We reached it.

An old rowboat floated empty.

Under the dock, someone coughed.

Adrian dropped to his knees.

Vanessa.

She clung to a support beam beneath the decking.

Wet.

Shaking.

She had jumped from the SUV near the lake road, run through the trees, and hidden.

“Celeste?” Adrian asked.

“They took her.”

“Marcus?”

“And two men.”

“Where?”

Vanessa pointed toward the northern service road.

“There’s an old hunting cabin.”

Adrian knew it.

Marcus used it as a teenager.

My phone showed nineteen minutes.

I asked Vanessa why Marcus needed Celeste.

“To sign something.”

“What?”

“I don’t know.”

“Think.”

She closed her eyes.

“He kept saying Theodore’s shares.”

Adrian stiffened.

Theodore still owned a hidden class of founder equity.

Upon verified death, those shares transferred to his spouse.

Celeste.

Not economically huge.

But attached to an old governance privilege.

Emergency custodian substitution.

If Celeste signed as Theodore’s surviving spouse, Marcus could replace himself with another Black Harbor custodian remotely.

Then he would not need to return to Bellwether.

“He’s trying to cancel the archive through a new custodian,” Daniel said.

Eighteen minutes.

We informed federal teams.

Agents split.

Some toward the cabin.

Others toward Bellwether.

Then the server sent another request.

CUSTODIAN SUBSTITUTION INITIATED.

Spouse authorization pending.

Celeste had not signed yet.

Good.

Adrian stared toward the trees.

“I’m going.”

“No.”

“She’s my mother.”

“She killed my father.”

“I know.”

He said it with enough pain that I stopped.

Celeste was both.

My father’s killer.

His mother.

People remain human even when what they have done becomes unforgivable.

That complexity does not erase accountability.

But it explains why Adrian could not simply abandon her.

I went too.

Daniel swore and came with us.

The hunting cabin appeared between the trees.

One vehicle outside.

No guards visible.

Federal teams were eight minutes away.

My phone:

11:42.

Inside, voices.

Marcus.

“Sign it.”

Celeste.

“No.”

“You already killed for us.”

“For Adrian.”

Marcus laughed.

“You keep saying that as if it makes you noble.”

Vanessa touched my arm.

“There’s a back entrance.”

She had been there earlier that week.

Marcus used the cabin to meet her.

She showed us a narrow path.

At the rear window we saw them.

Marcus.

Celeste.

Two men.

One was unfamiliar.

The other made Daniel stop breathing.

“Miriam.”

I looked again.

My aunt Miriam Ashcroft-Reyes stood beside Marcus.

Board chair.

The woman who entered the banquet and removed Adrian.

My father’s sister.

My closest surviving relative.

She held a folder.

No weapon.

No restraints.

She was there willingly.

Everything inside me went cold.

Daniel whispered:

“No.”

Miriam spoke.

“You’re wasting time, Marcus.”

He snapped:

“Then convince her.”

Miriam looked at Celeste.

“Sign.”

Celeste stared.

“You were Charles’s sister.”

“And he was sentimental.”

My aunt.

Part of Black Harbor.

Not merely feeding information.

Running something.

Marcus was afraid of her.

I could see it now.

“What does she want?” I whispered.

Daniel looked devastated.

“Control.”

The puzzle shifted.

My father believed someone inside Ashcroft Holdings leaked information.

Miriam.

She had access to every family structure.

She knew Vault Seven existed, though perhaps not its contents.

She signed Adrian’s removal because she wanted the dead-man switch activated.

Marcus thought he manipulated the banquet.

Perhaps Miriam manipulated him.

My phone:

08:51.

Miriam checked her watch.

“The archive releases in nine minutes.”

Marcus glared.

“How do you know?”

“Because I designed the external recipient protocol with Charles.”

Daniel closed his eyes.

Of course.

She knew the system better than anyone.

Celeste looked at her.

“You can stop it?”

“With the custodian substitution.”

“And then?”

Miriam smiled.

“Then Black Harbor becomes Ashcroft property permanently.”

Marcus stared.

“That wasn’t our agreement.”

“You’re a custodian, Marcus.”

His face changed.

Miriam continued.

“You mistook proximity to money for ownership.”

The same mistake Adrian made about Vale Meridian.

History repeating.

Marcus stepped toward Miriam.

“You need me.”

“No.”

She nodded toward the second man.

He opened his coat enough to reveal an access token.

A Black Harbor secondary credential.

Marcus understood.

Miriam had prepared a replacement.

She only needed Celeste’s signature.

Celeste tore the document in half.

“No.”

Marcus struck the table.

Miriam remained calm.

“Then Eleanor releases everything.”

“Good,” Celeste said.

For the first time, I saw something like courage in her.

“Let it burn.”

Marcus grabbed her.

Adrian moved before I could stop him.

He entered through the rear door.

“Let her go.”

Everyone froze.

Marcus stared.

Then smiled.

“Big brother.”

The two men moved.

Daniel shouted that federal agents were coming.

Miriam looked toward me through the open doorway.

Her eyes showed no surprise.

“Eleanor.”

I stepped inside.

“Aunt Miriam.”

She sighed.

“You were always going to find out.”

My phone showed:

04:12.

Marcus looked toward it.

“You stupid—”

Miriam interrupted.

“Too late.”

“Cancel it!”

“I can’t without Celeste.”

Marcus turned toward his mother.

“Sign!”

“No.”

03:31.

Marcus reached for the document pieces.

Adrian blocked him.

They struggled.

One of the men moved toward us.

Then sirens rose beyond the trees.

Federal vehicles.

02:44.

Miriam did something unexpected.

She sat down.

Marcus stared.

“What are you doing?”

“Accepting reality.”

“You said—”

“I said I wanted control.”

She looked at me.

“But Charles built the better trap.”

01:58.

Agents surrounded the cabin.

Commands shouted.

Hands raised.

Marcus backed toward the wall.

Celeste stepped away.

Miriam placed both hands on the table.

Vanessa cried.

Adrian looked at me.

00:42.

00:41.

Marcus screamed:

“Do you know what’s in that archive?”

I answered:

“No.”

“Neither did Charles!”

00:25.

Federal agents entered.

Marcus was forced to the floor.

Miriam did not resist.

00:11.

00:10.

I looked at my aunt.

She smiled sadly.

“Your father always did love dramatic timing.”

00:03.

00:02.

00:01.

ARCHIVE RELEASE COMPLETE.

Black Harbor’s hidden history left Bellwether House simultaneously for federal financial-crime units, independent counsel, two international regulators, and three newspapers selected by my father seven years earlier.

Marcus stopped struggling.

Then my phone displayed a final automated message from Charles Ashcroft.

Eleanor,

If you released the archive, then Miriam was involved.

Do not trust her confession.

She will tell you she wanted the money.

She didn’t.

Ask her what happened to your mother.

My mother had died when I was sixteen.

A car accident.

I slowly turned toward Miriam.

And for the first time since federal agents entered, my aunt looked afraid.
