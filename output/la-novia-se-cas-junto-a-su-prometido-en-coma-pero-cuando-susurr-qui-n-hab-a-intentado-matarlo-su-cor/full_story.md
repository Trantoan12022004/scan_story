## THE BRIDE GOT MARRIED WITH HER FIANCE IN A COMA... BUT WHEN HE WHISPERED WHO HAD TRIED TO KILL HIM, HER HEART REACTED

Nobody expected Lucía Valera to appear dressed as a bride.

Much less walk to the altar.

And absolutely no one imagined that the boyfriend would be there waiting for her in a medical bed.

Mateo Montenegro remained unconscious under the golden lights of the room.

Black suit.

White shirt.

Perfectly placed bow tie.

A small white pin on the chest.

But also an oxygen mask on his face, two IV bags hanging next to him and a heart monitor marking a slow and constant rhythm.

Sixty-nine beats.

Lucia stopped next to him.

More than forty guests remained silent.

Just a few hours earlier, Mateo had suffered a car accident on a mountain road outside Madrid.

The doctors had managed to stabilize him.

But no one could guarantee that he would wake up.

His family wanted to cancel the ceremony.

Lucia refused.

Father Esteban watched her with unshed tears.

—Lucia, do you choose it even now?

She looked at Mateo's still face.

He remembered six years together.

The discussions.

The trips.

The promises.

And a phrase that he repeated every time they talked about the wedding.

"It doesn't matter what happens. That day I want you to be by my side."

Lucía squeezed the bouquet.

—Yes. Even if you don't wake up, I choose you.

A tear fell down her cheek.

Someone among the guests began to cry.

Then another.

And when Esteban slowly lowered the book he was holding, the room erupted in applause.

Lucia smiled for the first time all day.

But that smile hid something.

An hour earlier he had received an envelope with no return address.

Inside was a photograph.

The brake duct of Mateo's car.

Cut.

Only one sentence appeared below:

“This was not an accident.”

Lucia had not shown it to anyone.

Not even the police.

Because the envelope also contained a warning.

“Don't trust the family.”

While the guests continued applauding, Lucía left the bouquet next to the bed.

He put a hand on Mateo's chest.

He bowed.

And he whispered:

—I know someone tampered with your brakes.

For half a second nothing happened.

Then:

BEEP.

BEEP.

BEEP.

The pace increased.

Seventy-eight.

Eighty-six.

Ninety-four.

Lucia raised her head.

—Matthew…

The monitor continued to speed up.

Among the guests, Álvaro Montenegro stopped applauding.

Mateo's uncle turned pale.

And Lucia saw it.

The doctors entered a few minutes later.

They checked Mateo.

His condition remained stable.

He hadn't woken up.

—Was it a coincidence? —Lucia asked.

The doctor denied.

—I can't say for sure.

—His heart changed just when I told him about the accident.

—Some apparently unconscious patients may respond to voices or stimuli.

—Then he heard me.

—I said it's possible.

Lucía looked back at the guests.

Álvaro was no longer there.

—Where is Mateo's uncle?

Nobody knew.

That was enough to arouse all his suspicions.

Álvaro had worked alongside Mateo's father for more than twenty years.

After his brother's death, he hoped to become permanent president of Montenegro Holdings.

But the will had established something else.

When Mateo turned thirty and married, he would receive the majority of the family's voting rights.

The wedding that day didn't just mean a marriage.

It meant power.

The next day, Álvaro would lose real control of the company.

Lucia felt cold.

He had a reason.

And he had reacted to the monitor.

But something didn't fit.

If Álvaro had wanted to kill Mateo, why stay at the wedding?

That night, when almost all the guests had left, Lucía found something under Mateo's pillow.

A small headset.

He did not belong to the medical team.

He took it.

At that moment Mateo's sister, Sofía Montenegro, entered.

—What is that?

—I don't know.

Sofia recognized it.

—It's yours.

—From Mateo?

—I used it to listen to messages while driving.

Lucia felt her heart race.

He remembered what a nurse had told him:

Mateo's phone had been recovered from the wrecked vehicle.

Lucia asked to see him.

The screen was broken.

But it still worked.

They found several messages.

The last one had been recorded the morning of the accident.

It was never shipped.

Lucia pressed play.

First Mateo's breathing was heard.

Then his voice.

—Lucía, if I don't arrive on time...

Car noise.

—I have discovered something about Álvaro.

Lucía and Sofía looked at each other.

The recording continued.

—I don't trust him. But I don't think he's the one behind everything either. I found transfers from a…

The audio cut out.

Damaged file.

—An account from whom? —Sofia asked.

Lucia hit the table in frustration.

—There has to be a copy.

Sofia denied.

—Maybe in the cloud.

But the account was blocked.

They needed a password.

Then Lucía remembered a conversation from months ago.

Mateo had told him, laughing:

“If one day I forget everything, try the place where we met.”

Lucia wrote:

RETiro2018.

Access granted.

There was another copy of the message.

Complete.

—I found transfers from a Valera Foundation account.

Lucia stopped breathing.

Valera.

His own family.

The Valera Foundation had been created by Lucía's father, Antonio Valera.

It financed hospitals, scholarships and social projects.

It was one of the most respected organizations in Madrid.

"That doesn't prove anything," said Lucia.

Sofia looked at her cautiously.

—Mateo wouldn't have mentioned the foundation for no reason.

The message continued.

—The payments arrived at a workshop that inspected my car. Lucia, I don't know who is using the foundation, but someone close to you is involved.

Lucía turned off the audio.

—My father would never do something like that.

A voice sounded behind.

—Not your father.

Álvaro was at the door.

Lucia immediately stood up.

—Where was he?

—Looking for this.

He placed a folder on the table.

Inside were bank statements.

Payments.

Dates.

Forged invoices.

—I was also investigating.

—Why did you run away from the wedding?

Álvaro frowned.

—I didn't run away.

—He disappeared when Mateo's heart changed.

—Because I understood that maybe he could hear us.

Lucia did not understand.

Álvaro lowered his voice.

—And because if Mateo knows who touched his car, that person also knows that he can wake up.

Sofia turned pale.

"Are you saying they could try again?"

—Yes.

Lucia looked towards the door of the room.

—Then we need security.

Álvaro denied.

—Not just anyone.

—Why?

—Because someone from security had access to the garage the morning of the accident.

They reviewed the internal images of the mansion.

At five seventeen in the morning, Mateo left the house.

At four forty-nine, a figure entered the garage.

Cap.

Dark jacket.

Partially covered face.

The person remained next to the car for six minutes.

Then he came out.

“Expand it,” said Lucia.

The image was too dark.

But there was something visible.

A ring.

Big.

With a black stone.

Lucia stopped breathing.

I knew that ring.

I had seen it hundreds of times.

—No…

Sofia looked at her.

—What's going on?

Lucia did not answer.

He left the room.

He crossed the hallway.

He entered one of the private rooms.

There was his mother.

Victoria Valera.

Still dressed in the elegant champagne-colored suit she had worn during the ceremony.

And in his right hand...

the black ring

—Mom.

Victoria looked up.

—What's happening?

Lucia looked at the ring.

—Where were you at five in the morning?

Victoria's face changed just for an instant.

—Sleeping.

—Lie.

—Lucia…

—You were in the garage.

Victoria remained silent.

—Did you touch Mateo's car?

—No.

—I have images.

The mother stiffened.

—You don't understand what you saw.

—Then explain it to me.

Victoria closed her eyes.

—I went into the garage.

Lucía felt the ground disappear under her feet.

—Why?

—Because he wanted to stop Mateo from driving.

—Did you cut the brakes?

—No!

—So what did you do?

Victoria started to cry.

—I took the gift away.

—What gift?

—The one I was going to look for.

Lucia remained motionless.

Mateo had said he was going out to pick up a hidden gift.

—Why?

—Because there were documents inside.

—What documents?

Victoria sat down.

—Evidence against your father.

Antonio Valera was not simply a respected businessman.

For years he had used the family foundation to hide payments related to construction contracts.

Montenegro Holdings had discovered irregularities.

Mateo too.

—Did Dad know that Mateo had discovered that?

Victoria nodded.

—They argued yesterday.

—And why were you looking for the documents?

—To destroy them before he ruined your father.

Lucia stepped back.

—Then you did have a reason.

—But I didn't try to kill him.

—Who did it?

Victoria raised her eyes.

—When I entered the garage there was someone else.

Lucia felt a chill.

—Who?

—I didn't see his face.

—Mom.

—I only saw him leave through the side door.

—Man or woman?

—Man.

Lucía immediately thought of Álvaro.

—Was it Álvaro?

—I don't know.

Then a male voice responded from the hallway.

—It wasn't him.

Antonio Valera entered.

Lucia looked at him.

—Dad.

His father closed the door.

—Your mother didn't cut the brakes.

—How do you know?

Antonio remained silent.

That was enough.

—Because you know who did it.

Victoria stood up.

—Antonio, no.

"You've already gone too far."

Lucia took a step towards him.

—Was it you?

—No.

—Who?

Antonio looked into her eyes.

—Your brother.

Lucia stopped breathing.

—I don't have a brother.

Antonio closed his eyes.

—Yes.

Twenty-seven years before Lucía was born, Antonio had had another son.

Gabriel.

Its existence was never made public.

He grew up far from family.

Years later Antonio discreetly incorporated him into the Valera Foundation as financial manager.

Lucia had seen Gabriel many times.

But he always believed he was simply an employee.

—Gabriel is my brother?

—Yes.

—And he wanted to kill Mateo?

Antonio shook his head slowly.

—I think he wanted to stop him.

—Cutting the brakes is not stopping someone.

—Gabriel discovered that Mateo was going to denounce the foundation's accounts.

Lucia clenched her fists.

—Where is it?

—I don't know.

Lucía's phone vibrated.

Unknown message.

A photograph.

Mateo in his bed.

Taken less than a minute ago.

Lucia looked up at the hallway.

Everyone was with her.

Who was next to Mateo?

He ran.

Álvaro and Sofía did the same.

When they entered, the room was empty.

Mateo was still unconscious.

But one of the IV bags had a small new hole.

—Call the doctor!

The staff ran in.

They immediately removed the serum.

A doctor examined the patient.

—Not enough amount has entered to affect you.

Lucia looked around.

On a chair there was an envelope.

Inside he found a note.

“Let him die or your whole family will fall with him.”

The police were notified.

The doors of the mansion were blocked.

Nobody could leave.

Then Mateo's monitor changed again.

BEEP.

BEEP.

Lucia ran up to him.

—Matthew.

His right hand moved.

Very little.

But this time there was no doubt.

It was a clear movement.

—Mateo, if you hear me, squeeze my hand.

Nothing.

Lucia put her mouth close to his ear.

—We know that Gabriel tampered with your car.

The monitor did not change.

Lucia frowned.

—Wasn't it Gabriel?

Suddenly…

Mateo's fingers squeezed his.

Once.

Lucia held her breath.

—Once means no.

Mateo squeezed.

Once.

Sofia quickly called the doctor.

Lucia continued.

—Alvaro?

A pressure.

No.

—My father?

A pressure.

No.

—My mother?

A pressure.

No.

Everyone looked at each other.

If none of them were...

who?

Mateo began to move his lips under the mask.

Lucia bowed.

Barely any sound came out.

—This…this…

—Esteban?

Sofia's eyes widened.

Father Stephen.

The priest who had officiated the wedding.

The man who had known both families for decades.

The man who had been inside the mansion since before dawn.

Álvaro ran towards the living room.

Father Esteban had disappeared.

Police found him an hour later trying to leave the property through a service entrance.

There were tools in his car.

A receipt from the workshop.

And a copy of the documents Mateo had gathered.

But Esteban did not try to deny anything.

—I hit the brakes.

Lucia looked at him horrified.

—Why?

The priest lowered his head.

—Because Mateo discovered something worse than your father's crimes.

Antonio Valera turned pale.

-Be quiet.

Esteban smiled sadly.

—Not anymore.

Lucia looked at her father.

—What else is there?

Stephen responded:

—Mateo discovered who Gabriel really is.

—My brother.

—No.

Silence.

—Gabriel is not Antonio's son.

Victoria covered her mouth.

Antonio closed his eyes.

—Then from whom?

Esteban looked towards Álvaro Montenegro.

Everyone followed his gaze.

Álvaro turned pale.

—No.

Stephen continued:

—Gabriel is your father's son.

The former president of Montenegro Holdings.

That meant that Gabriel was Mateo's half-brother.

And if I could prove it...

had rights to a huge part of Montenegro's heritage.

Suddenly everything changed.

It wasn't just about corruption.

Not even a wedding.

Not even from the company.

It was a war for two families.

Mateo had discovered that Gabriel had lived for decades under a false identity while someone siphoned money to keep him hidden.

And Esteban had tried to protect the secret.

—Why you? —Lucia asked.

The priest looked down.

—Because I falsified the birth documents thirty years ago.

The room fell silent.

—If Mateo talked, everyone would find out what I did.

—Is that why you tried to kill him?

Esteban did not respond.

It wasn't necessary.

Hours later, Lucía returned to Mateo.

Her dress was still white.

But the veil was wrinkled.

The makeup gone.

The perfect wedding had turned into a crime scene.

Lucia took his hand.

—All this because you wanted to give me something.

Mateo moved his fingers slightly.

She smiled through her tears.

"Don't put me through this again."

Then, very slowly…

Mateo's eyes began to open.

Lucia stopped breathing.

—Matthew.

He tried to speak.

—No… finished.

—What?

—Esteban…

He breathed heavily.

—He didn't act alone.

Lucia felt cold.

—Who helped him?

Mateo turned his eyes towards the door.

Álvaro was there.

His uncle.

Mateo shook his head weakly.

It wasn't him.

Then he looked at Victoria.

He also denied.

Finally his eyes stopped on Antonio Valera.

Lucia turned to her father.

Antonio didn't move.

Mateo managed to say two words.

—The envelope.

Lucía remembered the anonymous envelope with the photograph of the brakes.

He took it out.

Matthew pointed to the writing.

Antonio turned pale.

Lucia understood it.

—You sent me this.

His father nodded slowly.

—Yes.

—Why?

—Because I discovered too late what Esteban was going to do.

—Did you try to save Mateo?

Antonio began to cry.

—I tried to save you both.

Lucia looked at the envelope.

Then to his father.

—Then why did you tell me not to trust the family?

Antonio raised his eyes.

—Because Esteban was not the one who ordered Mateo's death.

Silence fell.

Mateo squeezed Lucía's hand.

Antonio looked towards the back of the room.

—The person who ordered it is still here.

Lucia followed his gaze.

And among the last guests appeared a woman elegantly dressed in gray.

Sofia Montenegro stopped breathing.

—Mom…

Lucia froze.

Mateo's mother.

The woman everyone thought had been dead for twelve years.

He smiled.

—I see that the wedding finally took place.

And from the expression of terror on Mateo's face, Lucía understood that the real mystery had just begun.
