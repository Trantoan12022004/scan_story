PART 8 — THE PRICE OF A SON

The security footage made Avery physically sick.

A man wearing an FBI jacket had entered through the service corridor.

He had approached Leo while the boy was getting water from the kitchen.

Then he showed him a phone.

On the screen was Liam.

His face.

His voice.

His exact mannerisms.

"Hey, buddy. Your mom sent someone to bring you to me."

Leo had hesitated.

The fake Liam smiled.

"It's okay. Your mom knows."

Four seconds.

That was all it took.

The video was artificial.

A perfect digital imitation.

Leo had taken the man's hand.

And disappeared.

Avery watched the footage twice.

Then turned it off.

"Where is Liam?"

---

He arrived twelve minutes later.

When Avery showed him the video, Liam went completely white.

"He trusted that because it was my face."

Avery's voice was dangerously calm.

"He barely knows you, and they still used you to take him."

Liam lowered his eyes.

"I know."

"I told you I didn't want you anywhere near our lives."

"I know."

"You don't get to say that like it makes things better."

"It doesn't."

He looked at her.

"Tell me what I can do."

Avery threw the ransom note onto the table.

Liam read it.

"Hudson Biotech."

"You know it?"

"My father owned it."

"Past tense?"

"Officially abandoned eight years ago."

Julian opened a map.

"There are four Sterling properties with private underground access."

"Three," Liam corrected.

Julian looked at him.

"The Hudson complex still has a lower research level."

"Records say it was sealed."

"Records lie."

Avery stared at him.

"What is down there?"

"My father built a private emergency facility after 9/11. Independent generators. Underground exits. No connection to Sterling's normal network."

Julian nodded slowly.

"Perfect kidnapping site."

Avery grabbed the transfer papers.

"I'm going."

Liam stepped forward.

"No."

Her eyes flashed.

"Do not tell me what to do."

"I'm not."

"Sounded like it."

Liam stopped.

Then said something unexpected.

"Conrad doesn't only need your signature."

Avery frowned.

"What?"

"Arthur structured the trust with dual authorization."

Julian started searching the documents.

Liam continued.

"Until Leo turns twenty-five, permanent transfer of his voting rights requires approval from his legal guardian..."

Avery.

"...and the current Sterling family trustee."

Liam.

Julian found the clause.

"He's right."

Avery stared at Liam.

"Then Conrad needs both of us."

"Yes."

"So the note telling me to come alone is a lie."

"He expects me to follow."

"Will you?"

Liam looked toward the image of Leo frozen on the security screen.

"Yes."

Avery's expression hardened.

"I'm not giving you another opportunity to play hero."

"I'm not trying to be one."

"Then what are you trying to be?"

Liam took several seconds before answering.

"Useful."

That silenced the room.

He continued.

"I don't deserve Avery back."

His voice broke slightly.

"I don't deserve Leo calling me Dad."

"But if there is one thing I can do tonight that keeps him alive, you cannot ask me to stay behind."

Avery hated that he was right.

---

At 11:48 p.m., Avery entered Hudson Biotech alone.

At least that was what Conrad believed.

Julian tracked her through a transmitter hidden inside her earring.

Federal tactical teams waited several blocks away.

They could not move until Avery confirmed Leo's location.

The underground laboratory smelled like dust and antiseptic.

Emergency lights flickered along the corridor.

At the end stood Conrad Vale.

He wore a dark suit.

No panic.

No anger.

Only the controlled expression of a man who had spent forty years believing other people were pieces on his board.

Behind a glass wall sat Leo.

Alive.

A security guard stood beside him.

"Mom!"

Avery moved forward.

Conrad raised one hand.

"That's far enough."

"Let him go."

"Sign."

Avery held up the folder.

"You murdered Arthur."

Conrad smiled faintly.

"Victoria always was terrible at keeping secrets after losing control."

"You stole billions."

"I created billions."

"You killed your best friend."

"Arthur became sentimental."

Avery stared at him.

"That was your justification?"

"Arthur began believing companies existed for employees, families and communities."

Conrad almost sounded disgusted.

"We built Sterling Global to control markets."

"You mean you built it to control people."

"Same thing, if you're competent."

Avery looked toward Leo.

"You need my signature."

"And Liam's."

"You won't get his."

A slow smile appeared.

"I already knew he would come."

Footsteps echoed behind Avery.

She turned.

Liam entered the corridor.

Conrad smiled.

"Right on schedule."

Avery glared at him.

"I told you—"

"I know."

Liam walked forward.

He placed a signed document on the metal table.

Conrad picked it up.

Then his face changed.

"This isn't the authorization."

"No."

"What did you do?"

Liam looked directly at him.

"I transferred every personal voting share I control into an independent employee trust."

Conrad stared at the page.

Avery understood.

Even if Conrad obtained Leo's block...

Liam had just destroyed the remaining pathway to permanent family control.

Conrad's composure cracked.

"You gave away your inheritance."

Liam looked through the glass at Leo.

"No."

His voice was quiet.

"I finally figured out what my inheritance cost."

Conrad reached beneath his jacket.

Avery saw the gun.

"Liam!"

The shot exploded through the corridor.

Leo screamed.

Liam collapsed.

Blood spread across his white shirt.

Conrad grabbed Avery.

And pressed the gun against her ribs.

"Now," he whispered.

"You're going to sign."
