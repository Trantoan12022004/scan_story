Inside, the atmosphere was thick enough to choke on.

Dominic sat behind his massive executive desk, staring down at an open leather ledger. In front of him lay the contents of Mia Carter’s apron pocket, recovered by Daniel during the rescue: a crumpled grocery list, a broken hair clip, and a small, encrypted flash drive that looked like an ordinary plastic storage key.

I stood near the corner of the room, my tablet open, displaying the estate's internal network logs for the past three weeks.

“Explain this,” Dominic said. His voice wasn't loud. It was terrifyingly calm.

Nathan stood in the center of the Persian rug, his hands clasped neatly behind his back. Beside him stood Claire Bennett, Dominic’s fiancée. Claire looked stunning even at dawn, draped in a silk robe, her blonde hair perfectly coiffed, though her fingers trembled slightly as she clutched a porcelain coffee cup.

“Dom, you’re letting your emotions cloud your judgment,” Nathan said, projecting an aura of aggrieved loyalty. “Mia is a valued employee, yes. But she’s an eccentric woman. She probably locked herself down there during a psychotic break or tried to pull some bizarre stunt for attention. Why are you looking at me like I’m the enemy?”

“Because,” Dominic said slowly, lifting his head, “the security logs show that the basement camera covering the wine cellar was manually disabled for four hours on Tuesday night. The override code used to cut the feed wasn't mine. It wasn't Marcus’s.” Dominic’s eyes shifted slowly to Nathan. “It was yours, Nathan. Your master biometric login.”

Claire set her coffee cup down with a sharp clatter that made the porcelain crack.

“That’s absurd!” Claire interjected, stepping forward with a practiced, indignant pout. “Nathan has been helping me plan our wedding for months! He was with me in the city on Tuesday looking at floral arrangements while you were gambling away your sanity in Atlantic City, Dominic! If some maid had a mental breakdown and locked herself in the cellar, that’s a tragedy, but blaming Nathan is paranoid!”

I stepped out from the corner, sliding my tablet across the mahogany desk toward Dominic.

“She wasn't locked in the cellar by accident, Mr. Vale,” I said, my voice cutting through the tension.

Nathan’s head snapped toward me, his eyes narrowing into cold slits. “Watch your mouth, kid. You don't know what you're talking about.”

“Actually, I do,” I replied evenly. “I cross-referenced the network override logs with the estate’s power consumption records. On Tuesday night at 11:15 PM, someone accessed the smart-lock terminal in the master suite—using Claire’s biometric fingerprint—to program a temporary digital passkey for the new brass padlock. The exact same passkey was used again thirty minutes later from a tablet registered to Nathan’s private quarters.”

The study went utterly dead.

Claire’s face lost every trace of color. Her lips parted, but no sound came out. She looked frantically at Nathan, her panic bubbling instantly to the surface.

“You... you said you wiped those logs!” Claire blurted out before she could catch herself.

Nathan closed his eyes for a single second. When he opened them, the mask of the loyal brother was completely gone. In its place stood a ruthless, calculating operator who had spent years quietly carving out a parallel empire right beneath his boss's nose.

“You talk too much, Claire,” Nathan said coldly.

Dominic didn't yell. He didn't smash his desk or draw his weapon. He simply stood up, towering over the room, his massive frame radiating an aura of lethal, concentrated fury.

“Tell me why,” Dominic whispered. His voice carried the weight of a falling mountain. “Seventeen years, Nathan. I pulled you out of the gutter in Boston. I gave you my trust, my money, my life. Why would you lock an innocent woman in a dark cellar to starve for three days?”

Nathan let out a dry, humorless chuckle. He walked over to the mahogany bar, poured himself two fingers of neat bourbon, and took a slow sip before answering.

“Because Mia found the ledger, Dom,” Nathan said smoothly. “Your loyal little housekeeper was cleaning the master study late last week while you were away. She moved a false panel behind the bookshelf—the one where you keep your offshore tax shelters and the records of the Russian syndicate payoffs. She saw how much money you’ve been hoarding, and she threatened to tell you unless you cleaned up the house.”

“She was trying to protect Harper’s future,” I added quietly. “She wasn't blackmailing you. She was begging you to walk away from the syndicate before it got her daughter killed.”

“And she became a liability,” Nathan sneered. “Just like anyone who gets in the way of what’s coming next.”

“And what is coming next, Nathan?” Dominic asked, taking a slow step around the desk.

Claire stepped back, trembling as she clutched her silk robe tighter around her chest. “We’re taking the syndicate, Dominic. You’ve gone soft. You spend your time playing domestic father to a maid’s kid while the Board of Directors in Chicago wants aggressive expansion. Nathan is the future of the Vale family. Not you.”

Dominic stared at them both for a long, agonizing moment. Then, a slow, terrifying smile touched the corner of his mouth.

“You think,” Dominic whispered, “that because I built an empire, I forgot how to tear one down?”

Before Nathan could raise his glass or reach for the firearm tucked inside his tailored vest, Daniel and Marcus burst back through the study doors, dragging two of Nathan’s loyal enforcers by their collars, their weapons already drawn and trained squarely on Nathan and Claire.

“Tie them up,” Dominic ordered, his eyes never leaving Nathan’s face. “We’re going to have a long talk about loyalty. But first... I need to make a phone call.”
