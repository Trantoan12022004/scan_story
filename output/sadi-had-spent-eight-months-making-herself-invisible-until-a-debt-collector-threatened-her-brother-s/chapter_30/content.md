Three days after the incident at Pier 42, the Castello estate had returned to its pristine, immaculate state of quiet luxury, but the undercurrent of power had shifted perceptibly. The attempted encroachment by the Maroni syndicate had not weakened our position; instead, it had served as a stark demonstration of our absolute supremacy over the city's infrastructure.

In the grand study, the massive mahogany desk was no longer cluttered with emergency manifests. Instead, clean architectural blueprints, municipal zoning proposals, and high-level financial portfolios lay neatly arranged under the afternoon sun streaming through the arched glass windows.

I sat at my desk, reviewing the consolidated quarterly earnings report from our newly expanded shipping and logistics division, while Victor stood by the private bar, pouring two glasses of aged bourbon over hand-carved ice spheres.

He walked over, setting one crystal glass down on the blotter before me before leaning against the edge of the desk, swirling his drink slowly.

"Maroni called this morning," Victor said, his tone casual, almost conversational.

I looked up, raising an eyebrow. "Oh? And what did the great Don have to say for himself after his little expedition into our docks was dismantled?"

"He offered a formal apology, full restitution for the delayed shipments, and a signed treaty ceding control of the entire southern shipping lane to our syndicate," Victor replied, a faint, sardonic smile touching the corners of his mouth. "He realized that trying to fight us for the river was like trying to stop a hurricane with a paper umbrella."

I took a slow sip of the bourbon, letting the smooth, fiery warmth settle across my tongue before looking out over the manicured rose gardens below.

"And did you accept his treaty?" I asked quietly.

Victor chuckled, setting his glass down and reaching across the desk to take my hand. The platinum signet ring gleamed under the warm afternoon light, matching his own heavy gold band.

"I accepted his surrender, Sadi. But treaties imply equals. There are no equals left in this city." He squeezed my hand gently, his stormy gray eyes filled with a deep, unbreakable devotion. "We didn't just survive the climb from the bottom, my love. We became the mountain."
