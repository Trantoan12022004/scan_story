The conference parlor emptied with the quiet, reverent efficiency of a cathedral after morning mass. The heavy mahogany doors clicked shut, leaving behind the faint, lingering scent of expensive cologne, old paper, and high-stakes negotiation. Victor and I remained seated at the head of the long table, the golden fountain pen still resting between us like a scepter left behind on a throne.

I looked down at the signed document, tracing the sharp, deliberate lines of my signature. Beneath it, Victor’s counter-signature slashed across the page with effortless dominance—a testament to the blood, sweat, and unyielding will that had fused our names and our empires into a single, unstoppable force. The North Atlantic shipping corridors were ours. Every port, every customs waiver, every transit route from New York to Rotterdam now operated under the sovereign umbrella of the Costello global logistics network.

"You're quiet," Victor said, breaking the morning stillness. His voice was a low, rich baritone that seemed to vibrate against the crystal chandeliers overhead. He hadn't moved his hand away from mine where our fingers still intertwined on the polished wood, the heavy gold band of his wedding ring pressing comfortably against my platinum signet.

"I was just calculating the ripple effects," I murmured, lifting my gaze to meet his stormy gray eyes. "Van Der Berg's board thinks they've secured stability. They don't realize that stability in our hands is just a polite word for monopoly."

A slow, wolfish smile touched the corners of Victor’s mouth. "Monopolies are messy when they're built by amateurs. When we build them, they look like architecture. Impressive, permanent, and entirely inescapable."

He was right. Every rival who had ever doubted us, every corporate backstabber who had tried to bury us in debt, scandal, or litigation, was either six feet under or working as a minor middle-manager in a subsidiary they thought they owned. The journey had not been kind. I remembered the cold stone of the floor when I was nothing more than a forgotten maid with bleeding hands and a burning thirst for survival. I remembered the nights in grimy back-alley safehouses, dodging bullets and double-crosses, wondering if the dawn would find us breathing.

Now, the sun flooded the magnificent study with brilliant, unblemished light, casting long, golden geometric patterns across the Persian rug. We weren't hiding anymore. We were ruling.

"The board meeting in London is scheduled for Thursday," I noted, slipping my hand out of his to gather the contract folders into a neat, precise stack. "Lord Sterling is still dragging his feet on the Channel Tunnel logistics hub. He thinks because his family has held those ancestral land grants for three centuries, he can negotiate from a position of leverage."

Victor stood up, buttoning the front of his custom-tailored charcoal suit with smooth, mechanical grace. "Lord Sterling's family history is a liability, not an asset. Antiquated titles don't move freight, and they certainly don't stop hostile share acquisitions. If he wants to play feudal lord, we'll buy his estate out from under him and turn his ancestral drawing room into a regional shipping depot."

I couldn't help the dry, amused laugh that escaped my lips as I rose beside him. "You’re merciless, Mr. Costello."

"Only to those who stand in our way," Victor replied, pulling me gently against his chest by the waist, his gaze dropping to meet mine with an intensity that could melt steel. "To you, I am entirely at your mercy."

"A terrifying thought for anyone watching," I whispered, resting my hands against the lapels of his jacket, feeling the steady, powerful rhythm of his heart.

"Let them watch," he murmured, before pressing his lips softly to mine.
