Later that evening, after the Marseille emissaries had been escorted off the estate under armed guard, I retreated to the private underground vault beneath the Castello library. This was where the true history of the syndicate was preserved—not in digital files that could be hacked or wiped, but in massive, leather-bound ledgers containing every debt, every betrayal, and every asset acquired over the past forty years.

I sat at the oak desk beneath a single brass swing-arm lamp, turning the heavy pages of the active financial register. Beside me sat a glass of neat bourbon and my platinum signet ring, which I had temporarily slid off my finger to rest on the wood.

The door clicked open, and Victor walked in, carrying a thick manila folder stamped with red classification seals.

"The internal security audit for the East River docks is complete," Victor said, pulling up a chair beside mine and placing the folder on the desk. "Turns out one of our mid-level dock foremen, a guy named Miller, was taking kickbacks from the Russian Bratva to look the other way on unmanifested cargo containers."

I didn't look up from the ledger immediately. I finished writing a row of calculations with my fountain pen before closing the book with a soft, authoritative thud.

"What did you do with him?" I asked quietly.

"I let you decide," Victor replied, resting his arm on the back of my chair. "We can hand him over to the enforcers for a public example at the docks, or we can handle it quietly."

I picked up my platinum ring, sliding it back onto my finger before turning to meet his gaze. "Public examples breed hesitation in the ranks. Quiet disappearances breed absolute paranoia. Fire his entire shift, freeze his family's offshore accounts, and ensure his name is scrubbed from every payroll registry in the city. Let the Russian Bratva know that anyone who tries to buy our dockworkers is buying a ghost."

Victor smiled, a dark, dangerous look of pure admiration crossing his face. "Cold, calculated, and entirely thorough. My wife, the ultimate CEO."

"We didn't survive the bloodbath six months ago by being merciful, Victor," I reminded him, leaning back against his chest as he pulled me into his arms. "An empire isn't built on kindness. It's built on the terrifying certainty that betrayal carries a 100% mortality rate."

"And that," Victor murmured into my hair, "is why no one will ever touch us."
