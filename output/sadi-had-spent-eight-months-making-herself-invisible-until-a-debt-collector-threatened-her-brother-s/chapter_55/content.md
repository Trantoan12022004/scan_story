An empire at the summit cannot afford stagnation. With terrestrial politics, global finance, and planetary defense fully integrated under their sovereign oversight, Elena and Victor turned their gaze toward the final frontier of human dominance: deep-space infrastructure and orbital resource hegemony.

In the subterranean R&D complex beneath the Chicago tower, lead aerospace engineers presented the blueprints for the Vance Orbital Network—a constellation of heavy-lift satellite platforms designed to monopolize low-Earth orbit and secure near-Earth asteroid mining rights.

Dr. Aris Vance (no relation, but handpicked for his ruthless brilliance) pointed a laser pointer at the holographic projection of the solar system.

"By deploying our automated mining drones to Near-Earth Object 433 Eros, we will secure a virtually infinite supply of platinum-group metals and rare earth elements within the next three years," Aris explained rapidly. "This will effectively render terrestrial mining obsolete and give our corporate syndicate total monopoly over advanced manufacturing materials."

Elena stood at the head of the briefing table, her arms crossed, evaluating every data point with cold precision. "What are the failure probabilities during the atmospheric launch phase?"

"Less than zero point zero one percent, Madam Vance," Aris answered confidently. "Our propulsion systems utilize the proprietary electromagnetic rail technology developed under your direct authorization."

"I don't want low probabilities, Aris. I want zero," Elena stated flatly. "Double the redundancy protocols on all telemetry navigation units. If a single thruster misfires, I want the automated override to correct the trajectory before human operators even register the variance."

"Immediately, Madam Vance."

Victor, sitting beside her, leaned forward and nodded in approval. "Proceed with the launch schedule for the first orbital node. Fund it through our sovereign reserve accounts. We want no public debt exposure."

"Understood, Mr. Vance."

As the engineering team dispersed, leaving Elena and Victor alone in the cavernous briefing room, Victor smiled. "You never stop tightening the screws, do you?"

"Perfection is not a destination, Victor; it is a continuous act of will," Elena replied, walking over to the master control terminal to lock the briefing files. "If we relax our grip for even a single second, entropy takes over. And entropy is the only enemy we haven't completely exterminated."

"Then let us keep the fires burning," Victor said, stepping up behind her and wrapping his arms around her waist. "The earth was not enough for us anyway. Let's take the stars."
