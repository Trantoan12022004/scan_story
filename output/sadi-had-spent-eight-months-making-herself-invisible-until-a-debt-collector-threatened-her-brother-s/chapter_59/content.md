Absolute power, once fully secured and institutionalized, sheds its aggression. It no longer needs to roar, to threaten, or to conquer, because there are no frontiers left unconquered. In this new era, the Vance household settled into a rhythm of serene, contemplative tranquility that mirrored the macro-level harmony of the globe outside.

Martha, their lifelong head of domestic operations, had retired to a magnificent estate on the Mediterranean coast, fully endowed with lifetime security and corporate pensions—a token of appreciation from two people who rarely sentimentalized, but never forgot loyalty. Their new domestic staff operated with clockwork precision, remaining almost entirely invisible, moving through the expansive halls like shadows of pure efficiency.

On a crisp Thursday morning, Elena sat at the heavy mahogany writing desk in her private library, not to review corporate ledgers, but to examine a rare collection of antiquities she had acquired from European auctions. Among them was an ancient Roman coin stamped with the profile of Augustus—the man who transformed the chaotic Roman Republic into an enduring imperial peace.

Victor walked in carrying a silver tray with two porcelain cups of Earl Grey tea. He set the tray down and picked up the heavy bronze coin, turning it over between his fingers.

"Augustus thought he had secured eternity," Victor noted, a faint, sardonic smile playing at the corners of his mouth. "He left behind a crumbling dynasty, civil wars, and a Senate of sycophants."

"Because he relied on mortal heirs and fragile political alliances," Elena countered, looking up from her book with a cool, brilliant gaze. "He tried to govern an empire with human egos. We governed ours with immutable systems and absolute economic gravity."

"A crucial distinction," Victor agreed, setting the coin back down and handing Elena her cup of tea. "The world outside doesn't even know who maintains the code anymore. To them, peace is simply the natural state of the atmosphere now. They breathe it in without ever knowing who filtered the air."

"And that is the ultimate triumph," Elena said, taking a slow sip of the fragrant tea. "True sovereignty is invisible. When people look up at the sky, they shouldn't see a dictator looking down; they should simply feel safe."

Victor pulled up a leather armchair opposite her desk, sitting down and crossing his legs with effortless grace. "So, Madam Sovereign, now that the machinery of state is fully autonomous, what do two retired monarchs do with infinity?"

Elena met his gaze, a spark of familiar, dangerous ambition flickering momentarily in her dark eyes before softening into profound contentment. "We enjoy the kingdom, Victor. Every single inch of it."
