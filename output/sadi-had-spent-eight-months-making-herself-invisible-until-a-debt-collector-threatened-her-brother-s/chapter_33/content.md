The silence that followed was heavy with mutual understanding. Victor didn't offer empty platitudes or cheap comfort; he didn't need to. He knew better than anyone the cost of our ascension. He had watched me transform from a desperate, exhausted maid hiding in the servants' quarters into the undisputed queen of an empire, just as I had watched him tear down the corrupt foundations of a rotting syndicate to build something unbreakable in its place.

"The past is a foreign country, Sadi," Victor said quietly, straightening up and offering me his hand. "They don't speak our language there anymore. Come with me."

Curious, I placed my hand in his, letting him pull me gently to my feet. We walked through the double glass doors of the conservatory and out onto the western terrace, where the evening breeze carried the crisp, refreshing scent of approaching autumn.

Below the terrace, winding stone steps led down into the hidden alcove of the estate known as the Whispering Garden—a private courtyard enclosed by high, ivy-covered stone walls where the family crest was carved deep into the limestone archway. We descended the steps side by side, our footsteps echoing softly in the cooling air.

In the center of the alcove stood an antique wrought-iron bench beneath a sprawling weeping willow tree. Victor stopped beside it, reaching into his pocket to pull out a small, heavy velvet box. He didn't open it immediately; instead, he looked down at me with a rare, vulnerable tenderness that only I was ever privileged to witness.

"Do you remember the night of the winter gala, five years ago?" Victor asked, his voice barely above a murmur against the rustling leaves. "When you slipped out onto the service balcony to escape the suffocating crowd, and I found you crying over a torn hem?"

A genuine, soft laugh escaped my lips. "I wasn't crying over a torn hem, Victor. I was furious because one of the senior stewards had deliberately spilled wine on the only clean dress I owned, hoping to humiliate me out of the household."

"And I walked out, handed you my custom silk handkerchief, and told you that one day, the people who looked down on you would be begging for an audience at your gates," Victor continued, his eyes gleaming with dark amusement. "I didn't know your name then, Sadi. But the moment you looked up at me with those defiant, furious eyes, I knew my life had fundamentally changed."

He opened the velvet box. Inside rested a magnificent antique pendant—a platinum teardrop encrusted with rare black diamonds and a single, flawless drop-cut ruby at its center, catching the pale silver light of the rising evening star.
