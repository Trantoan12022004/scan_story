The black armored Mercedes-Maybach cut through the rain-slicked asphalt of Manhattan like a blade through silk. The wipers slapped a frantic, metronomic rhythm against the windshield as the city blurred past in streaks of neon red and yellow.

Sadi sat in the plush leather backseat, her hands folded tightly in her lap, staring down at her split knuckles. Every breath felt shallow, trapped behind the cage of her ribs. Across from her sat Victor Costello, his dark overcoat draped over the seat, his holster strapped securely beneath his left arm. He hadn't spoken a word since they left the Castello estate fifteen minutes ago, but his presence filled the entire cabin—heavy, dominant, and inescapable.

In the front seat, Carmine, Victor’s senior enforcer and head of security, kept his eyes locked on the rearview mirror while his fingers danced across an encrypted satellite terminal.

"Mick’s cell pinged leaving the Upper East Side heading north toward the Bronx, Boss," Carmine reported smoothly, his voice devoid of emotion. "His two associates are with him in a black Lincoln Navigator. Should we intercept them before they reach the hospital or let them arrive?"

"Let them arrive," Victor replied, his voice calm, steady, and chillingly casual. "I want Mick to greet us at St. Jude's. I want him to look me in the eye while he explains his business model."

"Understood," Carmine murmured, tapping another command into the console. "The hospital board has already been cleared. The entire oncology wing floor has been locked down by our private detail. No city cops, no media, no interruptions."

Sadi swallowed hard, finally raising her head to look at Victor. The dashboard lights cast sharp shadows across his high cheekbones and stern brow.

"Mr. Costello," she whispered, her voice barely louder than the hum of the engine. "You... you didn't have to do all this. Mick is connected to the docks syndicate. If you start a war over a—over a maid who owes money—"

"You aren't just a maid anymore, Sadi," Victor interrupted, turning his dark, penetrating gaze toward her.

"I'm staff," she insisted, her voice trembling slightly. "I scrub your floors. I empty your trash. I’m nobody."

"Nobody who scrubs blood off my floors with bleach so strong it destroys their own skin is 'nobody' in my house," Victor said, his tone brooking no argument. "And nobody threatens a child inside my city without paying a price that scales with the offense. Mick O’Shea thought he was operating in the shadows. He forgot whose light keeps those streets illuminated."

Sadi looked away, staring out the window at the towering skyscrapers of Manhattan. For eight months, she had convinced herself that survival meant staying invisible—that if she kept her head down, worked double shifts, and never made a sound, the violence of the city would pass her by. But tonight, standing outside Victor’s office door with her whole world collapsing, invisibility hadn't saved her.

It was the most feared man in New York who had reached down to pull her up.

The Maybach slowed smoothly, turning sharply off the FDR Drive and pulling directly into the ambulance bay of St. Jude’s Children’s Hospital. The sliding glass doors hissed open before the car even came to a complete stop.

Waiting under the glaring fluorescent lights of the entrance was a terrifying welcoming committee: four men in tailored black suits, flanked by two local hospital administrators whose faces were the color of wet plaster.

And right in the center of them, looking like a trapped animal facing execution, was Mick O’Shea.
