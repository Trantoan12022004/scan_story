The Zurich ping was not a threat; it was an invitation.

Within two hours, our private intelligence cell had traced the digital footprint back to an encrypted satellite relay routed through a luxury penthouse in Manhattan’s Upper East Side. The name associated with the signal was Julian Thorne—a former senior partner at the international investment firm where my father had worked before the Castello syndicate systematically dismantled his assets and erased his name from financial history.

I sat in the back of the black Maybach as it glided silently through the rain-slicked streets of Manhattan, the city flashing by in a blur of neon and steel. Victor sat beside me, his hand resting casually over mine, his thumb stroking the platinum signet ring on my finger. He didn't need to speak; his silent, overwhelming presence was a fortress standing behind me.

"You don't have to face him alone, Sadi," Victor said softly as the car pulled up to the curb outside a high-rise residential tower guarded by private security. "If this is a trap, a single word from you, and my enforcers will tear this building down to the foundation."

"It's not a trap," I replied, my voice steady, cold, and absolute. "It’s a reckoning. For ten years, I thought my family’s destruction was just collateral damage in your uncle’s war for territorial dominance. I need to know why Julian Thorne’s name is suddenly showing up on our secure servers."

The penthouse suite was bathed in dim, amber light when the private elevator doors opened directly into the foyer. Julian Thorne stood by the floor-to-ceiling glass windows overlooking Central Park, an older man with silver-streaked hair, wearing a bespoke navy suit that screamed old money and calculated survival.

As I stepped out of the elevator, followed closely by Victor and two armed guards, Thorne turned slowly. His eyes widened slightly as he looked at me, taking in the tailored designer suit, the cold diamond jewelry, and the undeniable aura of supreme authority that radiated from my posture.

"Sadi," Thorne murmured, his voice trembling slightly. "Good God... you look so much like your mother. But the eyes... those belong to your father. Unyielding and sharp."

"Cut the sentimentality, Julian," I said, walking toward him and stopping ten feet away, my hands clasped loosely in front of me. "Men in your tax bracket don't send encrypted Zurich pings down memory lane unless they want something or they're running out of time. Which is it?"

Thorne let out a dry, humorless chuckle, walking over to a crystal decanter and pouring himself a finger of scotch. "A bit of both, my dear. A bit of both."
