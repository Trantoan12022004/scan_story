The drive down to the East River docks was conducted in absolute, tactical silence. A convoy of three black armored SUVs cut through the midday city traffic with the ruthless efficiency of a military advance. Inside the lead vehicle, Victor and I sat side by side, reviewing encrypted transcripts of employee communications intercepted by our private cyber-intelligence unit over the past forty-eight hours.

When the convoy rolled through the heavy reinforced gates of Pier 42, the bustling activity of longshoremen, crane operators, and freight handlers instantly ground to a halt. Heavy machinery hissed to a stop, and dozens of pairs of eyes turned nervously toward our vehicles as the doors swung open.

I stepped out first, my heels clicking sharply against the reinforced concrete. The salty air of the river carried the scent of diesel, rust, and brine. Victor stepped out right behind me, his towering frame commanding immediate, fearful obedience from the workers scattered across the concrete apron.

Waiting by the administrative container office was Thomas Vance, the veteran dock supervisor who had managed Pier 42 for over a decade. His face was pale, beads of sweat glistening on his forehead despite the cool breeze coming off the water.

"Mr. Costello. Mrs. Costello," Vance stammered, wiping his hands on his high-visibility vest as he hurried forward. "This is... unexpected. We didn't receive notice of an inspection today."

"Inspections are scheduled, Thomas," I said, stepping closer, my gaze locking onto his shifting eyes. "What we are doing today isn't an inspection. It's an excavation."

Victor didn't say a word. He simply walked past the supervisor toward the cavernous interior of Warehouse B, where the disputed containers had supposedly been processed. I followed closely, leaving Vance sputtering behind us, flanked by four of our elite security enforcers who smoothly cut off any possibility of retreat or communication.

Inside the dim, cavernous warehouse, rows of towering metal shelving units stretched toward the ceiling, housing industrial parts, raw materials, and high-value cargo. But as we walked deeper into aisle four, the sharp, unmistakable odor of chemical solvents hung heavy in the air—a scent that had nothing to do with standard shipping protocols.

"Open container 88-Delta," Victor commanded, pointing a leather-gloved finger at a massive steel box resting near the back wall.

Vance, who had scurried in behind us, turned ashen. "Sir, that unit contains heavy machinery components for the municipal transit expansion. It's fully sealed under federal seal—"

"Open it, Thomas," I repeated softly, the quiet deadliness in my tone freezing him in his tracks.

Two of our enforcers didn't wait for further hesitation. With practiced efficiency, heavy bolt cutters snapped the security seals, and the massive steel doors of the container swung wide open with a metallic groan.

Inside, there were no transit parts. Stacked neatly to the ceiling were wooden crates bearing the insignia of a rival international syndicate, filled to the brim with unregistered tactical firearms and encrypted communication relays.
