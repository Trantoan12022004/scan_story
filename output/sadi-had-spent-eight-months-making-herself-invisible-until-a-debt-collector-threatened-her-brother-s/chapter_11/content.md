Six months after the complete restructuring of the Castello syndicate, the sprawling estate in upstate New York stood as a fortress of absolute power wrapped in quiet, classical elegance. The heavy wrought-iron gates, once symbolizing a prison for the desperate and the damned, now functioned as the heavily guarded threshold of a sovereign state. Armed sentries in bespoke tactical suits patrolled the perimeter with disciplined precision, while the subterranean intelligence center hummed twenty-four hours a day, intercepting communication feeds across three continents.

Inside the master suite, I stood before the floor-to-ceiling mahogany armoire, fastening a diamond-encrusted platinum cuff link onto the sleeve of my charcoal-tailored blazer. My reflection stared back with cold, unwavering clarity. Gone was the bruised, trembling maid who had once scrubbed the marble floors of this very house under the tyrannical thumb of the old regime. In her place stood Sadi Castello—co-ruler of the East Coast underworld, architect of the syndicate's fiscal modernization, and a woman whose name commanded absolute terror in executive boardrooms and back-alley syndicates alike.

The heavy double doors of the suite swung open silently, and Victor strode in. He wore a crisp white dress shirt with the sleeves rolled to the elbows, revealing the faint, jagged scars of a life forged in blood and betrayal. His stormy gray eyes locked onto mine through the mirror, softening instantly with an intensity that only I could ever command.

"The European delegation's private jet just touched down at JFK," Victor said, his voice a deep, gravelly hum as he stepped up behind me, wrapping his hands around my waist and pressing a warm kiss to the crown of my head. "Vance is handling the transit detail, but the Marseille syndicate wants a direct audience with both of us before the sun sets."

I turned slowly in his embrace, resting my hands against his chest, feeling the steady, powerful rhythm of his heartbeat. "Let them wait in the East Wing conservatory," I replied, a cool smile touching my lips. "If they want to renegotiate the drug and shipping corridors through the Mediterranean, they need to understand that the rules have changed. We don't share territory; we lease it at our discretion."

Victor let out a low, appreciative laugh, his fingers tracing the line of my jaw. "They think they're dealing with old-world mobsters, Sadi. They have no idea they're walking into an execution chamber managed by an economist."

"An economist with a sovereign militia," I corrected, leaning up to press a firm kiss to his lips before stepping back to grab my briefcase. "Come on, husband. Let's go show them what an empire looks like when it's built to last."
