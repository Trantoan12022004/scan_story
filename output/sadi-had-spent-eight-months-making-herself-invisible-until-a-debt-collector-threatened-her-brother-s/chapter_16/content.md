Julian Thorne took a slow sip of his scotch before turning back to face us, his expression grim.

"You always believed the old Castello regime destroyed your family out of pure greed, Sadi," Thorne began, gesturing toward a plush leather armchair by the window. "You believed your father was just an innocent accountant caught in the crossfire of turf wars."

I didn't sit. I remained standing, my eyes locked onto his every micro-expression. "My father’s firm managed the syndicate's laundering accounts until he tried to back out. Then your uncle had him ruined, and my mother died of the stress six months later."

"Your father didn't just try to back out, Sadi," Thorne corrected softly, dropping a heavy file onto the glass coffee table. "He engineered a quiet coup. He wasn't the victim; he was the master strategist who tried to siphon forty million dollars from the Castello syndicate’s sovereign accounts to buy his family a clean escape to Switzerland."

The words hit the silent room like a physical blow. For a fraction of a second, the ground beneath my feet felt unsteady. I looked at Victor, whose gray eyes had darkened into a lethal storm, his hand instinctively resting on the grip of the concealed firearm beneath his jacket.

"That's a lie," I whispered, the ice in my voice cutting through the heavy air. "My father was an honest man."

"Your father was a genius who bit off more than he could chew," Thorne said, opening the file to reveal dozens of signed bank transfers, encrypted wire logs, and holographic signature verifications dating back fifteen years. "The old Castello regime didn't find out about it through routine auditing. They found out because someone inside the firm leaked the routing numbers to them."

I stepped forward, my hands flat on the glass table as I stared down at the documents. Every signature was authentic. Every account number matched the hidden ledgers of my childhood.

"Who?" I demanded, my voice dropping an octave into a terrifying, resonant whisper. "Who leaked the routing numbers to the Castellos?"

Thorne looked up, meeting my gaze without flinching. "Julian Vance’s father. And the man who helped him execute the cover-up isn't buried in a pauper's grave, Sadi. He’s currently sitting on the federal banking regulatory commission, drafting the exact oversight policies that are currently threatening to freeze your offshore assets."

The pieces clicked together with terrifying clarity. The past wasn't buried; it was weaponized. And the game we thought we had won six months ago was only the opening move.
