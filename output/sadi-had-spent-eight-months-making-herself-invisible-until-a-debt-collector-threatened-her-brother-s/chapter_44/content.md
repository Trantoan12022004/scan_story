The private jet banked smoothly over the Indian Ocean, turning its nose back toward the Americas as the golden lights of Perth faded into the star-studded Southern night. Inside the cabin, the heavy mahogany tables were clear of dossiers, threat assessments, and corporate spreadsheets for the first time in what felt like a lifetime. The war was over. The map of global commerce was entirely painted in the sovereign colors of the Castello empire.

I sat by the window, watching the endless velvet expanse of the sea blur beneath the wing. The platinum signet ring on my finger caught the soft reading light, gleaming beside Victor’s heavy gold band. Every scar, every sleepless night, every calculated risk and brutal betrayal had led us to this exact coordinate in space and time.

Victor walked out from the galley carrying two crystal tumblers filled with rare bourbon, setting one down before me before sliding into the plush leather seat across the aisle. His stormy gray eyes were free of the heavy, calculated focus that had defined them through every boardroom battle and back-alley confrontation.

"You're uncharacteristically quiet, Elena," he observed, taking a slow sip of his drink. "Usually, by the time a continent is secured, you're already drafting the five-year projection for the next expansion."

"There is no next expansion," I murmured, turning to meet his gaze with a faint, knowing smile. "We've reached the edge of the board. Every shipping lane, every resource corridor, every major financial hub on this planet answers to us. There's nothing left to conquer."

Victor let out a rich, resonant laugh, leaning back against the cushions. "A terrifying realization for a pair of apex predators. What do we do now that the world has run out of walls for us to break down?"

"We live in the empire we built," I answered softly, reaching across the narrow space to rest my hand over his. "No shadows. No enemies. Just us."
