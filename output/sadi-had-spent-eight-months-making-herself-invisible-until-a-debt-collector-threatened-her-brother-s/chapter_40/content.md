Singapore welcomed us with a suffocating, tropical heat and a futuristic skyline that rose like a cluster of glittering crystal spires from the edge of the South China Sea. The Marina Bay Sands dominated the harbor, its iconic boat-shaped rooftop floating effortlessly above the humid haze, mirroring the ambition of a city-state built on sheer willpower and economic supremacy.

Our limousine slipped through the immaculate streets of the financial district, delivering us directly to the corporate headquarters of the Lion City Logistics Syndicate—a loose confederation of regional shipping barons who had stubbornly resisted foreign consolidation for over a decade.

"They've called an assembly of all seven regional directors," Bruno murmured from the front passenger seat, turning his massive frame slightly to look back at us. He had traded his tactical gear for a bespoke charcoal suit that strained slightly across his broad shoulders, though his eyes remained as sharp and vigilant as ever. "They think they can intimidate us by sheer numbers."

"Let them try," Victor said smoothly, adjusting his cuffs. "Numbers only matter if the men behind them possess actual leverage. These regional directors are divided by greed and mutual distrust. They're already eating each other alive behind closed doors."

"That's why we're not here to negotiate with them," I added, smoothing the fabric of my tailored ivory blazer. "We're here to deliver an ultimatum."

The boardroom on the fiftieth floor was a sprawling, glass-walled amphitheater that offered a breathtaking, panoramic view of the bustling shipping lanes stretching out toward the Strait of Malacca. Seven men—representatives of the most powerful maritime families in Southeast Asia—sat around a massive circular conference table. Their expressions ranged from thinly veiled hostility to nervous anticipation.

As Victor and I entered the room, flanked by Bruno and our security detail, the murmurs died down instantly. Director Chen, an aging shipping tycoon with cold, reptilian eyes and a tailored silk suit, stood up slowly at the head of the table.

"Mr. Costello. Mrs. Costello," Chen said, his voice clipped and laced with haughty disdain. "Welcome to Singapore. We were informed that Castello logistics wished to discuss a partnership. But looking at your entourage, it feels less like a partnership and more like an occupation."

I didn't smile as I walked to the opposite side of the table, resting my palms lightly on the polished surface. My gaze locked onto Chen with an icy, unwavering intensity that made the older man subtly shift his weight.

"Partnerships require equals, Director Chen," I said, my voice carrying clearly across the silent room. "You and your syndicate are not our equals. You are a fragmented collection of regional operators whose profit margins have shrunk by thirty percent over the last two quarters due to inefficient routes and rising security costs."

Chen's face darkened instantly. "You presume a great deal, madam—"

"I presume nothing," Victor interrupted smoothly, stepping forward to stand beside me. He tossed a thick leather-bound dossier onto the center of the table, sliding it directly toward Chen. "That folder contains the financial audit of every single subsidiary operating under your syndicate. It details your offshore tax shelters in the Caymans, your smuggled cargo workarounds through the Sunda Strait, and the names of the port authorities on your payroll."

The color drained from Chen’s face as he stared down at the folder. The other six directors began whispering frantically among themselves, casting terrified glances at one another.

"If those documents find their way to Interpol and the Monetary Authority of Singapore," I continued softly, "every single one of you will be spending the next twenty years in a maximum-security prison while your family fortunes are seized by the state."
