The rain beat a relentless tattoo against the reinforced glass windows of the Senate office building as Senator Harrison Vance stared in horror at the glowing monitor on his desk. It was 2:00 AM, and the digital kingdom he had spent two decades building was collapsing in real-time.

His chief of staff had already been picked up by federal agents two hours prior, singing like a canary about offshore bribes, illicit campaign contributions, and direct coordination with European crime syndicates. And worst of all, the documents leaked to the DOJ weren't crude forgeries; they were airtight financial records complete with wire receipts, encrypted emails, and notarized confessions bearing the signatures of his closest associates.

The heavy oak door of his private office burst open without a knock.

Two men in bespoke charcoal suits stepped into the room. They weren't federal agents. They moved with the cold, silent efficiency of high-tier syndicate enforcers. They didn't draw weapons; they didn't need to.

Senator Vance stumbled backward against his mahogany bookshelves, his face drained of all color. "Who... who sent you? How did you get past Capitol security?"

The lead man—a tall, imposing operative bearing the Castello family insignia on his lapel pin—stepped forward, placing a single manila envelope on the center of the desk.

"The Castello family sends their regards, Senator," the operative said, his voice flat and devoid of emotion. "Mrs. Costello extends her apologies that your political career had to be cut short, but your services are no longer required."

Vance swallowed hard, his hands trembling as he stared at the envelope. He opened it with shaking fingers. Inside was a single, typewritten document: a resignation letter addressed to the President of the Senate, pre-signed with a digital notary seal that matched his own parliamentary credentials.

"Sign it," the operative commanded quietly. "Hand over your parliamentary access codes, and you will be permitted to board a private flight to a non-extradition country before dawn. Refuse, and your federal indictment will include charges of high treason carrying a life sentence."

There was no negotiation. There was no appeal to justice. There was only the brutal, inescapable gravity of Castello power. With trembling hands, Senator Harrison Vance picked up a pen and signed away his legacy.
