The morning light that had initially promised serenity over the Castello estate quickly gave way to the harsh, unrelenting reality of corporate and territorial governance. By mid-morning, the expansive mahogany-paneled study on the top floor of the main villa was a hive of controlled chaos. Dossiers, shipping manifests, customs clearance certificates, and digital tablet displays covered the massive oak desk, organized into meticulous color-coded piles by our chief logistics coordinator, Marcus.

I sat in the high-backed leather chair, my fingers resting lightly on the polished armrests, while Victor paced slowly near floor-to-ceiling windows that overlooked the private helipad. His presence was a towering, immovable force, yet his eyes remained fixed on a satellite feed of the East River docks displayed on the wall-mounted monitor.

"Marcus," I said, my voice cutting through the ambient hum of secure servers and muted phone lines. "Walk me through the discrepancy at Pier 42 again. The tonnage reported by our harbor master doesn't match the container counts registered by the municipal port authority."

Marcus adjusted his wire-rimmed glasses, swallowing hard as he stepped forward to tap a section of the digital map. "Mrs. Costello, sir... according to our internal manifests, three containers of high-grade microprocessors designated for the downtown tech hub arrived on schedule early Tuesday morning. However, port logs show those same container serial numbers were officially checked out and loaded onto a freight train heading north toward Montreal twelve hours before they even docked."

Victor stopped pacing. The silence in the room instantly dropped several degrees, the air thickening with a dangerous, electric tension. He didn't raise his voice when he spoke, but every person in the room froze.

"A phantom shipment," Victor said, his stormy gray eyes narrowing. "Someone is either skimming our logistics network or using our secure customs clearance channels as a ghost front for contraband."

"Or both," I replied, standing up and smoothing the lapels of my tailored charcoal blazer. The platinum signet ring caught the overhead light, a stark symbol of authority. "A discrepancy of that magnitude requires more than a simple clerical error. It requires inside knowledge of our security protocols—the exact protocols we were just reviewing this morning."

Victor walked over, stopping just beside my chair, his hand resting briefly on the backrest. "Then we don't just audit the docks, Sadi. We pull the entire supply chain apart, starting with the dock supervisor. If someone thinks they can play games inside our perimeter, they're about to learn what happens when you wake a sleeping predator."
