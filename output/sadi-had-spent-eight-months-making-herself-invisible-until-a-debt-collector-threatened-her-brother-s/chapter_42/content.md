The private jet banked sharply over the rugged, sun-scorched coastline of Western Australia, descending toward the sprawling mining and logistics hubs of Perth. Below us, the landscape unfolded in a breathtaking tapestry of rust-red earth, jagged mountain ranges, and deep blue ocean.

We had conquered North America, dominated Europe, and subjugated Asia in what felt like a whirlwind of calculated corporate warfare. But the Southern Hemisphere represented the final, untamed frontier—a vast network of resource extraction, heavy industry, and raw mineral trade that fed the manufacturing engines of the entire planet.

"The Iron Baron of Perth," Bruno muttered from the opposite seat, scrolling through a fresh intelligence dossier on his ruggedized tablet. "Marcus Vance—no relation to your uncle, thankfully. He controls ninety percent of the iron ore and lithium exports out of the Pilbara region. He's notoriously ruthless, operates his own private security militia, and has historically refused to deal with international syndicates."

"Marcus Vance sounds like a man who has lived too long without facing a superior force," I remarked coldly, sipping my black coffee as I gazed out the reinforced cabin window at the endless expanse of the Australian outback.

"Men like Vance build their kingdoms on physical isolation," Victor observed, his eyes scanning the satellite telemetry of the massive mining compounds below. "They think distance protects them from the rules of global finance. We'll show him that distance is just another variable to be managed."

Our landing at Perth International Airport was met with a stark, intimidating reception. A convoy of three armored matte-black SUVs waited on the tarmac directly beside our private jet, flanked by a dozen heavily armed security operatives wearing tactical body armor bearing the insignia of Vance Minerals.

As the jet stairs lowered, Marcus Vance himself stood at the base of the steps. He was a towering, heavily built man in his fifties with a weathered, sun-baked face, a thick salt-and-pepper beard, and a gaze as hard and unyielding as the iron ore he mined from the earth.

"Mr. and Mrs. Costello, I presume?" Vance rumbled, his voice deep and gravelly, carrying the distinct Australian accent of the outback. "You've traveled a long way to knock on a door that wasn't open to visitors."

I descended the stairs first, my posture erect, my expression completely impassive as I met his intimidating gaze without a single flinch. "We don't wait for doors to open, Mr. Vance. We build our own entrances."

Vance’s thick eyebrows rose in mild surprise, a grim smile touching his lips. "I heard stories about the legendary Castello takeover machine cutting through Zurich and Singapore. Didn't expect the architect to be quite so... elegant."

"Elegance is just camouflage for absolute power," Victor said smoothly, stepping down beside me and standing shoulder-to-shoulder with a presence that instantly commanded the entire tarmac. "Shall we discuss terms in your boardroom, or would you prefer to surrender your supply chains out here in the sun?"
