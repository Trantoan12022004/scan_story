Dawn broke over the Castello estate with a fierce, blinding explosion of golden light that burned away the last wisps of the nocturnal fog. By six in the morning, the massive mahogany-paneled study on the top floor of the villa was already humming with intense, focused energy.

Digital monitors displayed real-time financial ticker streams from Tokyo, London, and New York, while physical dossiers detailing the upcoming European shipping acquisitions were stacked in precise, orderly columns across the center desk.

I sat at the head of the long oak table, dressed in a sharp, impeccably tailored cream-colored pantsuit that exuded quiet, absolute authority. My hair was pulled back into a sleek, professional knot, and the antique black-diamond pendant caught the morning sun streaming through the arched windows.

Marcus, our chief logistics coordinator, stood near the projection screen, tapping a stylus against a digital map tracking our cargo fleets across the Atlantic.

"Mrs. Costello," Marcus reported, his voice crisp and professional. "The European trade delegation has landed at the private airstrip ten minutes ahead of schedule. Their lead negotiator, Director Van Der Berg, has already signaled his willingness to accept our proposed tariff rates without arbitration."

I nodded slowly, not the least bit surprised. "Van Der Berg is a pragmatist. He knows that trying to bypass our shipping lanes in the North Atlantic would cost his conglomerate millions in delays and insurance spikes. He made the only rational choice available to him."

The heavy oak doors of the study swung open smoothly, and Victor stepped into the room. He wore a crisp charcoal suit with an open-collared white shirt, looking every bit the ruthless titan of industry who commanded the respect of global markets. He walked over to my chair, placing a steaming cup of dark espresso on the corner of the desk before leaning down to murmur quietly in my ear.

"The delegates are waiting in the main conference parlor," Victor said, a faint, sardonic smile playing on his lips. "Van Der Berg looks like a man who expects to be eaten alive for breakfast."

I took a sip of the rich, bitter espresso, feeling the familiar, invigorating caffeine flood my senses as I stood up from my chair and smoothed the front of my jacket.

"Not eaten alive, Victor," I corrected him with a cold, elegant smile, the platinum signet ring catching the morning light as I adjusted my cuffs. "Simply reminded of who writes the rules of international trade."
