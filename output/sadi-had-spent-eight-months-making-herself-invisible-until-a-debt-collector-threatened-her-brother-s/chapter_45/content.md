Chicago welcomed us with a crisp autumn dawn that painted the towering skyscrapers in strokes of copper and violet. As our limousine glided through the familiar downtown streets, the city hummed its steady, vibrant song of commerce and human endeavor—a song that now played entirely beneath our oversight.

When the elevator opened directly onto the private penthouse foyer, the silence of the sanctuary wrapped around us like a warm embrace. Bruno had already vanished downstairs to manage the estate logistics, leaving the sprawling glass-walled residence entirely to the two of us.

I walked straight to the floor-to-ceiling windows of the main living area, pressing my palms against the cool, thick glass. Below, the bustling morning traffic of Michigan Avenue looked like tiny, orderly ants tracing predictable paths across the concrete grid.

Victor came up behind me, sliding his arms around my waist and drawing me back against his chest. His chin rested gently on my shoulder, his breath warm against my neck.

"It feels different this time," I whispered, closing my eyes as I leaned into his strength. "When we first came back from Zurich, I kept waiting for the server to ping, for another shadow to crawl out of the woodwork."

"The woodwork has been burned down, Elena," Victor murmured, his voice a steady, grounding anchor. "There's nothing left out there except the world we permit to exist."

"Then let's make it a quiet one," I said, turning within his embrace to wrap my arms around his neck, looking up into those deep, unreadable eyes that had once been my greatest mystery and were now my only home.

For the first time since the blood and broken glass of my past life, the horizon felt completely clear.
