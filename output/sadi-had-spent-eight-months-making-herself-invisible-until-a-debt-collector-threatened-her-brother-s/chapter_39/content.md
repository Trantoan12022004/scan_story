The transition of power was swift, surgical, and entirely bloodless—at least in the physical sense. By Thursday afternoon, the board of Sterling Holdings had been restructured from top to bottom. The ancestral land grants, the Channel Tunnel logistics contracts, and the extensive port facilities along the English coast were integrated seamlessly into the Castello empire.

We stood on the reinforced glass balcony of our temporary penthouse suite at the Shard, looking out over the sprawling, rain-slicked panorama of London. The city lights twinkled like scattered diamonds against the darkening velvet of the evening sky, stretching out toward the horizon where the Thames wound its serpentine path toward the sea.

"Sterling retired to his country estate in Devon," I noted, swirling a glass of aged vintage burgundy as I watched the distant traffic crawl across Tower Bridge below. "His lawyers called an hour ago to confirm the final asset transfer. No legal challenges. No resistance."

Victor stood beside me, his arm resting lightly around my shoulders, his thumb tracing the smooth fabric of my sleeve. "Men like Sterling spend their entire lives believing their birthright is a shield against the laws of commerce. They forget that the only thing that truly protects an empire is absolute, unyielding control."

"Just like my uncle Heinrich," I murmured, the memory of the Zurich conspiracy flitting briefly across my mind like a distant ghost. "He thought his name and his bloodline made him untouchable. He found out the hard way that power belongs to those willing to build it from the dirt."

Victor turned me gently to face him, his stormy gray eyes searching mine with a fierce, possessive warmth. "Heinrich was a relic of an older, clumsier world, Elena. He tried to rule through fear and betrayal. We rule through inevitability. Every brick in this global empire has been laid by our hands, cemented by our loyalty to one another."

"And the world keeps getting bigger," I whispered, a slow smile touching my lips.

"Then we keep expanding the borders," Victor replied instantly. "Where to next, my queen? Asia? The resource corridors of South America? Name the territory, and I'll clear the board."

"Let's look east," I said, my mind racing through the global logistics map like a grandmaster surveying a chessboard. "The Pacific shipping lanes are still fragmented. Too many regional cartels squabbling over scraps in Singapore and Hong Kong. It's time someone brought order to the chaos."

Victor’s smile widened, sharp and triumphant. "Singapore it is. Let's book the jet for tomorrow morning."
