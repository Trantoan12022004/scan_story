As evening descended upon the Castello estate, the twenty acres of lawns, stone ramparts, and ancient timberlines were wrapped in profound, absolute stillness. The security monitors pulsed with their steady, rhythmic blue light, while the global shipping networks we controlled moved millions of tons of commerce across the oceans in silent, automated perfection.

The world outside continued to spin in ignorance and awe, terrified of the invisible sovereign state that held the levers of modern civilization. But inside the master study, the room was bathed in the warm, amber glow of a single brass lamp.

I sat at the heavy mahogany desk with a fresh journal open before me, my fountain pen moving in smooth, confident strokes across the heavy-grain paper. On the page taking shape beneath my hand wasn't a tactical blueprint, a financial ledger, or a threat assessment.

It was a sketch of the Hudson Valley ridgeline at sunset, capturing the exact play of violet and gold light that had marked our victory over the old regime.

Soft, rapid footsteps padded down the hallway outside the heavy oak doors, followed by a muffled giggle and the faint, sweet sound of a silver chime.

Clink.

The heavy oak doors swung open an inch, and two young greyhound puppies—gifts from Victor to mark our anniversary—peeked into the study, watching me with a mixture of curiosity and playful affection.

I looked up from my desk, setting the pen aside, and broke into a rare, genuine smile.

"Past your bedtime, commanders," I called out softly, standing up from the leather executive chair.

Without hesitation, they bounded into the room, rushing toward my desk like a victorious infantry after a long campaign. I knelt down, laughing as they showered my hands with energetic affection, completely erasing the weight of the empire we carried on our shoulders.

We walked out of the study together—not as syndicate bosses, not as rulers of an invisible global network, but simply as a family sharing a quiet evening at home.
