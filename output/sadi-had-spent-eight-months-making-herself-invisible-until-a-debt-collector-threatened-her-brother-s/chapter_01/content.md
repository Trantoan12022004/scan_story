Industrial bleach had ruined Sadi Hayes’s hands.

At twenty-four, her knuckles were cracked, red, and bleeding from scrubbing stains no one at the Castello estate dared explain.

The mansion paid three times the usual wage, but the money wasn’t for cleaning.

It was for silence.

The staff never stared at the armed men arriving after midnight. They never asked why Persian rugs suddenly needed replacing. And they never looked Victor Costello—the most feared man in the city—directly in the eye.

For eight months, Sadi had made herself invisible.

She needed every paycheck for her sixteen-year-old brother’s leukemia treatment. When hope became more expensive than she could afford, she borrowed sixty thousand dollars from Mick O’Shea, a lender who made certain desperate people never escaped their debts.

At one forty-five in the morning, Sadi was polishing a brass sconce outside Victor’s closed office when her burner phone vibrated.

“Sadi, darling,” Mick said. “You owe me twenty thousand. Tonight.”

“I don’t have it.”

“I know.”

Then his voice turned almost pleasant.

“I’m looking at St. Jude’s right now.”

Sadi stopped breathing.

“My boy Declan walked into the oncology wing without anyone stopping him. Your brother looks peaceful with all those tubes connected to him.”

“No,” she whispered. “Stay away from Sam.”

“You have one hour. Send the money—or one of those machines fails.”

The call ended.

Sadi stared at the cracked screen. Then her knees gave way, and she slid down the wall outside Victor Costello’s office.

She tried to smother her sobs with both bleeding hands.

Inside, the feared mafia boss stopped speaking.

The office door opened without a sound. Sadi looked up and saw Victor standing over her in shirtsleeves, a pistol resting against his thigh.

Before she could explain, the phone buzzed again.
