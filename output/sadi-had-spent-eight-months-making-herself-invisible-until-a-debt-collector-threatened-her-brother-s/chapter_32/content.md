The sprawling expanse of the Castello estate glowed beneath a magnificent canopy of amber and amethyst as the sun began its descent toward the distant mountain ridge. The manicured lawns, stretching for acres around the perimeter of the main villa, seemed to absorb the twilight, casting long, elegant shadows across the private rose gardens and the winding stone pathways.

Inside the grand conservatory, the atmosphere was a sanctuary of refined tranquility. Rare orchids and exotic ferns filled the glass-domed room with a rich, earthy fragrance, mingling with the subtle scent of beeswax and polished mahogany. I sat on a velvet-upholstered chaise lounge, wearing a midnight-blue silk gown that pooled softly around my ankles. The platinum signet ring caught the dying amber rays of the sun, flashing a brilliant, unyielding beacon of light against my finger.

Across from me, Victor sat in a high-backed leather armchair, a leather-bound journal resting open on his knee, though his stormy gray gaze remained fixed entirely on me. The heavy burdens of the day—the corporate acquisitions, the municipal restructuring, and the silent enforcement of our dominance across the city's financial sector—seemed miles away in this quiet, sacred pocket of time.

"You've been remarkably quiet this evening, Sadi," Victor remarked, his deep voice carrying a note of tender curiosity as he set the journal aside on a low marble table. "Even for someone who usually uses the evening hours to plan tomorrow's victories."

I took a slow sip of herbal tea from a delicate bone-china cup, letting the warmth soothe my throat before looking up to meet his intense, unwavering eyes. "I wasn't planning, Victor. I was simply remembering."

Victor stood up, crossing the polished stone floor with the effortless, predatory grace that had once made half the city fear him and the other half revere him. He stopped beside my chaise, leaning down slightly to rest his warm hand gently on my shoulder, his thumb tracing the line of my collarbone through the silk fabric.

"Remembering the cracked hands and the cold stone floors of the old mansion?" he asked softly, his tone devoid of pity, filled instead with a deep, reverent pride.

"Remembering everything," I whispered, leaning my cheek against the back of his hand. "Every cold morning, every whispered insult in the corridors, and every single step of the climb that brought me here, standing beside you at the top of the world."
