The silence in the boardroom was absolute. You could hear the distant, muffled hum of cargo ships moving across the harbor far below. Chen slowly reached out, his trembling fingers opening the dossier, his eyes scanning the damning financial records with mounting horror. He knew every page was authentic. He knew there was no escape.

"What... what do you want?" Chen whispered, his voice broken of all its previous arrogance.

"Total integration," Victor stated, leaning down slightly, resting his knuckles on the table as he loomed over the older man. "As of this moment, the Lion City Logistics Syndicate is dissolved. All vessels, port facilities, and transit contracts are absorbed directly into the Castello global network. You and your directors will retain minor advisory shares, but operational control belongs entirely to us."

"That's... that's a hostile takeover of an entire regional economy," one of the junior directors stammered, half-rising from his chair.

"It’s not a hostile takeover," I corrected him, my voice cutting through the air like a blade. "It’s a mercy. We could have destroyed you overnight. Instead, we’re allowing you to keep your titles and your wealth, provided you step in line and keep the ships moving on schedule."

Chen looked around the table at his panicked, silent colleagues. He saw no support, no defiance—only the stark realization that they were entirely outmatched by a force they could neither bribe nor intimidate. Slowly, defeatedly, Chen picked up the heavy gold fountain pen that had been placed beside the dossier and signed his name at the bottom of the surrender agreement.

One by one, the other directors followed suit, their hands shaking as they signed away their regional empire to the new sovereigns of the global shipping lanes.

As we left the boardroom and stepped back out onto the sunlit observation deck overlooking the harbor, the warm tropical breeze ruffled my hair. Below us, hundreds of massive container ships traced their precise, orderly paths across the glistening waters of the strait, carrying the lifeblood of global commerce.

"Well," Victor murmured, sliding his arm around my waist and pulling me close as he looked out over the endless sea. "The Pacific is secured. Every port from Los Angeles to Shanghai now answers to our flag."

"Not every port," I replied with a soft, knowing smile, turning my face up to meet his eyes. "There's still the vast expanse of the Southern Hemisphere. Africa, Australia, the untamed frontiers of global trade."

Victor’s eyes flashed with that familiar, dangerous fire of endless ambition. "Then I suppose we have work to do, my queen."
