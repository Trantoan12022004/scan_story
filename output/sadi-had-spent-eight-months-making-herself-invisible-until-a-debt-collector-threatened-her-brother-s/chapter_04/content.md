The heavy double doors of the seventh-floor oncology ward swung open with a soft pneumatic hiss.

The corridor was dead silent. The usual frantic scurrying of night-shift nurses had been entirely halted; instead, Victor’s private security guards stood like iron statues every ten feet down the pastel-painted hallway, their dark suits and concealed sidearms turning a sterile children's hospital wing into an armed fortress.

Sadi walked beside Victor, her legs feeling like lead. Carmine trailed a step behind them, dragging a sweating, shaking Mick O’Shea by the expensive lapel of his silk-blend suit. Mick’s face was slick with cold sweat, his breath coming in ragged, shallow gasps as his heels dragged across the polished linoleum.

"Mr. Costello—please, Boss, I didn't know!" Mick whimpered, his voice echoing hollowly against the walls. "If I knew she was under your protection, I swear on my children, I would have paid off her debt myself! I’m a businessman, I'm reasonable—"

"You were reasonable until you walked into a children's ward and threatened a sixteen-year-old boy fighting for his life," Victor said softly, not even bothering to look back at the loan shark as they approached Room 704.

The door to Sam’s room was glass. Through the pane, Sadi could see her little brother lying motionless in the narrow hospital bed. A spiderweb of clear plastic tubes connected his frail, pale arms to IV poles and heart monitors. The steady, rhythmic beep-beep-beep of the machine was the only sound cutting through the heavy silence of the floor.

Sadi’s hand flew to her mouth, a fresh wave of tears pricking her eyes. Sam looked so small, so fragile, swallowed up by the oversized hospital gown.

Victor stopped outside the door, raising his hand to halt Carmine and Mick. He turned his dark, unblinking gaze onto the trembling loan shark.

"Open the door, Mick," Victor instructed quietly.

"I—I can't go in there—"

"Open the door," Victor repeated, his voice dropping an octave into a tone of absolute, terrifying command.

Mick fumbled frantically, his shaking fingers pushing the heavy glass door open just enough for them to step inside. The smell of antiseptic, sterile linen, and faint ozone hung thick in the air.

Sam didn't wake up as they entered; his breathing was shallow, his chest rising and falling beneath the thin white blanket with agonizing slowness.

Victor stepped up to the side of the bed, looking down at the sleeping sixteen-year-old boy. For a long moment, the ruthless mafia boss—the man who controlled the city's ports, unions, and financial underworld—looked at Sam with an expression that was shockingly tender, almost sorrowful.

Then he turned back to Mick O’Shea, who was pressed flat against the opposite wall like a pinned insect.

"You told her she had one hour before one of those machines failed," Victor said, his voice as cold as a glacier.

"I was bluffing! It was just a threat, a negotiation tactic! I never touched a dial!" Mick cried out, dropping heavily to his knees on the hospital floor, pressing his palms together in a desperate plea.

"A negotiation tactic," Victor repeated slowly. He reached down, unholstering his Heckler & Karen USP compact with a smooth, practiced flick of his wrist, and laid the heavy black steel weapon flat on the rolling bedside tray right beside Sam’s chart.

The room went dead silent. Even the heart monitor seemed to tick louder in the vacuum.

"Pick it up, Mick," Victor whispered.

Mick stared at the gun on the tray as if it were a live cobra. "What? No—no, Mr. Costello, I don't carry piece, I’m a financier, I—"

"You threatened to turn off his machines," Victor said, stepping closer, towering over the cowering loan shark. "You wanted to play God with a child's life to squeeze twenty thousand dollars out of a maid who works twelve hours a day scrubbing my floors. So let's see how much stomach you have for it."
