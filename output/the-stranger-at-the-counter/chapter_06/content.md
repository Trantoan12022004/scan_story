Poppy’s surgery was scheduled for Monday morning.

For the next forty-eight hours, Marlo lived inside a strange suspended reality.

Her daughter watched cartoons from a hospital bed.

Preston brought books.

Ruthie brought flowers despite having six stitches over one eyebrow.

Catherine brought legal documents.

And Elias brought nothing.

He simply came.

He sat outside Poppy’s room.

Sometimes for hours.

He never demanded conversation.

Never tried to force himself into the family.

Poppy eventually noticed.

“Why does Mr. Eyebrows sit out there?”

Marlo nearly choked.

“Who?”

“The scary one.”

“He can hear you.”

“Good.”

Elias’s voice came from the hallway.

Poppy grinned.

“Come in.”

Marlo looked at him.

Elias hesitated.

Poppy patted the chair.

“You look weird standing there.”

He entered.

Poppy studied his tattoos.

“Did those hurt?”

“Yes.”

“Why did you get them?”

“Bad judgment.”

“Mom says mistakes are how your brain learns.”

Elias looked at Marlo.

“Your mother is probably right.”

Poppy leaned closer.

“Are you Mom’s boyfriend?”

“Poppy.”

“What?”

Elias remained perfectly calm.

“No.”

Poppy looked disappointed.

“Do you want to be?”

Marlo wanted the hospital floor to open.

Elias considered.

“That is a complicated question.”

Poppy nodded seriously.

“Mom likes complicated things.”

“I do not.”

“You own a bakery.”

“That isn’t complicated.”

“You wake up at three.”

Elias’s mouth twitched.

Poppy pointed triumphantly.

“See?”

For five minutes, Marlo forgot about everything.

Then Catherine arrived.

“We found something.”

They stepped into the hall.

“What?”

“Your father had a safe-deposit box.”

Marlo stared.

“Where?”

“A bank that merged twice since then.”

“How did you find it?”

“The florist.”

Marlo turned.

Ruthie raised one hand.

“I remembered he gave me a key.”

“Twenty years ago?”

“I have a drawer.”

“That is not an explanation.”

“It is when you’re sixty-eight.”

They retrieved the box that afternoon.

Inside were three things.

A cassette tape.

A ledger.

And a letter addressed to Marlo.

Her hands shook as she opened it.

My sweet girl,

If you ever read this, then I failed to come home.

Marlo stopped.

Elias stood beside her.

She continued.

I need you to understand that whatever people tell you about my work, I did everything because I wanted you and your mother safe.

There are men who believe money belongs to whoever is strong enough to take it.

I learned too late that silence helps those men.

Marlo wiped her eyes.

Your mother’s inheritance was stolen before she ever knew it existed.

I found the records.

I intended to return everything.

But Vittorio discovered what I knew.

There is one person I trust to make this right someday.

Elias.

Marlo looked at him.

He closed his eyes.

The letter continued.

He is young now. Too young. But there is goodness in him that his family has not managed to destroy.

If I am wrong, forgive me.

If I am right, trust him only when he gives you a reason.

Do not trust the Ashbys.

Especially Charles.

Marlo breathed slowly.

Then she read the final paragraph.

And please, sweetheart, do not spend your life avenging mine.

Live.

Love.

Raise a family.

Bake something your mother will complain is too sweet.

Let the people who hurt us become smaller than the life you build afterward.

Dad.

Marlo cried.

Not loudly.

She simply folded in on herself.

Elias caught her before she reached the floor.

For several minutes, she let him hold her.

Then she remembered the cassette.

“What’s on it?”

They found an old player through Ruthie.

The recording crackled.

Her father’s voice filled the room.

Marlo had not heard it in twenty years.

She covered her mouth.

The recording captured an argument.

Her father.

Vittorio.

And Charles.

Money.

Trust documents.

Threats.

Then Vittorio said clearly:

“You should have left it alone.”

Marlo’s father answered:

“It belongs to Ellen and Marlo.”

Charles interrupted.

“Give us the ledger.”

“No.”

Vittorio’s voice became colder.

“You have a daughter.”

The recording ended.

Silence.

Catherine spoke first.

“That is evidence.”

“Of murder?”

“Not directly.”

Elias opened the ledger.

Pages of payments.

Shell corporations.

Transfers.

Names.

Then he stopped.

“What?”

Elias turned the ledger toward them.

One payment had been made two days before Marlo’s father died.

Recipient:

A private security company.

Owner:

Charles Ashby.

Preston stared.

“My father…”

Catherine nodded grimly.

“May have arranged the accident.”

Preston left the room without speaking.

Marlo did not follow.

She could not.

Everything felt too heavy.

Police and federal investigators became involved that evening.

Catherine made the calls.

Elias handed over copies of records connected to Vittorio.

Marlo noticed.

“You’re giving them everything.”

“Yes.”

“Even things that hurt your family?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

He looked at her.

“Because your father was right.”

“About what?”

“Silence.”

The next morning Charles Ashby disappeared.

His house was empty.

His phones were off.

His passport remained behind.

Police assumed he had fled by car.

Preston sat beside Poppy’s bed looking twenty years older.

“I helped him.”

Marlo sat across from him.

“You didn’t know.”

“I believed him.”

“Yes.”

“I filed for custody.”

“Yes.”

He looked at her.

“I’m sorry.”

Marlo was quiet.

Three years earlier, she had imagined this apology countless times.

In every fantasy she screamed.

Or slapped him.

Or delivered a perfect speech.

Instead she said, “You hurt us.”

“I know.”

“You don’t get to erase that because you’re sorry.”

“I know.”

“But if you want to know your daughter, then show up.”

Preston looked stunned.

“Really?”

“Not for me.”

He nodded.

“For her.”

A nurse entered.

“Surgery prep begins in twenty minutes.”

Everything else vanished.

Marlo stayed beside Poppy until they rolled her toward the operating room.

Poppy looked frightened.

“Mom?”

“I’m here.”

“What if I wake up and you’re gone?”

“I won’t be.”

“Promise?”

“Promise.”

Poppy looked at Elias.

“You too?”

He froze.

Marlo looked at him.

Elias stepped closer.

“I’ll be here.”

Poppy nodded.

“Okay.”

The doors closed.

Marlo stood staring at them.

Then her strength disappeared.

Elias took her hand.

This time, she was the one who laced their fingers together.

Hours passed.

Three.

Four.

Five.

Finally the surgeon appeared.

Marlo stood so quickly the chair toppled.

“How is she?”

The surgeon removed his cap.

And smiled.

“The repair went beautifully.”

Marlo sobbed.

Preston covered his face.

Ruthie cried.

Elias closed his eyes.

“She’ll need recovery time,” the surgeon said, “but I expect her to have a much easier life.”

Marlo hugged the doctor.

Then Ruthie.

Then Preston.

Finally she turned toward Elias.

He stood a few feet away.

Still.

Watching.

She crossed the distance.

And hugged him.

His arms closed around her carefully.

Then his phone rang.

He ignored it.

It rang again.

Marlo pulled back.

“Answer.”

Elias looked at the screen.

Unknown number.

He answered.

“Marchetti.”

A voice spoke.

Elias’s expression went blank.

“Where?”

He listened.

Then disconnected.

“What happened?”

He looked at Marlo.

“They found Charles.”

“Where?”

“Outside Boston.”

“Alive?”

“Yes.”

Marlo exhaled.

“Then it’s over.”

Elias did not move.

“What?”

“He wasn’t running.”

“What do you mean?”

“He was going somewhere.”

“Where?”

Elias looked toward the hospital room where Poppy was recovering.

“To the bakery.”

Marlo stared.

“Why?”

Elias’s voice turned quiet.

“Because he says there’s something hidden inside Heart and Crumb that your father never intended you to find.”
