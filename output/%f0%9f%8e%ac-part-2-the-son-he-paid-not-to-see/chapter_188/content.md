“I know.”

The whole corral went silent.

The man stared at her.

“What did you say?”

The girl kept her hand against the stallion’s face.

Her voice was quiet.

“He missed her.”

The man went completely still.

The horse closed its eyes under her touch.

The girl swallowed hard.

“My mama used to sing to him.”

The crowd looked at one another, confused.

The man took a slow step closer.

“Your mama?”

The girl nodded.

“She said if I ever found him, he’d know me.”

The stallion breathed out heavily and pressed its head against her chest.

A few of the ranch hands actually stepped back.

The man’s face had changed now.

All the color was gone.

“What was your mother’s name?”

The girl looked up at him.

“Evelyn.”

The lead rope slipped from his hand.

Nobody laughed now.

Nobody moved.

The girl’s eyes filled with tears, but she kept her voice steady.

“She told me he was never dangerous after she left.”

The man looked at the horse.

Then at the little girl.

Then back at the horse kneeling in the dust before her.

His lips parted, but no sound came out.

The girl reached into the pocket of her dress and pulled out an old folded photograph.

A younger woman stood beside the same black stallion.

Beside her was the man in denim, smiling.

The girl held it out with shaking fingers.

“My mom said if you saw me with him, you’d know who I am.”

The man took the photo like it might burn him.

His eyes filled instantly.

The girl’s chin trembled.

“She said you didn’t leave because you stopped loving us.”

The man looked shattered now.

The crowd watched him fall apart in front of them.

Then the girl said the words that broke him completely.

“She said you left because Grandpa told you she lost the baby.”

Her lip quivered.

“But I’m the baby.”
