The teenage boy stared at the woman.

“Who are you?”

She struggled to answer.

“Someone who should’ve come sooner.”

He immediately moved his brother behind him.

“Stay away from us.”

“I understand.”

Her lips trembled.

“But your mother asked me to wait until you needed me.”

The teenager’s face hardened.

“My mother is dead.”

The woman nodded through tears.

“I know.”

She reached carefully into her bag and pulled out a sealed envelope.

The boy froze.

He recognized the handwriting immediately.

“That’s Mom’s.”

The woman handed it to the judge.

The judge opened the letter silently.

His expression changed as he read.

Then he looked at the woman.

“You’re named here.”

The teenager’s breathing quickened.

“What does it say?”

The woman finally broke.

“I’m your mother’s sister.”

He stared at her.

“We don’t have an aunt.”

“You do.”

Her voice cracked.

“Your mother and I hadn’t spoken in twelve years.”

The younger boy peeked around his brother.

“Why?”

The woman covered her mouth for a moment.

“Because I chose our father when he threw her out.”

The teenager’s eyes filled with anger.

“So why are you here now?”

She looked directly at him.

“Because she called me from the hospital.”

His face changed.

“She made me promise something too.”

The boy’s lips trembled.

“What?”

The woman looked at both brothers.

“That I wouldn’t replace you.”

She turned toward the judge.

“That I’d help him become his brother’s guardian the moment the law allowed it.”

The teenager stopped breathing.

The judge slowly set the letter down.

Then the younger boy whispered the question he’d been terrified to ask all morning.

“Are they taking my brother away?”

The judge looked at him.

“No.”

The teenager’s knees nearly gave out.

His little brother grabbed him tighter.

And the judge’s final words broke whatever strength he had left.

“Your mother didn’t leave you without a family.”

He glanced at the teenager.

“She made sure the family she trusted most was still you.”
