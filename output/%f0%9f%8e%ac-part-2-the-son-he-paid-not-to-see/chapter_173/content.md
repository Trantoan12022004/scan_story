The final digit beeped.

A green light flashed.

Then the lock clicked open.

A sharp breath rippled through the ballroom.

The older man went pale.

“No.”

The boy opened the locker door slowly.

There was no stack of money inside.

No prize.

Just a small sealed envelope and an old silver key.

The boy stared at them for a moment, then reached in.

“Don’t touch that.”

The older man’s voice came out too fast.

Too afraid.

The boy turned, the envelope in his hand.

“So you knew what was in here.”

The man stepped closer, trying to lower his voice.

“Give that to me.”

The boy’s fingers tightened around it.

“My mother told me if I ever found you, I should watch your face when I opened the locker.”

The man froze.

The room seemed to stop breathing with him.

The boy looked down at the handwriting on the envelope.

It was shaky.

Faded.

But clear.

He swallowed hard.

“It has my name on it.”

The older man’s mouth opened, but nothing came out.

The boy broke the seal and unfolded the letter with dirty, trembling hands.

His eyes moved across the page.

Then suddenly stopped.

His whole face changed.

He looked up slowly.

The man was already crying.

The boy’s voice was barely above a whisper.

“You didn’t hide money in here.”

The older man shut his eyes.

The boy’s grip tightened on the paper.

“You hid proof.”

A tear slid down the man’s face.

The boy lifted the letter slightly.

“My mother was telling the truth, wasn’t she?”

The man’s lips trembled.

The boy’s next words broke the room in half.

“You’re not the man who ruined my father.”

His voice shook now.

“You’re my father.”
