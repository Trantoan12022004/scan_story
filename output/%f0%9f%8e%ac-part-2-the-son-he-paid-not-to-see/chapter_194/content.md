The woman froze.

The little girl touched the bow in her hair.

“You remember this?”

The boy stared at her.

His lips began to tremble.

“I gave you one.”

The mother covered her mouth.

The girl looked confused.

“When?”

The boy’s breathing quickened.

“You were little.”

His eyes filled with tears.

“You kept pulling it out.”

The woman broke into a sob.

Only one person had ever known that.

Her missing son.

The girl slowly stepped closer.

“Are you my brother?”

The boy looked at her for a long time.

Then something softened in his face.

“You used to call me Benny.”

The girl’s eyes widened.

Her mother collapsed beside him, crying openly now.

“Benjamin…”

He looked back at her.

That name reached somewhere deeper.

His face crumpled.

“Mom?”

She pulled him against her before he could say another word.

He resisted for half a second.

Then his arms wrapped around her.

Tight.

Desperate.

Like three lost years had finally caught up with him.

The little girl dropped beside them and wrapped her arms around both.

Benjamin started sobbing into his mother’s shoulder.

“I thought nobody was coming.”

She held his face against her.

“I came.”

Her voice shook violently.

“Every day, I came looking.”

He pulled back just enough to look at his little sister.

Then at the untouched sandwich still in his hand.

His lips trembled into the smallest smile.

“You found me first.”

The girl began crying too.

She took his hand.

“No.”

She squeezed his fingers.

“You found your way back to us.”
