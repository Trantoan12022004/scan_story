Her first step was small.

Shaking.

Unsteady.

But it was a step.

The man released the boy’s arm so fast it was like he’d been burned.

He just stared.

The girl swayed, her face tight with fear, then took another trembling step across the gravel.

The crutches hung useless at her sides.

The boy looked at her like he couldn’t breathe.

“You’re doing it,” he whispered.

Tears filled the girl’s eyes.

“I’m walking.”

The words came out broken, like she didn’t trust them yet.

The man moved toward her slowly now, his anger gone, replaced by something much more fragile.

“Sweetheart…”

She looked at him, crying openly now.

“I’m walking.”

His face collapsed.

He dropped to his knees in the gravel, staring at her legs, then at the filthy little boy standing beside the tub.

The same boy he had just grabbed.

The same boy he had treated like he didn’t belong there.

The man’s voice came out rough.

“How… how did this happen?”

The boy lowered his eyes.

“I just didn’t want her to be sad anymore.”

That answer hit harder than anything else.

The girl took one more shaky step and reached for the boy’s hand first.

Not her father’s.

His.

The man saw it.

And in that painful second, he understood exactly who had given her hope when everyone else had only given her pity.

The girl’s fingers tightened around the boy’s.

Then she looked at her father through tears and said the words that left him shattered.

“He believed me before you ever did.”
