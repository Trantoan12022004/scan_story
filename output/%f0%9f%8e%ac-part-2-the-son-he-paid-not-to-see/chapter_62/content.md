Emma stared at the photograph until the words blurred through her tears.

“He is yours.”

Oliver stood quietly in front of her, still clutching his toy car.

Emma looked at him again.

Really looked.

The shape of his eyes.

The small dimple near his mouth.

The tiny scar above his eyebrow.

Something inside her cracked open.

“Oliver… how old are you?”

“Eight.”

Her breath caught.

Eight years ago, Emma had woken in a hospital after a terrible accident with months of her memory missing.

Rachel had disappeared shortly afterward.

The family believed she had simply run away.

Emma had believed it too.

Until now.

She sat down slowly on the bench.

“Did your mom ever tell you about me?”

Oliver nodded.

“She said you were my first mom.”

Emma covered her mouth.

A broken sob escaped her.

“What?”

“She said you got hurt.”

His little voice softened.

“And you couldn’t remember me.”

Emma’s hands began trembling violently.

Fragments came back.

A hospital room.

Rachel crying beside her bed.

A baby wrapped in a blue blanket.

A doctor telling her not to force memories that weren’t ready.

Emma looked at Oliver.

“No…”

Oliver stepped closer.

“She said she took care of me until you were better.”

Emma’s eyes filled.

“But she never came back.”

The boy looked down.

“She was scared.”

“Of what?”

Oliver was silent for a moment.

Then he handed her the red toy car.

Something rattled inside.

Emma turned it over.

A tiny folded note had been hidden beneath the plastic base.

She opened it.

Rachel’s handwriting again.

Emma read the first line and started crying before she reached the second.

“I never stole your son. I saved him from the man who caused your accident.”

Emma’s face went pale.

Oliver whispered,

“Mom said if anything happened to her, I should find you.”

Emma pulled him into her arms.

At first, he stayed stiff.

Then his little arms slowly wrapped around her neck.

She held him tighter.

Eight years of missing memories seemed to collapse into that one embrace.

Then Oliver whispered against her shoulder,

“He found us yesterday.”

Emma’s blood went cold.

She pulled back.

“Who?”

Oliver looked past her toward the walking path.

His face suddenly lost all color.

“The man Mom was hiding me from.”

Emma turned.

A man stood near the fountain.

Watching them.

And in that instant, she finally remembered his face.
