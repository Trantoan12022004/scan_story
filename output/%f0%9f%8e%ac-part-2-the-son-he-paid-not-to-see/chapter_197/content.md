The older woman stared at her.

“You’re lying.”

The bride shook her head.

“I wish I were.”

The older man moved closer.

“What was your grandmother’s name?”

The bride answered softly.

“Eleanor Vale.”

His face collapsed.

The older woman turned toward him.

“Why do you know that name?”

He couldn’t answer.

The bride watched him carefully.

“Because he knew my grandmother.”

Silence spread through the ballroom.

The younger man in the bowtie finally stepped forward.

“Dad?”

The older man looked at his son, then at the bride.

His voice came out broken.

“Eleanor was my mother.”

The bride froze.

The older woman stared at him.

“What?”

He closed his eyes.

“She had another daughter before she married my father.”

The bride’s breathing changed.

“My mother.”

He nodded.

The room seemed to tilt beneath her.

The older woman shook her head violently.

“No. No, that’s impossible.”

The bride looked at the man.

“You knew?”

Tears filled his eyes.

“I found the records last month.”

Her voice cracked.

“And you said nothing?”

“I was afraid.”

The bride looked around at the family who had treated her like an outsider.

Then back at the woman who had struck her.

The older man whispered,

“This house was never ours alone.”

The bride’s eyes filled.

“No.”

She looked at the woman in silver.

“It was stolen from my mother.”

The older woman stepped back.

The bride’s voice dropped.

“And the woman you just slapped…”

She swallowed hard.

“…is the granddaughter your family erased from the will.”
