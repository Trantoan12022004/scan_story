The groom walked toward the children.

Slowly.

Like moving too quickly might make them disappear.

The older child held out the photograph.

His hands were shaking.

The groom took it.

And stopped breathing.

A woman stood in the picture with both children.

Emily.

His former fiancée.

The woman who disappeared six years earlier.

The woman he had once planned to marry in this exact city.

He had searched for her until his family convinced him she had left willingly.

No goodbye.

No explanation.

Just gone.

The bride had been Emily’s closest friend.

She had been the one who sat beside him through the worst months.

The one who told him Emily had started another life.

The one who eventually became the woman waiting beside him at the altar.

The groom looked at the children again.

The younger one stared at him with frightened eyes.

His eyes.

The same dark grey.

The same small dimple beneath the left cheek.

“How old are you?”

The older child answered.

“Six.”

The groom’s knees nearly weakened.

The younger girl whispered,

“Mom said you’d know us.”

He looked back toward his bride.

She was crying now.

Not loudly.

Just standing beneath the white roses as everything she had built collapsed around her.

The groom lifted the photograph.

“You knew Emily had children.”

The bride shook her head.

“I didn’t know they would come.”

The words slipped out before she could stop them.

Silence.

The groom stared at her.

The children stared too.

His voice turned quiet.

“So you did know.”

She covered her mouth.

The older child reached into his coat again.

This time, he removed a small envelope.

“We were supposed to give you this.”

The groom opened it.

Emily’s handwriting.

He recognized it immediately.

There were only a few lines.

She had tried to contact him years earlier.

Someone answered first.

Someone told her he knew about the pregnancy.

Someone told her he wanted nothing to do with her or the babies.

Someone sent her photographs showing him with another woman.

The groom slowly raised his eyes.

Toward his bride.

The bride’s face collapsed.

“I was scared.”

He stared at her.

“Of what?”

“That you’d go back to her.”

The younger child began crying again.

The groom looked at her.

Then at the woman he had nearly married.

“You let them grow up believing I abandoned them.”

The bride shook her head desperately.

“I thought Emily would move on.”

“She had my children.”

“I didn’t know at first.”

“At first?”

The groom’s voice cracked.

The bride stopped.

Too late again.

She had found out later.

Years later.

Emily contacted her.

Not asking for money.

Not threatening the wedding.

She wanted only one thing.

For the children to know their father.

The bride promised she would arrange it.

She never did.

Then, three days before the wedding, Emily sent one final message.

She was ill.

She couldn’t keep waiting.

If the bride refused to tell him, the children would come themselves.

That was why the bride had been watching the back of the room all morning.

That was why she reacted before anyone else.

That was why her first instinct wasn’t confusion.

It was to have them removed.

The groom looked at the muddy clothes.

The wet shoes.

The exhausted faces.

“They came here alone?”

The bride couldn’t answer.

The older child did.

“Mom put us on the bus.”

His voice broke.

“She said you’d help us.”

The groom covered his mouth.

Then dropped to his knees in front of them.

He did not reach out.

He let them decide.

The younger girl moved first.

She stepped into his arms.

Then her brother followed.

The groom broke completely.

Both children held him while he cried into their muddy shoulders.

Behind them, hundreds of white roses surrounded the bride.

But nobody was looking at her anymore.

The younger girl pulled back slightly.

“Are you really our daddy?”

The groom nodded through tears.

“Yes.”

She touched his face.

Then whispered,

“Mom said you didn’t know.”

That sentence destroyed the last part of him still trying to understand.

He looked over the children’s heads toward the bride.

The woman he had trusted.

The woman who knew.

The woman who almost watched him say vows while his children were somewhere outside believing their father had chosen not to love them.

And in that moment, the wedding ended without anyone needing to say another word.
