Daniel turned toward his mother.

“You knew?”

“No.”

She dropped to her knees.

“No, sweetheart. I swear I didn’t.”

Caleb backed away.

“But he said you chose Daniel.”

Her face crumpled.

“I was told you were dead.”

Caleb slowly opened his coat.

Around his wrist was an old hospital identification band.

The mother recognized the hospital immediately.

So did she recognize the signature printed beneath the discharge authorization.

Her husband’s.

Daniel’s father.

Her breathing stopped.

“No…”

Daniel looked at her.

“Dad signed it?”

She took out her phone with shaking hands.

But Caleb whispered,

“He’s not my dad.”

Silence.

“What?”

“The man who kept me told me your husband paid him.”

Daniel stared at his mother.

“Why would Dad do that?”

She already knew the answer.

Eight years earlier, her husband’s father had created a trust.

The firstborn grandson would inherit controlling ownership of the family company.

Caleb had been born eleven minutes before Daniel.

The mother covered her mouth.

“He wasn’t trying to hide a second child…”

She looked at Daniel.

“He was removing the first heir.”

Caleb’s eyes filled.

“So I was thrown away because I was born first?”

She immediately pulled him toward her.

“No.”

Caleb stiffened.

Then slowly allowed her to hold him.

“You were stolen because grown men cared more about money than their own child.”

Daniel stepped closer.

“Is he really my brother?”

She nodded.

“Your twin.”

Daniel looked at Caleb’s torn shoes.

Then at his own polished ones.

His face changed.

“You had birthdays?”

Caleb shrugged.

“Sometimes.”

Daniel started crying.

He took off his expensive coat and wrapped it around Caleb’s shoulders.

“You can have mine.”

The mother froze at the words.

Because Daniel had no idea he had repeated almost exactly what she once whispered while holding two newborn babies:

“You’ll always share. You’ll always have each other.”

Caleb looked at him.

“Are we going home?”

Their mother wiped her tears.

“Yes.”

Then Daniel asked quietly,

“Is Dad going to be there?”

She looked at both of her sons.

One protected for eight years.

One abandoned for eight years.

And for the first time, there was no fear in her voice.

“No.”

She took one boy’s hand in each of hers.

“Not after what he did to our family.”

Caleb hesitated.

Then squeezed her fingers.

And on a crowded city sidewalk, two identical boys who had spent eight years living completely different lives finally walked away together—

one discovering he had a brother,

and the other discovering he had never been unwanted.
