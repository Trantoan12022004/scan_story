The biker looked down at his vest.

“My father gave me this name.”

The older man’s eyes narrowed.

“Your father?”

Before the biker could answer, the bar door opened.

Three men entered from the rain.

Not soldiers.

Not bodyguards.

Older bikers.

Men with grey beards, worn faces, and the same silver symbol on their rings.

The room went silent.

One of them looked at the patch.

Then at the older man.

“Arthur…”

The biker frowned.

“You know him?”

The man gave a bitter laugh.

“Everyone knew Arthur Dalton.”

The biker’s face changed.

“Dalton?”

Arthur stood for the first time.

“My son was Rex Dalton.”

The biker took a step back.

“That’s impossible.”

“Rex died thirty-two years ago.”

The biker shook his head.

“No. Rex raised me.”

Arthur froze.

The entire bar seemed to disappear around him.

“What did you say?”

The biker swallowed.

“My father’s name was Rex Dalton.”

Arthur walked closer.

For the first time, his calmness cracked.

“Is he alive?”

The biker stared at him.

“He died last winter.”

Arthur closed his eyes.

His hand tightened around the back of a chair.

One of the older men quietly said,

“We buried an empty coffin.”

The biker looked between them.

“What are you talking about?”

Arthur’s voice broke.

“My son disappeared after testifying against men who were using our club to move stolen money.”

“He was told his family would be killed if he came home.”

The biker’s face slowly changed.

Arthur looked at him carefully now.

The eyes.

The jaw.

The small scar beside his mouth.

Then he asked,

“What was your mother’s name?”

The biker answered quietly.

“Elaine.”

Arthur nearly fell back.

One of the men caught his arm.

The biker stared at him.

“Why?”

Arthur reached into his wallet.

Inside was a photograph of a young Rex standing beside a pregnant woman.

Elaine.

The biker stopped breathing.

Arthur turned the photograph over.

Written on the back were five words:

For Dad — meet your grandson.

The biker’s eyes filled.

“No…”

Arthur looked at the man who had just mocked him minutes earlier.

Then at the patch carrying his dead son’s name.

“You’re not wearing Rex Dalton’s name because you stole it.”

His voice cracked.

“You’re wearing it because he was your father.”

The biker slowly sat down.

All the arrogance left him.

Arthur looked at him for a long moment.

Then quietly said,

“I came here ready to bury that name a second time.”

He placed a trembling hand on the biker’s shoulder.

“Instead, I found my grandson.”
