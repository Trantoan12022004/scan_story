Eleanor turned the photograph over again.

Her fingers could barely hold it.

Written beneath the date was one name.

Clara.

The little girl watched her.

“You know her?”

Eleanor closed her eyes.

Clara had been her younger sister.

The person who gave Eleanor the gold watch.

The person who disappeared from her life shortly after that photograph was taken.

Eleanor looked again at the image of herself holding the baby.

She remembered that day.

The crying infant.

Clara standing behind the camera.

The little rented house.

And one sentence Clara kept repeating:

“If anything happens, promise me she won’t be alone.”

Eleanor had promised.

Then everything changed.

Clara vanished.

The baby vanished with her.

And within weeks, Eleanor was given documents saying both had left the country voluntarily.

No address.

No contact.

No explanation.

Eleanor searched until every lead disappeared.

Eventually, the family convinced her Clara wanted to be gone.

But Eleanor never believed it.

The little girl touched the gold watch.

“Mommy kept this hidden.”

Eleanor looked at her.

“Your mother gave this to you?”

The child nodded.

“Last week.”

Eleanor froze.

“Last week?”

The girl’s eyes filled.

“She said she had to go somewhere.”

Eleanor leaned forward.

“Where?”

“I don’t know.”

The girl pulled something else from her pocket.

A folded restaurant napkin.

On it was Eleanor’s full name.

And the address of this exact restaurant.

Eleanor stared at the handwriting.

Clara’s handwriting.

Her sister was alive.

At least recently enough to send the child here.

Eleanor’s voice cracked.

“Did she say anything else?”

The girl nodded.

“She said not to trust the man who comes looking for me.”

Eleanor went still.

At that exact moment, the restaurant door opened.

A well-dressed older man stepped inside.

The little girl saw him.

Her entire body tightened.

She moved closer to Eleanor.

“That’s him.”

Eleanor slowly turned.

The man stopped when he saw the gold watch open on the table.

Then his eyes moved to the child.

And fear crossed his face.

Eleanor recognized him immediately.

He had been the family attorney twelve years earlier.

The same man who brought her the papers claiming Clara had disappeared willingly.

He took one cautious step toward them.

“Eleanor.”

She stood.

The little girl grabbed the back of her dress.

Eleanor looked at the two photographs again.

One showed her holding the baby.

The other carried Clara’s name.

Neither proved whose child the girl truly was.

But one thing was suddenly clear.

Someone had spent twelve years making sure Eleanor never got the chance to ask.

And now the child who had been told Eleanor abandoned her had arrived carrying the one object that could reopen everything.
