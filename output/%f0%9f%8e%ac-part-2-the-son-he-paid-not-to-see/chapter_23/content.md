Emily stared at her mother.

“Who is he?”

The woman could barely speak.

“His name is Noah.”

The boy slowly pulled away.

“You know my name?”

She nodded through tears.

“I gave you that name.”

Emily’s face changed.

“Mom… what does that mean?”

Her mother looked at both children.

“Noah is your brother.”

Emily went completely still.

“But I don’t have a brother.”

Her mother closed her eyes.

“I thought you didn’t.”

She turned back to Noah.

“Eight years ago, they told me my baby boy died in the hospital.”

Noah looked terrified.

“I didn’t die.”

“I know.”

She touched the scar above his eyebrow.

“I remember this. You were born with it.”

Noah’s eyes filled.

“The man I lived with said my real mother didn’t want me.”

Her face collapsed.

“Who told you that?”

Noah pointed toward the end of the alley.

“He makes me sleep here when I don’t bring enough money.”

The mother stood immediately.

“Who?”

Noah hesitated.

Then reached into his torn shirt and pulled out an old photograph.

The woman took it.

Her breathing stopped.

A younger version of herself held newborn Noah in a hospital bed.

Standing beside her was the doctor who had told her the baby died.

Emily looked at the picture.

“Mom… who is that?”

Her mother’s hand started shaking.

“The man who signed your brother’s death certificate.”

Noah looked down the alley.

His face suddenly went pale.

“He’s coming.”

The mother turned.

A man had just stepped around the corner.

She recognized him instantly.

And after eight years of mourning her son, she finally understood—

her baby had never been lost.

He had been taken.
