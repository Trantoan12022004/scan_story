The mother stared at him.

“Your father?”

The boy nodded.

“He brought me here when I was little.”

Her face went pale.

“No. Your father told me you disappeared from the park.”

The boy shook his head.

“I didn’t disappear.”

Emily slowly moved closer.

“What happened?”

The boy looked down at the sandwich in his hands.

“Dad said you had a new baby.”

He glanced at Emily.

“He said there wasn’t room for me anymore.”

The mother covered her mouth.

For six years, she had blamed herself for looking away for only a few seconds that afternoon.

There had been searches.

Posters.

Police interviews.

Birthdays spent beside an empty bedroom.

And eventually a funeral with no body.

She touched his cheek again.

“I never stopped looking for you.”

The boy began crying.

“Then why didn’t Dad bring me home?”

She had no answer.

Until Emily quietly said,

“Mom… Dad knew we were coming here today.”

The woman turned toward her.

“What?”

“He told me not to walk down this alley.”

Silence.

The mother slowly reached for her phone.

Then she remembered something.

Her husband had called only minutes earlier asking exactly where they were.

The boy suddenly grabbed her coat.

“He’ll be angry if he sees me.”

She pulled him against her immediately.

“No.”

Her voice changed.

“He will never take you away from me again.”

Then headlights appeared at the entrance to the alley.

A familiar black car stopped.

Emily whispered,

“That’s Daddy.”

The boy began shaking.

The mother stood in front of both children.

Her husband stepped from the car.

He saw the boy.

And instead of looking shocked…

he closed his eyes.

That was all she needed.

“You knew.”

He said nothing.

She began crying again, but this time from rage.

“You let me bury my own son.”

His voice cracked.

“I thought I was protecting our family.”

“From what?”

He looked at the boy.

Then at Emily.

And finally confessed.

“He isn’t my biological son.”

The mother froze.

The boy’s face collapsed.

But she immediately knelt beside him.

“Look at me.”

He couldn’t.

She lifted his chin.

“You are my son.”

She took Emily’s hand and placed it in his.

“And you are her brother.”

Then she looked back at the man who had stolen six years from them.

“You were so afraid of raising another man’s child…”

Her voice broke.

“…that you threw away mine.”

She took both children by the hands and walked out of the alley.

And for the first time since he was two years old, the boy wasn’t wondering whether anyone was coming back for him.

His mother already had.
