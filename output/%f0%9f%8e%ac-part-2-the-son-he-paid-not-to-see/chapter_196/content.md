The older woman pulled out her phone.

Her face changed immediately.

“No…”

Another notification appeared.

Then another.

The older man moved beside her.

“What happened?”

She stared at the screen.

“My cards are frozen.”

The bride said nothing.

“My accounts…”

Her breathing quickened.

“They’re gone.”

The bride’s new husband finally stepped forward.

“What is happening?”

The older woman pointed at the bride.

“She’s lying. She has no authority over anything.”

The bride slowly removed her veil.

“My father founded this company.”

The ballroom went silent.

The older woman went pale.

“That’s impossible.”

“No.”

The bride’s lips trembled.

“What was impossible was growing up watching you convince him I wanted nothing to do with him.”

The older woman froze.

The bride continued.

“You intercepted his letters.”

The older man stared at the woman beside him.

“You told us she refused every invitation.”

The older woman shook her head.

“She’s making this up.”

The bride reached into her bouquet and removed a small envelope.

“My father found the originals before he died.”

The older woman’s knees weakened.

The bride’s voice cracked for the first time.

“He knew what you did.”

The older woman whispered,

“What did he leave you?”

The bride looked around at the ballroom.

At the chandeliers.

At the terrified faces.

Then back at the woman who had just slapped her.

“Everything.”

The older woman stopped breathing.

The bride stepped closer and delivered the truth she had waited years to say.

“You didn’t slap some poor girl who married into your family.”

Her eyes filled with tears.

“You slapped the woman who owns the family company.”
