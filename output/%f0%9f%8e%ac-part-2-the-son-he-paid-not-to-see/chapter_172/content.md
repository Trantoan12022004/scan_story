No one laughed now.

The older man stared at the open locker.

“That combination…”

The boy slowly pulled the door wider.

“My father chose it.”

The man’s face drained of color.

“That’s impossible.”

The boy reached inside.

There was no million dollars.

Only an old envelope.

The older man lunged forward.

“Don’t touch that.”

The crowd went silent.

The boy held the envelope against his chest.

“My dad worked for you.”

The man swallowed.

“He was a thief.”

The boy’s jaw tightened.

“That’s what you told everyone.”

The older man glanced nervously at the watching guests.

The boy opened the envelope.

Inside was a photograph and a handwritten letter.

His hands began to shake.

“My father spent his last years trying to prove he never stole from you.”

The older man whispered,

“Put that down.”

The boy looked at the photograph.

Then at the man.

“You knew he was innocent.”

“No.”

“You hid the proof in the locker he built for you.”

The older man stepped back.

The boy’s eyes filled with tears.

“My dad died believing nobody would ever know the truth.”

The man couldn’t answer.

The boy unfolded the letter.

His voice broke as he read the name written at the bottom.

Then he looked up.

“My father wasn’t just your employee, was he?”

The older man’s eyes filled instantly.

The boy held up the photograph.

It showed them decades younger, standing together like brothers.

The man whispered,

“He was my son.”

The boy went completely still.

Then his trembling lips parted.

“So you’re my grandfather.”
