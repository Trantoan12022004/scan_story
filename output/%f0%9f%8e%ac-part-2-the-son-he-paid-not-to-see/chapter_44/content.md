Nathan stared at the woman in blue.

“What is she talking about?”

Grace stepped closer.

“For months after the girls were born, I wrote to you.”

Nathan shook his head.

“I never got anything.”

“I sent pictures.”

“None.”

“Birth certificates.”

“None.”

Grace looked at the woman.

“You had access to his phone.”

Her face collapsed.

Nathan whispered,

“Why?”

She started crying.

“Because you were going back to her.”

Nathan looked sick.

“You told me Grace had lost the babies.”

“I thought if you believed there was nothing left—”

“Nothing left?”

One of the girls began crying.

Nathan immediately lowered his voice.

The youngest peeked from behind Grace.

“Are you our daddy?”

Nathan looked at her.

For five years, he had mourned daughters he believed had never taken a breath.

Now three little girls were standing a few feet away, waiting for him to answer.

He slowly crouched.

“I think…”

His voice broke.

“I think I was supposed to be.”

Grace covered her mouth.

The girl holding the stuffed bunny stepped forward.

“Mommy said you didn’t know where we were.”

Nathan looked at Grace.

“She always defended you.”

That hurt worse than anything.

The woman in blue whispered,

“I’m sorry.”

Grace turned toward her.

“No.”

Her voice stayed calm.

“You’re sorry because he found out.”

Nathan stood.

“What else did you hide?”

The woman looked away.

Then Grace’s oldest daughter reached into her tiny suitcase.

“Mommy said this was for Daddy.”

She handed Nathan a thick envelope.

His name was written across it.

Inside were dozens of unopened birthday cards.

One for every birthday he had missed.

Nathan’s hands began shaking.

Then one final document slipped out.

A hospital record.

Grace saw the signature.

Her face changed.

“That’s not mine.”

Nathan looked closer.

Someone had signed authorization to transfer the newborn girls into private temporary guardianship days after their birth.

The signature belonged to the woman in blue.

Nathan looked at her.

“You didn’t just hide them from me.”

She began backing away.

“You helped take them from Grace.”

Grace grabbed all three daughters closer.

The woman broke down.

“Your father paid me.”

Nathan froze.

“My father?”

“He said three daughters would complicate the inheritance.”

Silence.

Grace whispered,

“So this was never about jealousy.”

The woman shook her head.

“It started that way.”

Then she looked at Nathan.

“But your father turned it into something worse.”

Nathan stared at his daughters.

Five stolen years.

Five birthdays.

Five Christmas mornings.

Five years of Grace believing he had chosen to disappear.

He slowly knelt again.

“I don’t expect you to call me Dad.”

The youngest girl walked toward him anyway.

She held out the stuffed bunny.

“You can hold her first.”

Nathan took it with trembling hands.

Then she whispered,

“So you won’t be scared when you hold me.”

That was the moment he finally broke.
