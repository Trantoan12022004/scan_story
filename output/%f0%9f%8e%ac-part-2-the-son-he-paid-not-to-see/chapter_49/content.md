The man grabbed the edge of the piano.

“My son never had a child.”

Noah looked at Claire.

“That’s what she wanted you to believe.”

Claire shook her head.

“He’s lying.”

Noah opened his cloth bag.

Inside were photographs.

His mother beside a young man.

The man’s son, Adrian.

In one photograph, Adrian was placing the same gold ring on her finger.

The wealthy man’s hands began shaking.

“They were married?”

Noah nodded.

“Secretly.”

Claire whispered,

“Adrian was confused.”

“No,” Noah said. “He loved her.”

The man turned to his wife.

“You knew?”

Claire’s breathing became uneven.

She finally admitted that Adrian had married a poor music teacher named Hannah.

And Hannah had written the melody everyone believed Adrian composed.

Then Hannah became pregnant.

The family patriarch’s will contained one condition Claire had never expected:

If Adrian had a child, that child would eventually inherit controlling ownership of the estate.

The man stared at her.

“So when Adrian died…”

Claire closed her eyes.

“I told everyone Hannah lost the baby.”

Noah’s voice cracked.

“But I didn’t die.”

Silence.

The man looked at the thin child sitting in front of a piano worth more than everything Noah had ever owned.

“What happened to your mother?”

Noah lowered his eyes.

“She kept trying to contact you.”

Claire’s face changed.

Noah pulled out a bundle of unopened letters.

Every one was addressed to the mansion.

Every one had been returned.

“My mother thought you hated us.”

The man began crying.

“I never knew you existed.”

Noah looked at him.

“She waited anyway.”

“What happened to her?”

“Cancer.”

The man closed his eyes.

“She died last winter.”

Noah placed the family ring on the piano.

“Before she died, she told me to find this house.”

He looked around at the chandeliers and wealthy guests.

“She said if they laughed at my clothes, I shouldn’t leave.”

The man’s lips trembled.

“Why?”

Noah touched one piano key.

“Because I should play her song.”

“And someone would remember?”

Noah nodded.

The man slowly turned toward Claire.

“You let my grandson live on the street?”

“I was protecting what belonged to our family.”

He looked at Noah.

Then back at her.

“He is our family.”

Claire went silent.

The man knelt beside the boy.

Noah instinctively pulled away.

That tiny movement broke him more than anything else.

“I’m not going to hurt you.”

Noah studied his face.

“Are you my grandfather?”

The man nodded through tears.

“Yes.”

Noah looked down at his torn clothes.

“Do I have to leave after tonight?”

The man’s face collapsed.

He carefully picked up the gold ring and placed it back into Noah’s palm.

“No.”

His voice broke.

“You’re the person we should have been waiting for.”

Noah stared at him.

Then quietly asked,

“Can I finish Mom’s song?”

The man sat beside him.

“Please.”

Noah placed his hands on the keys again.

And this time, no one in the ballroom laughed.

Because the homeless child they had mocked minutes earlier was playing the song his mother had used to lead him home.
