The man couldn’t take his eyes off the photograph.

Seventeen years earlier, her name had been Elena.

She had been the only person who ever spoke to him like his family name meant nothing.

They met when he was still trying to prove himself to a father who measured love in success.

Elena taught music.

He pretended he hated lessons.

Then stayed after every one.

Together, they wrote that unfinished melody.

The first half was his.

The second was hers.

They never performed it.

Never recorded it.

Never wrote the full piece down.

It belonged only to them.

Then Elena vanished.

One week before he planned to leave his family business and marry her.

Her apartment was empty.

Her phone disconnected.

The only message he received said she had changed her mind and wanted no contact.

He believed it because the message included details only Elena should have known.

Eventually, he stopped searching.

Not because he stopped loving her.

Because everyone around him convinced him that continuing was humiliation.

The woman in white had been one of those people.

She had comforted him.

Helped him move on.

Years later, she became his wife.

Now she stood across the ballroom with her eyes closed.

The boy touched the Polaroid.

“She never stopped playing your half.”

The man looked at him.

“Where is she?”

The boy didn’t answer immediately.

That silence told him enough.

His eyes filled again.

“When?”

“Last winter.”

The man lowered his head.

For seventeen years, he had imagined Elena alive somewhere, choosing not to return.

Now even that painful hope was gone.

The boy continued quietly.

“She told me the song was how I’d know if you remembered her.”

The man looked up.

The boy nodded toward the piano.

“She said if you asked me to finish it, I could give you the picture.”

The man picked up the Polaroid with shaking fingers.

The baby in Elena’s lap was wrapped in a pale blanket.

Only part of the face was visible.

He looked at the boy again.

Same dark eyes.

Same small curve in the left eyebrow Elena used to tease him about.

But he didn’t ask.

He was afraid of the answer.

The woman in white finally spoke from across the room.

“She shouldn’t have come back.”

Everyone turned.

The man stared at her.

The boy did too.

Her face changed the instant she realized what she had said.

The man’s grief hardened.

“You knew?”

She shook her head.

But too late.

The boy reached into his jacket again.

Not another object.

Just a folded letter.

He placed it beside the photograph.

The handwriting made the man’s knees weaken.

Elena’s.

He opened it.

There were no accusations.

No long explanation.

Only a few lines.

She had tried to return.

She had tried to contact him.

Someone intercepted every attempt.

Then convinced her that he had married willingly and never wanted to know about the child.

The man stopped reading.

His eyes moved toward the woman in white.

She began crying.

He looked back at the boy.

“How old are you?”

“Sixteen.”

The man stared at the baby in the photograph.

Then at the teenager standing beside the piano.

The boy didn’t say father.

The man didn’t say son.

Neither needed to.

The melody had already said what seventeen years of lies had hidden.

The man slowly sat beside him on the piano bench.

For several seconds, neither moved.

Then the boy placed one hand on the keys.

He played the first half.

The man’s hand trembled above the second.

And for the first time since Elena disappeared, the song was no longer unfinished.
