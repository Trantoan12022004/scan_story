“Are you ready to come home now?”

The entire hall froze.

The sister stared at him.

The groom whipped around in shock.

The bride laughed nervously into the silence.

“What is this supposed to mean?”

The man in black didn’t even look at her.

His eyes stayed on the sister’s husband in the cream suit.

Then, for the first time, he spoke to the room.

“He’s not penniless.”

The bride’s face tightened.

The groom shook his head.

“Sir, please—”

But the man in black cut him off.

“He’s my son.”

The words hit the hall like a collision.

The bride’s microphone slipped in her hand.

The groom looked at the man in cream, then back at his boss, unable to process what he was hearing.

The sister’s husband finally stood.

Quietly.

Calmly.

Like he had let them talk long enough.

The bride’s voice cracked.

“No. No, that’s impossible.”

The man in cream looked at her with something sadder than anger.

“You mocked me because I let you believe I had nothing.”

The sister covered her mouth, crying harder now.

The groom took a stumbling step back.

“You said he was unemployed.”

The bride’s eyes flew to him.

“I— I thought—”

“You never asked,” the man in cream said.

The father in black moved closer, his voice sharp now.

“You told your employer you were marrying into a respectable family.”

The groom went still.

Then came the line that shattered him.

“You forgot to mention your bride has been publicly humiliating my daughter-in-law all night.”

The room turned toward the crying sister.

The bride’s whole body stiffened.

The sister looked up slowly at her husband.

He walked to her first.

Not to the bride.

Not to the crowd.

To her.

He held out his hand.

“Come with me.”

She took it with shaking fingers.

The bride’s voice broke into panic.

“Wait— who are you?”

The man in cream looked back one last time.

Then he gave her the answer that emptied every face in the room.

“The man you called poor.”

He tightened his hand around his wife’s.

“And the owner of the company your husband has been begging to inherit.”
