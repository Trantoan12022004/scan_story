The teenager stared at the judge.

For several seconds, he couldn’t speak.

His little brother lifted his tearful face.

“What does that mean?”

The judge closed his eyes.

When he opened them again, the professional distance in his face was gone.

“It means I cannot continue hearing this case.”

The lawyer looked stunned.

The judge’s voice trembled despite his effort to control it.

“If your father was Marcus Bennett… then I am your grandfather.”

The teenager instinctively pulled his brother closer.

“No.”

The word came out defensive.

Almost angry.

“My dad said his family wanted nothing to do with us.”

Pain crossed the judge’s face.

“He believed that?”

The teenager’s lips trembled.

“He died believing it.”

The woman in the plum coat suddenly stood.

Everyone turned.

She was crying openly now.

“I tried to find you.”

The teenager stared at her.

“Who are you?”

She walked closer but stopped several feet away, careful not to frighten the boys.

“I’m Marcus’s sister.”

The teenager’s breathing changed.

His little brother tightened his grip.

The woman pressed one trembling hand against her chest.

“Your aunt.”

The older boy shook his head.

“No. We don’t have anyone.”

“You do.”

Her voice broke.

“You always did.”

He looked at the judge again.

“Then where were you?”

That question hit harder than anything else said that morning.

The judge lowered his eyes.

“We were told your mother took you both away after Marcus died.”

The teenager’s face went pale.

“Our mother died before him.”

The woman covered her mouth.

The judge looked toward the lawyer.

Then back at the boys.

“Who told you we didn’t want you?”

The teenager swallowed.

“The foster agency said nobody from Dad’s family responded.”

The courtroom went silent again.

The woman in plum shook her head immediately.

“That’s impossible.”

She reached into her bag with trembling hands and pulled out a worn envelope.

“I wrote every month for almost two years.”

The teenager stared at it.

“I never got anything.”

“I know.”

Her voice cracked.

“They kept coming back.”

She held up the envelope.

On the front was his name.

His real name.

Written in faded ink years earlier.

The teenager’s knees seemed to weaken.

His little brother whispered,

“We had a family?”

That destroyed him.

The teenager bent down and wrapped both arms around the child.

For the first time, he stopped trying to be sixteen going on forty.

He just cried.

“Yes,” their aunt whispered through tears. “You had a family.”

The judge stood slowly behind the bench.

He could not approach them as their judge anymore.

So he stayed where he was.

A grandfather only a few feet away from two boys who had spent years believing nobody wanted them.

The teenager looked up at him.

His face was wet with tears.

“You really would’ve taken us?”

The judge’s jaw tightened.

“Son…”

His voice broke completely.

“We would have come the first day we knew you were alone.”

The younger boy started crying harder.

And the teenager pulled him close, realizing the most painful truth of all.

He had spent years fighting to keep the only family he thought he had…

while another family had been fighting to find them.
