The groom stared at Rebecca.

“You told me you’d never met her.”

Rebecca’s eyes filled.

“I hadn’t… not really.”

He turned the photograph over.

There was handwriting on the back.

His late wife’s handwriting.

If anything happens to me, don’t let Rebecca near my daughter.

His hands started shaking.

Rebecca whispered,

“That isn’t what you think.”

His daughter pressed herself against his side.

“Daddy, she had more pictures.”

He looked at Rebecca.

“How long did you know my wife?”

Rebecca said nothing.

That silence was enough.

He stepped closer.

“How long?”

“Since college.”

His face collapsed.

“You were friends?”

Rebecca shook her head.

“No.”

“Then what?”

Her voice broke.

“She knew something about me.”

“What?”

Rebecca looked toward the little girl.

The groom immediately stepped in front of his daughter.

“Don’t look at her. Look at me.”

Rebecca started crying.

“Your wife found out I’d been using another name.”

He stared at her.

“What name?”

She swallowed.

Then his daughter pulled one final folded paper from her purse.

“I found this with the pictures.”

The groom opened it.

A birth certificate.

Rebecca’s photograph was attached.

But the surname wasn’t Rebecca’s.

It was his late wife’s.

He slowly raised his eyes.

Rebecca whispered,

“She wasn’t just your wife.”

His voice dropped.

“What are you saying?”

Rebecca looked at the little girl.

Then back at him.

“She was my—”
