The father crouched beside his daughter.

“Who?”

The little girl looked toward her mother.

Her mother immediately shook her head.

“Sweetheart, don’t.”

That was enough.

The father stood.

“Let her speak.”

His daughter’s entire body trembled.

She looked toward the empty chair at the head of the table.

Grandpa’s chair.

The father followed her eyes.

His expression changed.

His own father was supposed to attend dinner that night.

He had canceled only an hour earlier.

The girl whispered,

“Grandpa brought it.”

Silence.

The mother closed her eyes.

The father stared at her.

“My father?”

She began crying.

“He came before dinner.”

The little girl nodded.

She had been playing beneath the staircase when her grandfather entered through the side door.

He didn’t greet anyone.

He went straight into the dining room.

The mother followed him.

Their daughter followed too.

Quietly.

From the doorway, she watched her grandfather place two identical cups on the table.

One in front of his son.

One beside it.

Then he said something she didn’t understand.

But she remembered her mother’s answer.

“No. I won’t do that.”

The father stared at his wife.

“You knew?”

Tears spilled down her cheeks.

“I knew he wanted something to happen tonight. I didn’t know exactly what.”

“Then why didn’t you tell me?”

Her voice broke.

“Because he said if I warned you, he’d take her.”

The father looked at their daughter.

His anger vanished beneath something colder.

Fear.

His wife continued.

She had tried to remove both cups before dinner.

Then guests arrived.

The table was rearranged.

Staff moved place settings.

And somehow one cup returned.

She didn’t know which one.

That was why she panicked when their daughter screamed.

Not because she wanted her husband to drink.

Because she realized she had lost track of the cup she feared.

The father looked toward the second untouched cup.

“Then why were there two?”

His wife hesitated.

That hesitation brought his suspicion back instantly.

“Tell me.”

She wiped her face.

“Your father said one was for you.”

The father waited.

“And the other?”

Her eyes dropped.

“For me.”

He stared at her.

The little girl moved closer to him.

The father slowly understood.

His wife had not been helping his father target him.

She had been trapped inside the same threat.

Two cups.

Two spouses.

One grandfather who expected neither of them to be sitting at that table by morning.

Then the little girl spoke again.

“But Grandma knew.”

Both parents turned toward her.

“What?”

Their daughter pointed toward the doorway.

“Grandma came after Grandpa left.”

The father’s face tightened.

His mother had died three years earlier.

Or at least—

that was what he had been told.

The girl saw their expressions and started crying harder.

“No, Daddy. Grandma from the picture.”

His wife went pale.

The father stopped breathing.

There was only one photograph in the house their daughter called Grandma.

A young woman whose portrait sat inside his father’s locked study.

His biological mother.

The woman he had been told abandoned him as an infant.

The girl reached into the pocket of her pink dress.

She pulled out a folded napkin.

Her father opened it.

One sentence was written across it in hurried handwriting.

Do not let either of them drink.

His hands began shaking.

He knew that handwriting.

Not personally.

But from the old birthday cards his father claimed had never been sent.

His mother’s.

The wife stared at the note.

“She was here?”

Their daughter nodded.

“She kissed my hair.”

The father slowly sat down.

His daughter had just saved both of her parents.

But someone else had been trying to save them first.

A woman he had spent his entire life believing chose to disappear.

And somewhere outside that elegant dining room, his supposedly absent mother was alive—

and knew exactly why his father wanted two cups on that table.
