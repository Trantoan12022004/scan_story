The man slowly turned toward the blonde woman.

She shook her head.

“No.”

Anna stood carefully, still dripping water onto the floor, one arm around her daughter.

“She told me you wanted me gone.”

The man looked back at Anna.

“What?”

Six years earlier, Anna had been pregnant.

They were engaged.

They had argued about his family, his work, and the pressure surrounding their wedding.

Nothing unusual.

Nothing worth disappearing over.

Then one morning, Anna received messages from his number.

Cruel messages.

Clear messages.

He wanted the engagement over.

He wanted nothing to do with the baby.

He would use his family’s lawyers if she tried to contact him again.

Anna left devastated.

The man stared at her.

“I never sent those.”

“I know that now.”

His eyes moved toward his fiancée.

Anna continued.

“Three days after I left, she came to see me.”

The blonde woman’s lips trembled.

“She said you had already moved on.”

The man’s face twisted with disbelief.

Anna swallowed hard.

“She showed me pictures.”

His fiancée whispered, “Anna…”

“Pictures of the two of you.”

The room went still.

The man remembered that time.

The blonde woman had been his family’s public-relations adviser.

She had been around constantly after Anna vanished.

Comforting him.

Helping organize searches.

Telling him Anna had probably left voluntarily.

Eventually becoming the person he trusted most.

Anna looked at him.

“She told me if I came back, your family would take my baby.”

The little girl held tighter to her mother.

The man looked down at her.

His voice almost disappeared.

“How old is she?”

Anna’s eyes filled.

“Five.”

That answer broke him.

He stepped backward as if he needed something solid beneath him.

Five.

The child standing barefoot beside the wet maid.

The child who had run to him for help without knowing who he was.

He looked at her face again.

Then at Anna.

“No…”

The little girl became frightened.

“Mama?”

Anna crouched and held her gently.

The man’s eyes were overflowing now.

“Is she…”

Anna nodded.

“She’s your daughter.”

The little girl stared at him.

He covered his mouth.

For six years he had mourned one woman.

For five of those years, somewhere beyond the walls of his enormous house, his daughter had been growing up without him.

The blonde woman stepped toward him.

“I can explain.”

He looked at her.

For the first time, there was no anger in his face.

Only devastation.

“You helped me search for her.”

She stopped.

“You watched me fall apart.”

Her composure finally cracked.

“I loved you.”

Anna looked at her in disbelief.

“So you erased me?”

The blonde woman shook her head desperately.

“I thought once you left, he would eventually—”

She stopped herself.

Too late.

The man understood.

This had never been an accident.

Not the messages.

Not the photographs.

Not Anna vanishing.

And perhaps not the humiliation he had just walked in on.

He looked at Anna’s soaked clothes.

“You recognized her.”

The blonde woman said nothing.

That silence answered him.

She had known exactly who the maid was.

Anna had returned weeks earlier under another surname because she finally wanted to tell him the truth.

Before she found the courage, his fiancée recognized her.

And began trying to drive her out of the house.

The little girl suddenly looked at the man.

Her voice was tiny.

“Are you my daddy?”

He froze.

Every emotion left his face except pain.

He slowly lowered himself to his knees.

But he didn’t reach for her.

He let her choose.

“I think I am.”

The girl looked at Anna.

Anna nodded through tears.

The child turned back.

Then took one cautious step toward him.

His chin trembled.

She touched the sleeve of his suit with the same small hand that had grabbed him in the hallway.

And whispered,

“Then I found you.”

That destroyed him.

He pulled her into his arms only after she leaned into him first.

Anna covered her mouth, crying.

Behind them, the woman in champagne stood completely alone.

Six years of lies had collapsed because a frightened little girl had run down a hallway looking for someone to save her mother.

She had no idea she was running toward her father.
