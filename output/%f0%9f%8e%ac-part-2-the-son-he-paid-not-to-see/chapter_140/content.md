The man tapped the second phone.

The woman immediately moved toward him.

“Don’t.”

He stopped.

Not because she had convinced him.

Because that single word confirmed there was something worth seeing.

The maid stood behind him, wiping tears from her cheeks.

“What happened in the elevator?”

The bronze-dressed woman shook her head.

“Nothing.”

The man pressed play.

The video had no clear audio at first.

Only the elevator doors closing.

The woman stepped inside.

She wasn’t alone.

A distinguished older man entered behind her.

The maid recognized him.

So did everyone else in the family.

The owner of the estate.

The bronze-dressed woman’s husband.

The man in olive watched carefully.

Inside the elevator, she handed him an envelope.

He opened it.

His expression hardened.

Then she pointed toward something outside the camera’s view.

The older man shook his head.

She became agitated.

Even without hearing every word, one thing was obvious.

They were arguing.

Then the audio cleared for a few seconds.

The woman’s voice came through.

“If she talks, everything comes out.”

The man paused the recording.

The hallway became silent.

The maid slowly looked up.

“Me?”

The bronze-dressed woman closed her eyes.

The man in olive looked at her.

“You didn’t plant the necklace for insurance.”

No answer.

“You planted it to get her fired.”

The maid stared at the woman.

Then something clicked in her memory.

A week earlier, she had entered the library while cleaning.

The bronze-dressed woman had been there with her husband.

They were arguing over documents.

The maid apologized and left immediately.

She never read anything.

Never asked questions.

But apparently they believed she had seen enough.

So the necklace was planted.

If she were accused of theft, she would be dismissed instantly.

Discredited.

Maybe frightened enough never to return.

The maid’s voice shook.

“I didn’t see anything.”

The woman finally looked at her.

“But you heard him.”

The maid froze.

A fragment came back.

The husband’s voice from behind the library doors.

A woman’s name.

Then something about a child.

The maid had forgotten it almost immediately.

Until now.

The man in olive lowered the phone.

“What name?”

The maid swallowed.

“Anna.”

The bronze-dressed woman visibly flinched.

That reaction was small.

But everyone saw it.

The man stared at her.

His calm expression finally cracked.

Because Anna was his sister.

She had disappeared eight years earlier.

The family had always said she left willingly after an argument.

No one had heard from her since.

The maid looked at him, confused by his reaction.

“I only heard the name.”

He turned back to the elevator footage.

This time he watched the older man’s face.

The envelope.

The panic.

The words about making sure the maid never talked.

Then he understood why the necklace had been planted.

This had never been about diamonds.

The maid had accidentally heard a secret connected to the disappearance of his sister.

And the woman who tried to frame her had just revealed, through her own fear, that Anna’s disappearance had never been as simple as the family claimed.
