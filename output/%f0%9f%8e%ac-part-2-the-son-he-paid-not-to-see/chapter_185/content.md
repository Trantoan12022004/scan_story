The bearded man looked at the girl like he’d forgotten how to breathe.

“Rose?”

She nodded once, terrified.

The young man’s jaw tightened.

“She’s coming with me.”

The biker slowly stood.

“No.”

Every nearby biker rose with him.

The young man glanced around, but his calm was starting to crack.

“You don’t understand.”

The bearded man never took his eyes off the girl.

“How do you know Rose?”

The girl swallowed hard.

“She’s my mom.”

The words hit him like a punch.

His hand gripped the edge of the table.

“She said if I got lost, I should find the wolf.”

His eyes filled instantly.

“She said the wolf was her father.”

The diner fell silent.

The young man stepped forward.

“She’s confused.”

The girl shook her head violently.

“I’m not confused.”

Then she reached into her pocket and pulled out a tiny silver pendant.

The biker stared at it.

A wolf engraved on one side.

A faded R on the other.

He knew it.

He had given it to Rose on her sixteenth birthday.

His voice broke.

“Where is she?”

The girl’s lip trembled.

“She couldn’t come.”

The young man cut in sharply.

“That’s enough.”

The girl flinched at the sound of his voice.

Then looked back at the biker.

“Mom said if he got to me first, he’d lie.”

The man in denim went pale.

The bearded biker’s expression changed.

Cold now.

Dangerously cold.

The girl’s next words came out in a whisper.

“She said he works for the people who took her.”

The biker looked at the young man.

The entire diner was on its feet now.

Then the girl said the sentence that shattered whatever hope he had left.

“She made me memorize one last thing.”

His voice was rough.

“What thing?”

The girl’s mouth trembled.

“Tell Grandpa the men who stole me twenty years ago… came back for my daughter.”
