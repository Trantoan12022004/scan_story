The man couldn’t answer.

He simply stared at the child’s face.

Her eyes.

Her nose.

Even the tiny crease that appeared beside her mouth when she was frightened.

All of it was painfully familiar.

“Where is Elena?”

The girl pointed toward an apartment above the narrow street.

“Home.”

His legs nearly failed him.

He followed her up the stone stairs.

At the top, the girl knocked.

“Mom?”

The door opened.

And the dead came back to life.

Elena stood there.

Older.

Thinner.

A faint scar along her temple.

But alive.

The man whispered her name.

“Elena…”

Her hand flew to her mouth.

“James?”

Neither moved.

Then James began crying.

“I buried you.”

Elena shook her head.

“You buried the woman they put in my place.”

His face changed.

“What?”

Elena pulled him inside.

From a locked drawer, she removed a folder filled with hospital records, photographs, and letters.

Letters addressed to James.

Every one returned unopened.

“I wrote to you for years.”

“I never saw these.”

“I know.”

She handed him one final envelope.

It carried a familiar handwriting.

His father’s.

James slowly opened it.

Inside was a payment record to the private clinic where Elena had given birth.

And one handwritten instruction:

Keep the mother and child away until James signs control of the company.

James stared at the page.

“My father did this?”

Elena nodded through tears.

“He told me you chose the company over us.”

James shook his head desperately.

“He told me you and the baby died during surgery.”

The little girl stood silently between them.

Seven years of grief had been built from two different lies.

James looked at his daughter.

“What’s your name?”

The girl answered softly.

“Sophie.”

He closed his eyes.

That was the name he and Elena had chosen before the pregnancy was even visible.

Sophie stepped closer.

“Mom said you might not want me.”

James immediately dropped to his knees.

“No.”

His voice broke completely.

“I wanted you before I ever saw your face.”

Sophie’s eyes filled.

“Then why didn’t you come?”

James looked at Elena.

Because there was no answer a seven-year-old should ever have to carry.

So Elena knelt beside them.

“He didn’t know where we were.”

Sophie looked at him for a long moment.

Then held out the old Polaroid.

James thought she was returning it.

Instead, she placed it carefully back into his coat pocket.

“You shouldn’t lose Mommy again.”

James covered his face and cried.

Then Sophie wrapped her arms around his neck.

And after seven years of being told his family was buried beneath a gravestone, James finally held the daughter whose photograph had been waiting to fall into his path.
