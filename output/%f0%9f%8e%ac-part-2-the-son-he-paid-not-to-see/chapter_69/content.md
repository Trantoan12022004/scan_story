The father slowly stood.

“Play it.”

His daughter touched the screen.

The video began several seconds earlier.

The stroller was still at the top of the staircase.

The maid was adjusting the baby’s blanket when his wife stepped close enough that nobody else could hear.

But the phone had.

Her voice came softly through the speaker.

“You should have taken the money and disappeared.”

The husband’s face changed.

The maid lowered her eyes.

On the recording, she answered quietly,

“I don’t want your money.”

His wife’s voice became colder.

“Then stop trying to tell Adrian who you are.”

The father froze.

He looked at the maid.

“What does that mean?”

She couldn’t answer.

The recording continued.

His wife leaned closer to her.

“He has a family. He doesn’t need you coming here claiming his father had another daughter.”

The father stopped breathing.

His daughter looked up at him.

The maid began to cry.

The video ended.

Silence filled the enormous foyer.

The husband stared at her.

“My father?”

The maid wiped her face with a shaking hand.

“My mother worked for your family when she was young.”

His expression slowly collapsed.

“She told me your father was mine too.”

“No…”

“I didn’t believe her at first.”

The maid reached inside the pocket of her apron.

She pulled out an old folded photograph.

Her hand shook as she gave it to him.

The picture showed his father decades younger.

Standing beside a woman.

Holding a newborn baby.

On the back was a date.

And one handwritten sentence.

“Tell Adrian about his sister when they’re old enough.”

The husband’s eyes filled immediately.

He looked from the photograph to the maid’s face.

And suddenly he saw it.

The same eyes.

The same small crease beside the mouth his father had when he tried not to smile.

“You’re…”

She nodded, crying.

“Your sister.”

His daughter covered her mouth.

The wife stepped backward.

The husband slowly turned toward her.

“You knew?”

Her lips trembled.

She said nothing.

“You knew she was my sister… and you made her work in my house without telling me?”

“I was protecting you.”

“From my own family?”

His voice broke.

The wife’s eyes filled.

“She was going to change everything.”

The husband looked at the maid sitting injured beside the stroller.

Then at the woman he had married.

“You tried to destroy her because she wanted me to know her name.”

The police officer stepped closer.

The wife lowered her eyes.

The maid reached for the stroller again instinctively, checking the baby before herself.

Her brother watched her do it.

Then he knelt beside her.

This time, she didn’t flinch.

“I spent years thinking I was an only child,” he whispered.

The maid’s lips trembled.

“I spent years knowing I had a brother who didn’t know I existed.”

That broke him.

He reached for her carefully.

And when she finally let him hug her, both of them started crying.

His daughter crouched beside them, still holding the phone that had changed everything.

Behind them, the wife stood alone at the staircase.

And beside the safe stroller, a man who had rushed into the room ready to punish a servant was now holding the sister his own family had hidden from him.
