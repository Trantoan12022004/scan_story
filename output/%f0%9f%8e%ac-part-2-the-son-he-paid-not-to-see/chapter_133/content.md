The woman’s face collapsed before she even spoke.

The father stared at her like he no longer knew who she was.

The little girl shrank deeper into the stroller, her shoulders trembling.

“Tell me I’m wrong,” he said.

But his voice had already broken.

The woman swallowed hard. “I did it for us.”

The words made the air feel colder.

The father looked down at his daughter. Her cheeks were wet now. She was trying so hard not to cry.

“For us?” he repeated.

The girl’s voice came out small and shaky.

“Mommy said I had to drink it or you’d leave.”

The man shut his eyes for one second, like the pain was too much to stand inside.

When he opened them, they were full of tears.

He crouched beside the stroller and gently touched his daughter’s hand.

“Can you see me?” he whispered.

The girl hesitated.

Then she gave the smallest nod.

His breath caught.

The woman covered her mouth, but it was too late. The truth was already standing between them.

The father reached up slowly and removed the sunglasses.

The little girl blinked in the daylight, scared and exposed. Her eyes searched his face.

Then finally found it.

“Daddy?” she whispered.

He broke.

He pulled her into his arms so carefully, as if he were afraid the whole world had already hurt her too much.

“I’m here,” he said, his voice shaking. “I’m right here.”

Over his daughter’s shoulder, he looked at his wife with quiet devastation.

And for the first time, she had no lie left to hide behind.
