The biker slowly turned toward the woman.

“Told me what?”

Her face crumbled.

The suited man gave a bitter laugh.

“She asked you to pretend to be her son?”

The biker’s hands curled into fists.

“Answer the question.”

The woman reached into her purse with trembling fingers.

“I didn’t know it was you.”

She pulled out an old photograph.

The biker stared at it.

A young woman was holding two newborn babies.

One of them wore a tiny metal bracelet.

The biker instinctively touched the old bracelet hanging from a chain around his neck.

His face changed.

“Where did you get this?”

Tears spilled down her cheeks.

“I’ve carried it for thirty years.”

The suited man stepped forward.

“She gave you away.”

The woman spun toward him.

“No.”

Her voice cracked.

“You took them.”

The diner fell silent.

The biker looked between them.

“What?”

The woman’s whole body shook now.

“Your father told me both my babies died.”

The suited man’s face went pale.

“But years later, I found out they’d been adopted.”

The biker glanced at the man beside him.

His brother.

Then back at her.

“You’re saying…”

She nodded, barely able to breathe.

“I came here because he found me before I could find you.”

The biker’s eyes filled.

The woman looked down at the photograph.

“When I asked you to pretend to be my son…”

Her voice broke completely.

“I had no idea I was asking my real one.”

For several seconds, the biker couldn’t speak.

Then he reached across the booth and gently took the photograph from her shaking hand.

His voice came out rough.

“You don’t need to pretend anymore.”

He looked at her through tears.

“Mom.”
