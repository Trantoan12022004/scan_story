Clara stared at him.

Her father crouched slowly beside the tub.

“What does that mean?”

The boy lowered his eyes.

“My mom said she saw what happened.”

Clara’s father stopped breathing normally.

“What happened where?”

The boy pointed toward the old stone path behind the hedges.

“There.”

Clara followed his finger.

Something in her face changed.

A memory.

Weak at first.

Then sharper.

She whispered,

“I fell there.”

Her father nodded carefully.

“Yes.”

But the boy shook his head.

“No.”

The garden went silent.

“She didn’t fall.”

Clara’s father looked at him.

The boy’s lips trembled.

“My mom said somebody scared her.”

Clara’s grip tightened on the crutches.

Suddenly she remembered running.

A voice behind her.

Someone grabbing her arm.

Then the ground.

Her breathing turned uneven.

“Dad…”

He looked at her immediately.

“I remember someone yelling.”

The boy nodded.

“My mom found you after.”

Clara’s father’s face went pale.

“Why didn’t she tell me?”

“She tried.”

His voice broke.

“But somebody told her she’d lose everything if she talked.”

The father looked toward the mansion.

Then back at the child.

“Who?”

The boy slowly untied the red scarf from his wrist.

Hidden inside its fold was a tiny brass key.

He placed it in the father’s palm.

“My mom said this opens the garden office.”

Clara’s father stared at it.

The boy continued softly.

“She hid a letter there.”

Clara whispered,

“From who?”

The boy looked toward the upper windows of the mansion.

One curtain moved.

Someone was watching them.

Then he said,

“Your stepmother.”

Clara’s father went completely still.

Clara looked toward the window too.

And another memory hit her.

A woman’s voice.

Cold.

Close to her ear.

“If you tell your father what you heard, he’ll leave you too.”

Clara’s face crumpled.

“It was her.”

Her father turned back toward the house, disbelief turning slowly into pain.

The boy stayed kneeling in the wet gravel.

He had not come to perform a miracle.

He had come because his mother died carrying a secret.

And Clara’s first tiny movement had done something no one expected.

It brought the memory back.
