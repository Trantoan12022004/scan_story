Colton didn’t move.

The footage continued.

Vanessa stood near the locked door while the maid backed away from her.

There was no attack.

No struggle.

No danger.

Only Vanessa speaking calmly while the maid held her hands close to her body and listened.

Then Vanessa picked up the pitcher.

The maid shook her head.

The little girl appeared in the doorway.

And Vanessa immediately changed.

Her expression softened.

Her posture changed.

She put the pitcher down and smiled like nothing had happened.

Colton paused the video.

His hands were shaking.

He opened another recording.

Two days earlier.

Same room.

Same maid.

Same locked door.

But this time Vanessa was holding something else.

An envelope.

She pushed it toward the maid.

The maid refused to take it.

Vanessa leaned closer.

Then pointed toward the front entrance.

Colton could not hear the audio clearly, but the meaning was obvious.

Leave.

His eyes moved toward the maid.

“Why didn’t you tell me?”

She looked down.

Vanessa answered before she could.

“She lies.”

Colton turned toward her.

“Stop.”

The word came out quiet.

That frightened Vanessa more than shouting would have.

The little girl moved closer to the maid and grabbed her wet sleeve.

Colton looked back at the phone.

There was one recording left.

A week earlier.

Vanessa standing in Colton’s private study.

Alone.

She opened one drawer.

Then another.

Then took out an old photograph.

Colton froze.

It was a picture of him years ago with a young woman.

Anna.

The woman he had loved before she disappeared.

The woman nobody had been able to find.

Vanessa stared at the photograph.

Then looked directly toward the maid entering the room.

The maid stopped.

Vanessa dropped the photograph.

Colton’s breathing changed.

He slowly looked at the woman kneeling on the floor in front of him.

Wet hair hid part of her face.

He stepped closer.

She lifted her eyes.

And suddenly six years disappeared.

He recognized her.

Not completely.

Not all at once.

But enough.

“Anna?”

Vanessa backed away.

The little girl looked between them.

The maid closed her eyes.

Colton stared at the child.

Then at Anna.

Then at Vanessa.

His voice cracked.

“You knew who she was.”

Vanessa said nothing.

That silence answered him.

Colton looked at the footage again.

Now he understood why Vanessa had been trying to force Anna out of the house.

Why she wanted him to believe the maid was unstable.

Why every confrontation happened when he was away.

She wasn’t protecting him from Anna.

She was protecting a six-year-old lie.

Anna finally stood.

Her voice was weak.

“She found me before you did.”

Colton’s face emptied.

“What?”

Anna looked at Vanessa.

“Six years ago.”

Vanessa began crying.

Colton stared at his fiancée.

“You knew where she was?”

Anna nodded.

“She told me you didn’t want me back.”

Colton closed his eyes.

The little girl clung tighter to Anna’s side.

Then Colton looked at the child again.

Her eyes.

Her chin.

The shape of her mouth.

All familiar now.

Too familiar.

His voice barely worked.

“How old is she?”

Anna swallowed.

“Five.”

Colton stumbled backward.

Vanessa covered her mouth.

The little girl looked at him, frightened.

Then whispered,

“Why are you crying?”

He couldn’t answer.

Because the security footage had exposed more than cruelty.

It had shown him the woman he thought vanished.

And the child who had been living under his roof without knowing he was her father.
