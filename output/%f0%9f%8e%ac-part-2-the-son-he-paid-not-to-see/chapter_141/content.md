The groom followed the boy’s eyes.

His bride had gone completely pale.

He looked back at the children.

“Where did you get this?”

The older child swallowed.

“Mom gave it to us.”

The groom’s face tightened.

“Who is your mother?”

The boy reached inside his muddy coat and pulled out a folded photograph.

He handed it over.

The groom stared at it.

Then stopped breathing.

A young woman stood in the picture holding both children.

Emily.

His former fiancée.

The woman who disappeared six years earlier, just weeks before their wedding.

The woman everyone told him had left because she changed her mind.

He had searched for months.

Then years.

Eventually, even he stopped asking questions.

Now two children were standing in front of him calling him Daddy.

The younger girl wiped her face.

“Mom said you’d know the ring.”

The groom looked down again.

His mother had adored Emily.

Days before she died, she had given Emily that ring privately.

She told her it should stay with the family.

The groom never knew.

After his mother’s death, both Emily and the ring vanished.

The bride finally spoke.

“She stole it.”

The older boy immediately shook his head.

“No.”

His voice trembled.

“She said someone made her leave.”

The groom slowly turned toward his bride.

Her eyes filled.

He remembered something then.

His current bride had been Emily’s closest friend.

The person who comforted him after Emily disappeared.

The person who told him Emily had boarded a flight overseas.

The person who eventually became the woman standing beside him at the altar.

His voice broke.

“You knew where she was.”

The bride shook her head.

“No.”

But the children were watching her now.

The younger girl suddenly grabbed her brother’s hand.

“She knows Mommy.”

The groom looked down.

“How?”

The little girl pointed at the bride.

“She came to our house.”

Silence.

The bride’s face collapsed.

The groom stepped backward like the floor had shifted beneath him.

“When?”

The older boy answered.

“Last week.”

That was why they came.

Emily had gotten sick.

She became frightened something would happen before her children ever met their father.

So she gave them the ring.

The photograph.

And the address of the wedding venue.

She told them only one thing:

Find him before he marries her.

The groom looked at the two children again.

Same dark eyes as his.

Same small cleft in the chin.

The older boy was six.

The younger girl was five.

The timeline hit him all at once.

His voice disappeared.

The younger child looked up.

“Are you really our daddy?”

He dropped to his knees.

Tears streamed down his face.

He didn’t reach for them.

He let them decide.

The little girl moved first.

Then her brother.

Both children wrapped their arms around him.

He broke completely.

Behind them, the bride stood beneath the white rose arch, watching the wedding she had spent years building collapse because two frightened children had carried one old ring through the rain.

And the groom finally understood why Emily had disappeared.

She hadn’t run away from him.

Someone had made sure she believed he had already chosen someone else.
