The father stared at the woman in red.

“Sister?”

Emma looked between the adults, confused and crying.

The woman closed her eyes.

For years, Sarah had told her husband that she once had a younger sister.

A girl named Rebecca.

The sister their family said had died when they were teenagers.

The father slowly stood.

“You’re Rebecca?”

The woman’s face crumpled.

“Yes.”

Emma tightened her grip on his jacket.

He looked at the woman he had welcomed into his home for months.

The woman who had introduced herself under another name.

The woman who had become close to his wife.

“You told us you met Sarah through the foundation.”

Rebecca nodded through tears.

“I lied.”

“Why?”

She looked toward the bed.

“Because Sarah didn’t know I was alive.”

The father’s face hardened.

“And you wanted to keep it that way?”

Rebecca shook her head.

“At first, I wanted to tell her.”

“Then what was in the milk?”

Rebecca went silent.

That silence made Emma start crying harder.

Her father immediately pulled her closer.

“Tell me.”

Rebecca looked at Sarah.

Then at the bottle on the floor.

“She found our mother’s old letters yesterday.”

The father’s expression changed.

“What letters?”

“The ones proving Sarah inherited everything.”

The room went still.

Rebecca’s voice broke.

“Our family told me she was dead too.”

The father stared at her.

“They separated you.”

Rebecca nodded.

“For years.”

She wiped at her face.

“When I finally found Sarah, she had the life I thought had been stolen from me. The house. The money. A husband. Emma.”

Emma looked up at her.

Rebecca’s eyes dropped with shame.

“I hated her for something she never even knew happened.”

The father’s jaw tightened.

“So you hurt her because your parents lied to both of you?”

Rebecca began to cry.

“I wanted her unconscious long enough to take the letters and leave.”

He stared at her in disbelief.

“You gambled with her life.”

Rebecca couldn’t answer.

Behind him, Sarah made a faint sound.

Emma instantly ran back to the bed.

“Mama?”

Sarah’s eyelids fluttered.

Her fingers moved weakly toward her daughter.

Emma grabbed her hand with both of hers.

“I’m here.”

Sarah’s husband dropped beside them again, tears spilling now as he held his wife’s other hand.

In the doorway, Rebecca stood completely alone.

Sarah slowly opened her eyes.

Her gaze found Emma first.

Then her husband.

Finally, Rebecca.

Tears gathered in her eyes.

Rebecca whispered,

“I’m sorry.”

Sarah stared at the sister she had mourned for half her life.

Then, weakly, painfully, she said,

“They lied to you too.”

Rebecca covered her mouth and broke down.

And beside the bed, Emma held onto her mother’s hand without understanding the inheritance, the lies, or the decades of betrayal.

She only understood one thing.

Her mother had opened her eyes.

And the woman Emma thought had come to destroy their family was part of the family that had already been destroyed years ago.
