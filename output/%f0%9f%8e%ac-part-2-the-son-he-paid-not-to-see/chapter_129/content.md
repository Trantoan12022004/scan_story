The room went silent.

The soaked maid stared at him, confused, breathless, frightened.

The blonde woman behind them tried to recover first.

“She stole it,” she said quickly. “She’s lying to you, just like her brat—”

“Be quiet.”

He never looked at the blonde woman when he said it. His eyes stayed locked on the maid’s face.

The maid’s lips trembled. She looked down at the ring as if she had forgotten she was wearing it.

“It was my mother’s,” she whispered. “She gave it to me before she died.”

He shook his head slowly, like he was trying to fight what he was seeing.

“No,” he said, almost to himself. “I gave that ring to someone.”

The maid’s breathing caught.

He reached into his wallet with unsteady hands and pulled out an old, worn photograph he had carried for years. In it, a younger version of him stood beside a smiling woman with the same eyes, the same mouth, the same quiet sadness now kneeling in front of him.

He held the photo next to her face.

The blonde woman stepped back.

The little girl had reached the doorway by then, still crying, still watching.

The maid looked at the photo and went pale.

Her voice broke.

“Where did you get that?”

His eyes filled.

“She was my wife.”

The blonde woman’s face collapsed.

The maid stared at him, then at the child in the doorway.

Her hand covered her mouth.

The girl ran in and clung to her soaked shoulders.

“Mama…”

He looked at the child then — really looked.

The same eyes.

The same shape of the face.

His voice came out as a whisper.

“How old is she?”

The maid closed her eyes for one second, like she had been dreading this moment for years.

“Six.”

He did the math instantly.

The blonde woman turned to leave.

“Don’t move,” he said coldly.

She froze.

The maid held her daughter tighter, tears spilling down both their faces.

He looked back at the woman kneeling on the floor — the woman he had mourned, the woman he had been told disappeared willingly, the woman now trembling in front of him wearing the ring he had slipped onto her finger years ago.

And when he spoke again, his voice broke completely.

“You’re not the maid,” he said softly. “You’re my wife… and that little girl is my daughter.”
