The waitress followed his eyes.

She pulled her sleeve down.

“What do you want?”

The man could barely speak.

“That scar.”

Her expression tightened.

“What about it?”

“You got it when you were sixteen.”

She stepped backward.

“No.”

His eyes filled.

“You fell through the glass door at your mother’s house.”

The waitress stared at him.

The little girl remained wrapped around her waist.

“How would you know that?”

The man’s voice cracked.

“Because I was your husband.”

Silence.

The waitress shook her head.

“No.”

He reached slowly into his wallet.

Not toward her.

He kept his distance.

Then he removed an old photograph.

A younger version of himself.

A woman beside him.

And a newborn baby between them.

The waitress looked at the photograph.

Her knees weakened.

The woman in the picture had her face.

The girl pointed immediately.

“That’s you.”

The waitress covered her mouth.

The man whispered,

“Her name is Lily.”

The waitress looked down at the child clinging to her.

Lily.

Something moved behind her eyes.

Not a full memory.

A fragment.

A white hospital room.

A tiny pink blanket.

A man crying while holding a newborn.

Then nothing.

She stumbled toward a chair.

“I don’t understand.”

The man knelt several feet away.

“Five years ago, you disappeared.”

There had been a highway accident during a storm.

Her car was found wrecked near a river.

Her purse was inside.

Her phone.

Her wedding ring.

But not her.

Search crews looked for weeks.

Eventually, the family accepted what seemed impossible to fight anymore.

She was presumed dead.

The man never had a body to bury.

Only an empty grave beside her parents.

The waitress shook her head.

“I woke up in a clinic.”

Her voice was barely audible.

She remembered that much.

A woman had brought her there.

No identification.

No clear memory of where she came from.

A serious head injury had left parts of her past confused and fragmented.

When she recovered enough to leave, the woman who had helped her gave her one piece of information.

A first name found written inside her coat.

Mara.

So Mara became the name she used.

The man looked at her name tag again.

Mara.

His wife’s name.

He had assumed his eyes were playing tricks on him when he first saw it.

Until she spoke.

Until he saw the scar.

The waitress looked down at Lily.

“But she remembers me.”

The man shook his head.

“She was a baby when you disappeared.”

Mara’s face fell.

Then Lily pulled something from beneath the collar of her dress.

A tiny laminated photograph.

The same woman.

The same face.

Her mother.

“Grandma gave me this.”

The man looked at his daughter.

“When?”

“Before she went to the hospital.”

His expression changed.

Lily’s maternal grandmother had recently become seriously ill.

For years, she had kept a box filled with Mara’s photographs.

Whenever Lily asked about her mother, her grandmother showed her those pictures.

Again and again.

“This is your mama.”

“Never forget her face.”

So Lily hadn’t remembered the waitress from infancy.

She recognized the woman from hundreds of photographs.

The waitress began crying.

“You’ve been looking for me?”

The little girl nodded.

“Everywhere.”

Mara covered her face.

The man looked away, fighting tears.

Then she noticed something.

The white plush bunny.

One ear had a tiny stitched blue star.

Mara reached toward it.

Her fingers stopped before touching.

“I know that.”

Lily held it up.

“Daddy said you bought it for me.”

The man nodded.

“You gave it to her the day she was born.”

Mara stared at the bunny.

Another fragment.

A newborn crying.

Her own exhausted laughter.

Her voice saying:

“She needs something softer than this hospital blanket.”

Mara gasped.

Her hand flew to her mouth.

Lily immediately reached for her.

“Mama?”

Mara pulled the girl into her arms.

This time she cried openly.

The man stayed where he was.

He did not rush toward her.

Did not call her his wife again.

Too much had happened.

Too much remained uncertain.

Mara finally looked at him.

“You really thought I was dead?”

His face broke.

“I looked for you until people started telling me I was destroying Lily by refusing to accept it.”

She stared at him.

Then at the child.

“You raised her alone?”

He nodded.

“I kept telling her about you.”

Lily hugged her mother tighter.

“Daddy said you loved me.”

Mara’s eyes closed.

“I think I did.”

Then she corrected herself.

“No.”

She kissed the top of Lily’s head.

“I know I did.”

The man began crying.

But before he could say anything else, Mara suddenly looked at him strangely.

A new memory had surfaced.

Not the accident.

Not the hospital.

Something from the hours before she disappeared.

She whispered,

“There was someone in my car.”

The man froze.

“What?”

Mara’s breathing quickened.

“I wasn’t alone.”

He stared at her.

“Who was with you?”

She looked toward Lily.

Then back at him.

Her face slowly filled with fear.

“Your brother.”

The man went completely still.

Because his brother had been the person who identified Mara’s belongings after the crash.

And the person who first told him to stop searching.
