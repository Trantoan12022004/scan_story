The officer looked at Adrian.

“Two former employees made statements.”

Adrian slowly released the maid.

His anger disappeared.

Something worse replaced it.

Disbelief.

“What statements?”

His wife shook her head quickly.

“They were lying.”

The officer didn’t react.

“The first was your daughter’s former nanny.”

Adrian froze.

He remembered her.

Maria.

She had worked in the house for almost three years.

Then one morning his wife told him Maria had become unstable and resigned without warning.

Adrian believed her.

The officer continued.

“The second was a housekeeper who left four months ago.”

Another face appeared in Adrian’s mind.

Another woman his wife had described as careless.

Dishonest.

Dangerous around the children.

He looked toward the maid still sitting on the marble.

Three women.

Three stories.

And every story had come through the same person.

His wife.

“What did they accuse her of?”

The officer glanced at the blonde woman.

“Creating incidents.”

Silence.

Adrian’s daughter lowered her phone slightly.

The officer explained that both former employees described the same pattern.

Something would go wrong.

A broken object.

A fall.

A frightened child.

Then Adrian’s wife would immediately blame the employee before anyone could ask what actually happened.

The first woman had no evidence.

The second had only her word.

Neither case went anywhere.

Until today.

Adrian looked at his daughter’s phone.

Video.

Clear.

Undeniable.

His wife started crying.

“You don’t understand.”

His daughter looked at her.

“I do.”

Everyone turned toward the girl.

She opened another clip.

Not from the staircase.

From the hallway two weeks earlier.

The blonde woman was speaking to the maid.

No shouting.

No physical attack.

Just one cold sentence.

“If Adrian ever has to choose between you and me, remember whose house this is.”

Adrian stared at the screen.

Then at his wife.

The maid finally spoke.

“She wanted me gone.”

His wife shook her head.

“No.”

But the officer stepped closer.

“So did the other two women.”

Adrian’s face broke.

His daughter looked at him.

“She kept telling you they were the problem.”

He remembered every conversation.

Every warning.

Every employee he had dismissed without asking enough questions.

His wife had never needed him to witness anything.

She only needed him to trust her first.

And he always had.

The baby began crying softly.

The maid immediately moved toward the stroller.

Adrian’s wife stepped forward too.

But the child in blue raised one hand.

“Don’t.”

The blonde woman stopped.

Adrian looked at his daughter.

For the first time, he realized she wasn’t calm because she wasn’t scared.

She was calm because she had been waiting for someone to finally believe her.

He crouched beside her.

“How long have you known?”

Her eyes filled.

“A while.”

That answer crushed him.

The officer looked toward the wife.

“Ma’am, we need to talk.”

She stared at Adrian.

Waiting for him to defend her.

He didn’t.

He looked instead at the maid.

At the bruising beginning along her arm.

At the stroller she had protected while falling.

Then at his daughter.

The one person in the house who had finally recorded what everyone else had only been able to describe.

And Adrian understood why the word third had terrified his wife.

It meant this was no longer one accusation.

It was a pattern.
