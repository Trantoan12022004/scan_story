“Daughter.”

Rachel stared at Sophie.

“What?”

Tom closed his eyes.

Sophie pointed at the frozen woman on the television.

“She came to meet you.”

Rachel laughed once in disbelief.

“No. My mother raised me.”

“She did.”

Sophie’s voice cracked.

“But she didn’t give birth to you.”

The room went painfully quiet.

Rachel looked at Tom.

“You knew?”

He couldn’t answer.

Her expression collapsed.

“How long?”

“Three months.”

Rachel stepped backward.

“You knew for three months that my mother wasn’t my biological mother?”

Tom swallowed.

“I was trying to confirm it before telling you.”

Rachel pointed at the screen.

“And kissing her was part of confirming it?”

Sophie shook her head.

“He wasn’t kissing her.”

She rewound the footage.

From another angle, Tom had leaned close to stop the woman from shouting.

She had grabbed his face.

Then slapped him when he tried to move away.

Rachel stared.

“Why did she tell him to stay away from her daughter?”

Sophie looked down.

“Because Tom found her first.”

Rachel’s voice trembled.

“Why was he looking for her?”

Tom reached into his jacket and pulled out an old letter.

“Your mother gave me this before she died.”

Rachel took it.

The handwriting was unmistakable.

Rachel deserves to know who she really is. Find Evelyn.

Rachel looked at the woman frozen on-screen.

“Evelyn?”

Tom nodded.

“She’s your biological mother.”

Rachel’s eyes filled.

“Then why didn’t she come to me?”

Sophie whispered,

“She tried.”

Rachel turned.

“When?”

Sophie looked toward one of the older guests standing near the cake.

“At the funeral.”

Rachel followed her gaze.

The guest went pale.

Sophie continued,

“But someone in this room told Evelyn you never wanted to know her.”

Rachel stared at the trembling guest.

“Who told her that?”

Sophie raised the remote toward them.

“The same person who switched your hospital records thirty-two years ago.”
