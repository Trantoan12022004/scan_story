Her father stared at the woman.

“What did she just say?”

The woman shook her head quickly.

“She’s confused.”

Lily grabbed her father’s sleeve.

“No, I’m not.”

Her legs gave way.

He caught her and gently lowered her back into the wheelchair.

Then Lily pointed toward the house.

“She makes me practice standing when you’re at work.”

His expression changed.

“You knew she could stand?”

The woman’s eyes filled with panic.

“It wasn’t safe yet.”

“Then why hide it from me?”

Silence.

Lily looked down.

“She said if you knew I was getting better, she’d have to leave.”

Her father went still.

The woman whispered, “That’s not what I meant.”

But Lily continued.

“She gives me medicine before you come home.”

Her father slowly looked toward the woman.

“What medicine?”

“I was helping her sleep.”

“She sleeps every afternoon because you told me her condition was getting worse.”

The woman backed away.

“I can explain.”

He walked inside and returned moments later carrying Lily’s medication bag.

At the bottom was a bottle he had never seen before.

The label had been removed.

His hands began shaking.

“What have you been giving my daughter?”

The woman started crying.

“I needed this job.”

He stared at her in disbelief.

Then she finally admitted it.

Lily’s doctors had told her weeks earlier that the girl was regaining strength.

She had hidden the progress reports.

Canceled therapy appointments.

And kept telling the father there had been no improvement.

Because Lily’s long-term care trust paid for a full-time private caregiver only while she remained classified as unable to walk independently.

The father looked sick.

“You kept my daughter believing she couldn’t stand… for a paycheck?”

“I never hurt her.”

Lily’s tiny voice came from behind him.

“You told me if I tried walking, I could end up paralyzed forever.”

The woman closed her eyes.

That was enough.

Her father took out his phone.

“Leave.”

She looked at him.

“Please—”

“Leave before the police get here.”

She walked away without another word.

Then he returned to Lily and knelt in the water beside her.

She looked terrified.

“Dad… am I really getting better?”

He took both of her hands.

“I don’t know how far you can go.”

His voice broke.

“But from now on, nobody gets to lie to you about what your own body can do.”

Lily looked at the wheelchair.

Then at her trembling legs.

“Can I try again?”

Tears filled his eyes.

He held out both hands.

“This time, we do it together.”
