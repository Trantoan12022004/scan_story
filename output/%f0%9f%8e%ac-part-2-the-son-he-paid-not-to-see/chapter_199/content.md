The sister’s husband adjusted his cuff and stepped into the aisle.

“I was.”

The entire hall turned toward him.

The bride blinked.

“You?”

The groom’s face collapsed.

The man in black gave a single nod.

“Yes.”

The sister stared at her husband through tears.

He looked back at her first, not the crowd.

Then he faced the room.

“I told him.”

The bride laughed once, but it was thin now.

“Why would he listen to you?”

The answer came from the man in black.

“Because he works for me.”

The bride stopped breathing.

The groom shut his eyes.

The sister’s husband spoke quietly.

“And because I’m his son.”

The hall erupted into gasps.

The bride looked between the two men, unable to process what she was hearing.

“No.”

Her voice broke.

“No, he’s supposed to be nobody.”

The sister’s husband looked at her with tired disappointment.

“That was easier for you to believe.”

The groom stepped forward desperately.

“Sir, please—”

But the man in black cut him off.

“You told me you were marrying into a kind family.”

His eyes flicked toward the crying sister.

“You forgot to mention this.”

The bride’s face hardened for a second, then cracked.

The sister slowly rose to her feet.

Her husband crossed the space between them and held out his hand.

She took it.

The bride’s microphone shook in her grip.

“Who are you?”

The man in cream turned back toward her.

His voice was calm.

“The man with nothing to offer?”

He glanced at the groom.

Then at the stunned guests.

And finally at the bride.

“I’m the owner of the company your husband begged my father to hand him one day.”
