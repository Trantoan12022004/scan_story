The maid stared at her.

“What does that mean?”

The woman in bronze said nothing.

The man picked up the photograph.

Two young women stood together in front of the same mansion twenty-five years earlier.

One was the maid’s mother.

The other was the woman in bronze.

The maid’s voice trembled.

“You knew her?”

The woman backed away.

“No.”

The man turned the photo over.

There was handwriting on the back.

To my sister. If anything happens to me, protect my baby.

The maid stopped breathing.

“Sister?”

The man looked at the bronze-dressed woman.

“You’re her aunt.”

Guests began whispering.

The maid’s eyes filled.

“My mother told me she had no family.”

The woman finally snapped,

“She was supposed to disappear.”

The room went silent.

The man stepped closer.

“Why?”

Her hands started shaking.

“Because our father left everything to her.”

The maid looked around the mansion.

“What everything?”

The man slowly lifted his eyes toward the chandeliers, the marble floors, the entire estate.

“This house.”

The maid went still.

The woman in bronze whispered,

“She never knew.”

The man shook his head.

“No.”

He handed the photograph back to the maid.

“But her daughter does now.”

The maid stared at the woman who had just accused her of stealing.

Then quietly asked,

“So you planted the necklace because you recognized me?”

The woman’s eyes filled with panic.

“I recognized your mother’s face.”

The maid wiped her tears.

For years she had entered this mansion through the servants’ door.

Now she looked around differently.

Because the house she had been cleaning…

had belonged to her mother.
