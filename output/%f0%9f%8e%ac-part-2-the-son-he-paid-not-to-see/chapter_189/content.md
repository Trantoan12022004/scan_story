A heavy gasp moved through the crowd.

The giant black stallion lowered itself completely, kneeling before the little girl like it had been waiting for her all along.

The man in denim took one step forward.

“That’s not possible.”

The girl laid her hand gently against the horse’s face.

At once, the animal went still.

Its breathing softened.

Its eyes closed for a brief second under her touch.

The man’s voice came out rough now.

“Who are you?”

The girl looked up at him.

“My mama said he’d remember me.”

The crowd exchanged uneasy glances.

The man froze.

“Your mama?”

The girl nodded.

“She used to sing to him.”

All the color drained from his face.

He stared at the horse.

Then at the girl.

Then back at the horse kneeling obediently in the dirt.

“What was her name?”

The girl’s chin trembled.

“Evelyn.”

The lead rope slipped from his hand.

A long silence swallowed the corral.

The girl reached into the pocket of her dress and pulled out an old folded photograph.

She held it toward him.

A younger Evelyn stood beside the same black stallion.

And beside her stood the man.

Smiling.

His hand began to shake as he took the picture.

The girl’s eyes filled with tears.

“My mama said if he knelt for me, you’d finally believe her.”

The man looked up, already breaking apart.

“Believe what?”

The girl swallowed hard.

“That she didn’t lose the baby.”

His face collapsed.

The horse pressed its head gently against the girl’s shoulder.

And with tears sliding down her dusty face, she whispered the truth that shattered him.

“I’m the daughter they told you was dead.”
