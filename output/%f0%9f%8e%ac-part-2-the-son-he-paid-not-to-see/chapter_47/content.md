The mother stared at the paper.

“No. He loved both of you.”

The homeless boy looked down.

“He never met me.”

Her wealthy son stepped beside him.

“What’s your name?”

“Ethan.”

The mother nearly collapsed.

That was the name she had chosen for her firstborn twin.

She touched her chest.

“You were Ethan.”

The boy looked confused.

“Were?”

She began crying.

“You still are.”

He swallowed.

“Then why did nobody come for me?”

She had no answer.

Until she noticed something engraved beneath the locket’s photographs.

Two times.

8:41 PM.

8:47 PM.

Ethan had been born six minutes before his brother.

And suddenly she remembered her late father-in-law’s will.

The firstborn grandson would inherit controlling ownership of the family company at eighteen.

She stopped breathing.

Her husband had always told her the younger baby survived.

But if Ethan was really the firstborn…

everything belonged to him.

Her son looked at her.

“Mom, what is it?”

She whispered,

“Your father didn’t lose your brother.”

“He removed the heir.”

Ethan backed away.

“I don’t want money.”

She immediately stood.

“This isn’t about money.”

She reached for him, then stopped herself.

Ethan still looked afraid of being touched.

So instead she held out her hand.

“You are my son.”

He stared at it.

Before he could take it, a black luxury car stopped beside the curb.

Her husband stepped out.

He saw Ethan.

And froze.

Not confused.

Not shocked.

Afraid.

The mother’s face hardened.

“You recognize him.”

Her husband said nothing.

The wealthy boy looked at his father.

“Dad?”

Ethan’s voice became tiny.

“Is that him?”

The man finally whispered,

“This wasn’t supposed to happen.”

The mother stared at him.

“What wasn’t supposed to happen?”

He closed his eyes.

“For them to meet.”

Both boys went completely still.

The wealthy son slowly removed his expensive blazer.

He walked over to Ethan and placed it around his shoulders.

Then he stood beside him.

“You’re my brother.”

Ethan looked at him.

“You believe me?”

The boy held up the open locket.

“I believe this.”

Their father stepped forward.

“Come with me. We can fix this.”

The mother moved between him and both children.

“No.”

Her voice was calm now.

“You had eight years to fix it.”

She took out her phone.

“You used one son to protect your fortune…”

Her eyes filled as she looked at Ethan’s bare foot.

“…and let the other grow up believing he was an only child.”

Ethan finally reached for her hand.

Then his brother took the other.

And for the first time, the mother walked away with both of her sons—

while the man who had separated them stood alone on the sidewalk, watching the two identical boys leave together.
